# The experienced hallkeeper's working day

Research supplement · 7 September 2026 · T-606

**The intended experience: “Give me a workable agreement, the things I need, room to use my judgement, and a clean finish.”** This is a modelled voice, not an interview quotation. Every pain point and proposed response below is a hypothesis for observation and co-design with hallkeepers. Nothing establishes Trades Hall procedures, measured savings or shipped functionality.

The job involves stewardship, physical craft and relationships. A person may be welcoming a nervous organiser, adjusting a room, protecting its surfaces, explaining equipment and remembering an unfinished job within the same few minutes. More screen time is not inherently progress.

## New evidence beyond general event guidance

- **Actual hallkeeping duties:** Wareham's role includes preparing rooms from a weekly diary, heating and kitchen setup, welcoming the nominated hirer, joint condition checks, reporting variations, recording departures and working late or split shifts. It is one council's job, not a universal procedure. [Wareham job description][wareham]
- **Judgement and interpersonal work:** Edinburgh's cultural-venue duty manager balances competing client, visitor, contractor and staff demands, briefs colleagues and makes decisions without immediate supervision while spending most of the shift in public areas. [Edinburgh job description][edinburgh]
- **Skilled crews:** Traverse's festival technician role combines physical turnaround with specialist equipment knowledge, prioritisation and delegation. A task name alone cannot establish who is competent to perform it. [Traverse recruitment pack page, 2025][traverse]
- **The real building resists simplified plans:** Lowry's specification distinguishes lift dimensions from corridor bottlenecks, configuration-dependent staffing and equipment reinstatement. East Coker's hirer information locates controls and equipment, describes warm-up behaviour and gives return/reset instructions. These demonstrate useful information types; their measurements and operating instructions must not be copied into another venue. [Lowry specification, May 2025][lowry], [East Coker guide][eastcoker]
- **Fast does not necessarily mean easy:** Mark, Gudith and Klocke's 48-person laboratory email study found faster interrupted work alongside greater stress, frustration, time pressure and effort. Its mostly student sample and office task do not establish hallkeeper effect sizes. [CHI 2008 paper][interruptions]
- **Remembering to return is work:** NASA's research addresses deferred intentions and concurrent task demands. Applying interruption bookmarks to venue work is a design inference. [NASA research overview][nasa]
- **Autonomy matters:** Gagné and Deci's work-motivation review supports considering autonomy, competence and relatedness. It does not prove a particular interface will improve satisfaction. [2005 research review][autonomy]

## Small pains, considered responses

| Modelled micro-pain | Proposed response, including simple physical tools | Risk or counterexample to test |
|---|---|---|
| **The plan is beautiful but I cannot orient it from this doorway.** | Show a chosen entrance photograph, labelled fixed landmarks, viewing direction and stable table/zone IDs. Keep the plan orientation fixed while working. Paper gets the same landmarks. | Automatic rotation can confuse crews. A photograph or generated image is not a measured plan. |
| **“Cabaret for 120” leaves me to guess the actual arrangement.** | A receiving-layout check distinguishes total attendance, seats by table, audience facing, presentation area, service approach and what must remain clear. Record unresolved questions against the exact plan revision before moving furniture. | Do not invent dimensions or assume all attendees are seated. Ask only about genuinely missing operational decisions. |
| **I lose time making everything exact when only some positions are critical.** | Approved work packs distinguish fixed positions/clearances, aesthetic alignment targets and areas where the crew may adjust. Show the reason behind critical placement and the person who can authorise a change. | No generic tolerance or “close enough” rule. Safety, accessibility and contractual boundaries remain venue-defined. |
| **I move furniture twice because the delivery, cable run or cleaning needed to happen first.** | Give a short dependency sequence plus “keep / move / bring / remove”. Let the hallkeeper choose the order of independent jobs and stage items near their next use where locally permitted. | A shorter walking route can obstruct access or create awkward handling. Rehearse with actual crew and space. |
| **The microphone is present; the usable microphone kit is not.** | Reserve named kits: device, compatible accessories, stand, charged batteries and return container. A labelled case photograph and contents card can outperform item-by-item phone scanning. Record missing components once. | Kits drift; a sealed case is not evidence of function. Specialist compatibility and testing remain necessary. |
| **The stock exists, but somebody moved it, borrowed it or packed it behind another event.** | Storage homes have plain shelf/bin labels, recognition photos, access notes and last confirmed location. Record temporary relocation and expected return. Keep event allocation separate from mere physical presence. | Mandatory scans for every chair create work. Start with shared scarce items and recurring missing equipment. |
| **A guest interrupts halfway through setting or checking a row.** | One optional bookmark: “Resume at table H; chairs placed, alignment unchecked.” Preserve view, selected area and next intended action; show changes since pausing before resumption. A paper marker is valid. | Urgent situations allow no time to bookmark. Never treat a paused check as complete or silently resume after its plan changed. |
| **Everybody asks me because I know the building.** | Route routine questions to a reviewed room card or named duty role; offer a quiet queue with a promised response time. Keep a visible way to request immediate human help. | Suppressing interruptions indiscriminately damages hospitality and hides urgent needs. Avoid forcing visitors to use an app. |
| **The client says “the office promised it”; I become the person who disappoints them.** | Show the agreed request and decision contact, then support a brief human conversation: understood need, practical constraint, available options, person deciding and time of next update. Log the outcome once. | Audit history must not become ammunition against clients or colleagues. Do not send stiff automated replies during a sensitive interaction. |
| **An agency colleague needs help; the veteran finds the same instructions patronising.** | Provide “quick reference” and “show me how” on the same task. Use equipment photos, familiar local terms and a named buddy for unfamiliar work. Preserve identical outcome and escalation criteria. | Self-selected expertise is not authorisation. Do not infer skill from age, speed or seniority. |
| **The room is technically complete but feels wrong to welcome people into.** | Protect time for the hallkeeper's final guest-perspective walk: sightlines, welcome, warmth, lighting, clutter, presentation and agreed personal touches. Capture reusable preferences with permission. | An aesthetic walk does not replace inspection. AI must not certify comfort from a photograph or impose stereotyped guest preferences. |
| **A ten-minute gap becomes another job; I never actually stop.** | Separate committed work, optional care jobs and protected rest/cover. Make setup labour, walking and unavoidable waiting visible to the duty manager. Let the worker decline optional work without a performance penalty. | Software cannot manufacture staffing. Do not fill every predicted free minute or present a break as protected before cover is accepted. |
| **A defect is reported, yet I must keep chasing it and apologising.** | Capture symptom, location, impact and a photo once; assign a resolver and response deadline. Show an agreed workaround or restriction, affected events and the next update. Close the loop with the reporter. | Avoid automatic asset quarantine for cosmetic faults; never substitute AI troubleshooting for competent safety decisions. |
| **I finish the event and discover a second shift of administration.** | Capture variations, returns, actual departure and open work as they happen. Prepare one editable closing summary, proposed timesheet entries and follow-up evidence. Transfer ownership explicitly and show what can wait until the next staffed period. | Do not infer paid hours from phone activity or require a perfect report before urgent care/rest. Charges and fault attribution need review. |
| **My know-how disappears, or software removes the part of the job I take pride in.** | Let staff contribute a short room tip with reason, author and review status. Recognise useful fixes and client thanks privately; preserve choice within agreed constraints. Make the veteran a contributor to the workflow. | Avoid leaderboards, surveillance, compulsory testimonials and turning an undocumented workaround into an approved procedure. |

## What to make concrete first

Prototype five modest interactions: receive and clarify a layout; collect a complete kit; pause and resume work; resolve a client request with the right decision-maker; finish with accepted carry-forward work. Each should work with a readable paper equivalent, labelled storage and human conversation. Consider a shared charging station, working pen, clipboard and room-reference folder as part of the service, not peripheral accessories.

Observe one opening, setup, interruption, changeover and close with staff consent. Ask them to explain *what they noticed before acting*, where the sheet forced interpretation, which trips were avoidable and what made the room feel ready. Compare correctness, repeated explanation, movement, resumption effort and how they feel at the end—not just completion time. Let staff remove proposed features that add burden.
