# The hallkeeper's day: care and craft

Principal experience specification · 7 September 2026 · T-606 · proposed design

The ambition is a hallkeeper who starts with a workable agreement, makes a room feel beautifully ready, looks after people with confidence, and finishes knowing that every remaining responsibility has a home. “100/100” expresses the care we intend to put into this work. It is not a measured grade or a claim that every problem has been solved.

This specification develops the user-liked visual direction without replacing the [first workflow](workflow.md) or treating its appearance as proof of operational success. It synthesises [the experienced working day](research/veteran-work.md), [client care research](research/client-care.md) and [the design pressure test](research/design-pressure-test.md). Those documents distinguish primary evidence from design inference. No hallkeeper interviews, field trials or measured savings have been invented.

The story is fictional: Hillside House, Thursday 15 May 2025 at 14:10; Nora is the hallkeeper, Ellis the duty manager, Maya proposed break cover, Daniel the organiser and Sam the technical colleague. Heritage Trust Dinner in Grand Hall currently has **120 guests under brief v7**, room ready by 17:15 and doors at 18:00. A request for 132 remains pending. Later actions below describe intended interactions and possible outcomes, not completed work. Reference and generated imagery establish visual direction, not venue facts, geometry or operating permissions.

## 1. Give Nora an agreement she can work from

The day's preparation begins in the administrative team. They turn the organiser's agreement into a coherent working brief: event purpose, named host, room, important moments, current arrangement, equipment, service requirements, conditions and unusual promises. Information already supplied by the organiser should travel with its meaning intact.

Real venue documents demonstrate why this matters. Bramleigh's planning guide separates a running sheet, guest list and floorplan; Barbican's manual distinguishes coordination, hosting, technical work and client responsibilities. Our inference is that Nora should receive a joined-up outcome, with the original sources available when needed. She should not reconstruct it from competing attachments. [Bramleigh guide](https://s3.amazonaws.com/ts-prod-assets.tripleseat.com/assets/050/874/684/Bramleigh-Estate-Wedding-Planning-Guide-Web-Apr-23.pdf), [Barbican manual, October 2017](https://www.barbican.org.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2017-10/event_organisers_manual_October%202017%20UPDATES.pdf)

The first face says what the event is trying to achieve. An optional organiser-confirmed line, **“What matters most today?”**, might explain a particular sightline or an unhurried welcome. Beside it are the issued layout, readiness deadline and the few conditions that affect the work. Fixed requirements, aesthetic preferences and positions where the crew may exercise judgement are distinguishable. No generic tolerance substitutes for actual venue constraints.

Unresolved commitments remain with administration or their proper specialist. An unknown delivery time appears as an owned question with a reply deadline; it does not quietly become Nora's job to chase. Administration requests her feasibility input when arrangements are unfamiliar, unusually tight or physically doubtful, rather than requiring her approval for every routine booking.

Nora has three natural responses: **“Understood,” “I need one answer,”** or **“This will not work as written.”** A question attaches to the relevant table, instruction or timing and includes its context automatically. She can describe the observed problem and suggest a solution. Administration owns revising the promise. “Understood” acknowledges receipt; it neither approves a commercial change nor certifies the room.

## 2. Let the plan meet the actual room

Opening the room pack preserves room, event and issued revision. It shows a recognisable landmark and viewing direction so Nora can orient the arrangement from where she stands. Table and zone identifiers remain stable across screen, paper and labels. A generated illustration is never used as a measured plan.

The useful comparison is the agreement against **the room as found**. If its recorded state is unconfirmed, ask once whether that state is still accurate. With verified information, show a practical difference: what stays, what moves, what comes in and what leaves. A beautiful target layout is much less helpful if the previous event's furniture must first be removed.

Receiving the brief also means receiving the means to do it. A collection instruction includes the actual storage home, recognition photo where useful, access condition, available handling equipment and required colleague or specialist. If these are unknown, it is an owned preparation gap, not a ready task.

Nora collects furniture at **15:30**, after her proposed break. Before then, the pending 120-to-132 request needs a rework decision. The duty team identifies unaffected work and any short, explicit hold on work likely to change. “Keep building the current arrangement” must not cause avoidable double handling. Every hold has an owner, next update and fallback; waiting does not become an indefinite limbo.

Equipment should arrive as usable kits: the appropriate device, compatible accessories, charged consumables, stand and return container. Labelled cases and contents cards can remove more friction than repeatedly scanning individual objects. The system records one missing component against the kit and affected job. Physical presence, allocation and successful testing remain different facts.

Real building specifications distinguish bottlenecks, access and reinstatement, while hall guidance describes local controls and reset requirements. The proposed collection and return workflow applies those information types; it imports none of another building's measurements or operating instructions. [Physical-work research and source links](research/veteran-work.md)

## 3. Respect the craft, and help the unfamiliar colleague

Nora's normal task is **“Prepare the dinner arrangement.”** Its face answers what good looks like, what is unusual, who is involved and when it is needed. Familiar work opens in a quick-reference view. **“Show me how”** reveals approved steps, photos and a named person to ask. A supervisor can use the same material to brief temporary crew.

Both views require the same outcome. Self-selecting expert mode does not grant technical authority or remove required checks. Conversely, experience should not be punished with compulsory instructions for each ordinary movement. Nora can choose the order of independent tasks, offer a better method and ask for another pair of hands without being labelled slow.

The primary action describes the real boundary: **“Arrangement finished,” “Delivery received,” “Kit returned.”** The two dependable alternatives are **“Something is different”** and **“Get help.”** Known room, item and event information are prefilled. A mistaken tap has an immediate correction path; if other work relied on it, that consequence is made visible and handled.

Record meaningful outcomes, not a tick for every chair. If another colleague has already completed the same work, Nora sees that once and is not asked to repeat it. An approved inspection still identifies its criterion and competent checker. Craft, accountability and specialist approval remain legible without burying the day in forms.

## 4. Make the welcome a human moment

Before meeting Daniel, Nora sees a short arrival brief: his supplied form of address, their agreed meeting arrangement, the event's important intention, current promises and any relevant service preference. She can then put the screen away.

“Welcome, Daniel. I'm Nora, looking after the hall today. Would you like a quick look around, or a moment to settle in first?” This fictional exchange illustrates choice and attention. It is not a prescribed script, nor a requirement to log friendliness. If Daniel declines help, that is a valid outcome; it should not reappear as an overdue hospitality task.

A deliberate **“Show organiser”** view makes it comfortable to turn the tablet around. It contains the authorised event summary, next agreed moment, useful arrangement and contact, plus the actual state of Daniel's request. The extra guests are “being checked,” not accepted. He can identify a misunderstanding without viewing internal debate.

This view is built from an explicit audience allowance. It excludes staff breaks, restricted notes, other clients, security information and private assistance details. It cannot depend on Nora remembering to collapse a sidebar. A public welcome screen has an even narrower audience and does not inherit organiser permissions.

Service notes preserve the minimum useful instruction, who supplied it, intended audience and event scope. Sensitive explanations stay out of general work packs. Lasting preferences need permission to remember, a correction path and review or expiry. No mood inference, covert recording or personality profiling is needed to offer good hospitality. Ritz-Carlton's published values place personal care beside staff involvement and guest privacy; the particular controls here are our proposed implementation of those principles. [Ritz-Carlton Gold Standards](https://ritzcarltonleadershipcenter.com/about-us/about-us-foundations-of-our-brand/)

## 5. Give unresolved requests clear responsibility

Use a tracked request when something remains unresolved, needs coordination or creates a follow-up promise. Immediately fulfilled ordinary hospitality needs no mandatory record, case or three-role form. For tracked work, defaults automatically map the three responsibilities below to the applicable duty roles and authority; one authorised person can hold all three without repeated assignment clicks.

| Responsibility | What the person owns |
| --- | --- |
| Relationship | Making sure the organiser receives an understandable update and outcome |
| Decision | Agreeing any change to the venue's commitments, within actual authority |
| Delivery | Carrying out the accepted work and reporting what happened |

For the pending extra guests, Nora can remain Daniel's relationship contact, Ellis owns the decision, and the relevant catering, setup and inventory colleagues own their contributions. Nora supplies only the practical observation needed from her. She does not become the coordinator of four new investigations because she happened to hear the request.

The request compares the current **120 guests, 20 tables of six and 120 chairs** with the requested **132 guests, 22 tables and 132 chairs**. The office assembles feasibility, resource and timing answers. Any offer changing price, time, room or layout needs internal approval **and the authorised organiser's agreement to the exact offer** before a new working release. Asking for extra guests is not blanket acceptance of its tradeoffs. Changed terms require renewed agreement. The issued change updates affected work and selectively reopens relevant checks; unrelated completed work survives.

“Ellis has this” appears only after acceptance. Before that, the duty role sees an unaccepted request with its defined fallback. A sent notification alone transfers nothing. Nora retains the relationship obligation until an accepted replacement takes it; management owns addressing the coverage gap.

If Daniel says, “The office promised it,” preserve his statement, discreetly find the relevant agreement and involve its owner. Do not make him adjudicate an internal argument. Offer a next-update promise that can be kept without implying the answer will be yes. The organiser should receive the considered choice and consequence, not the venue's internal blame or shortage discussion.

## 6. Follow Daniel's cold-room concern all the way through

At the demo's present moment, Daniel says the room feels cold. Nora first understands the need: “Is it this part of the room that feels uncomfortable?” She offers ordinary help within the venue's established discretion and Daniel's wishes. A permitted adjustment can be made by an appropriately trained person; a system fault or restriction belongs with Sam. No invented temperature limit, heating diagnosis or repair estimate appears.

Nora captures **“Daniel says the room feels cold”** after the conversation. Room, event and time are already present. The record separates the reported experience from any measured condition. Nora owns the relationship; Sam is proposed for technical delivery; Ellis owns decisions that exceed ordinary discretion. Sam must accept the work before the interface claims he has it.

Nora can say, “I'm checking it with Sam. I'll come back by 14:25 with an update.” The sheet creates an **update due**, not a promise that the room will be warm by 14:25. If the technical answer is still unknown, Nora keeps that communication promise truthfully. A reviewed immediate alternative may help, but should not be presented as available before it is checked.

Three independent outcomes remain visible:

- **Update:** Daniel was told what is happening, by whom and when.
- **Technical work:** Sam records the actual adjustment, test, unresolved fault or restriction.
- **Client outcome:** Nora checks whether Daniel's need is met, including through an agreed alternative, with “confirmed,” “still a concern” or “not yet checked.”

Sam completing an adjustment does not manufacture Daniel's satisfaction. A checked seating alternative can meet Daniel's need while Sam's technical reassessment remains open. If Daniel leaves before the check, his outcome stays unconfirmed with an agreed follow-up owner and contact method. One linked issue preserves these independent outcomes.

EHL's guidance supports understanding the actual complaint, a relevant remedy, continuing communication and checking the guest's outcome. The separate update, work and outcome interactions are a design inference that makes this useful under interruption. [EHL service-recovery guidance](https://insights.ehl.edu/customer-service-recovery-program?hs_amp=true)

Routine kindness should stay easy: offering water, an appropriate place to wait or clear directions within known provision. Material changes, compensation and technical interventions use the venue's real discretion and authority. Urgent safety, medical or security matters follow verified procedures and responsible people immediately. Care includes protecting staff; it does not require tolerating abuse or promising the impossible.

## 7. Bring Nora back to the exact place she stopped

An interruption can consume the memory of an unfinished job. Keep independent bookmarks for each room, preserving its event, issued plan, selected zone, view position and last recorded outcome. Before furniture collection, Nora might bookmark **“Layout review: resume at table H; position to confirm.”** During later physical setup, a bookmark could distinguish chairs placed from alignment unchecked. **“Return to my work”** restores that room's recorded position and note; switching rooms does not overwrite another room's place.

If the plan changed while Nora was away, show the practical difference before resuming. Never infer an unrecorded completion or treat a paused check as passed. When there was no time to bookmark, retain only known context and let her establish what remains.

Routine messages join a quiet **“Being handled”** list. **“Needs your action”** contains something Nora can actually do. Only a real urgent event interrupts immediately; time-bound decisions surface before their consequence, and useful background information waits. Repeated messages about one issue update it rather than creating several jobs.

Interruption research supports treating resumption and effort seriously, but office-task findings do not establish hallkeeper effect sizes. This specific bookmark, urgency model and focus control remain hypotheses to test. [Research scope and sources](research/veteran-work.md)

## 8. Protect the final walk that makes the room feel right

The schedule protects time before the 17:15 readiness deadline for Nora's agreed physical room check and a proud guest-perspective walk. Required checks keep their criteria and responsible people. Alongside them she considers the actual welcome: what a guest sees on entering, where a speaker will look, whether presentation feels cared for, and whether agreed personal touches are in place.

The system presents unresolved items and relevant intent, then leaves space for judgement. It does not certify comfort from a photograph or require a beauty score. Nora can record an adjustment, request help or explain why release is not yet possible. A readiness percentage must never conceal one material unresolved condition.

Different occasions invite different questions. A wedding may need a nominated deputy so the couple can participate; a corporate event may centre on speaker preparation and confidential content; a memorial may need a chosen family contact and a particular welcome. These are prompts to ask, not emotional templates. Co-op's practitioner guidance describes varied relationships and wishes around funerals and wakes; it does not justify assuming that every memorial should be sombre. [Co-op family guidance](https://www.coop.co.uk/funeralcare/advice/a-guide-to-funeral-etiquette-for-families)

## 9. Make a break possible, and finishing true

Maya's proposed cover is **15:00–15:20, routine hallkeeper duties only**. Nora remains owner until Maya accepts; accepted cover starts at 15:00. If unaccepted by 14:55, Ellis owns resolving the gap. The screen must not describe Nora as covered because a request was sent.

The handover gathers open work, clarifications and promises across Grand Hall, Saloon and Reception. Nora can add context without retyping it. Both people review all three rooms and accept that explicit scope, immediate concerns and help arrangements. Ellis retains decision authority; Sam retains accepted technical work. Any client update due during the break has an explicit relationship owner.

At 15:20 Maya and Nora cross-check open items and record return of routine duties. The timer does not impersonate that conversation. Nora's furniture collection remains at 15:30 and her room check is due by 17:15.

A full shift handover uses the same principles with its own scope and successor. Nora sees who accepted each carry-forward item and what can wait until the next staffed period. Optional care jobs never silently fill every free minute.

At the event's physical handback, the on-duty hallkeeper and Daniel take a final look together: “Before you go, let's check your belongings, anything that needs attention, and who is collecting the remaining items.” Record actual condition against the agreed starting record, note discrepancies together and distinguish observation from a disputed account. Daniel can add his account; joint recording is not an automatic admission of liability.

The handback records who remains in the building, outstanding suppliers and collections, their accepted owners, and actual departure separately from the booked end. **“Client departed,” “Room clear for cleaning” and “Ready for next use”** remain different outcomes. Cleaning receives an explicit handback; the next use awaits clearance, cleaning and required checks. If departure runs late, Ellis owns the consequences for the next booking, staffing, any proposed charges and revised timings. The hallkeeper reports the physical reality and keeps people informed without personally absorbing the lost time. Follow-up promises remain owned after handback.

The final reassurance is factual: which responsibilities ended and who owns what remains. An organiser's actual thanks can be shared appropriately. Invented praise, confetti and productivity rankings add nothing to that relief.

## 10. Remove more work than the system asks for

These are initial burden budgets for evaluation, not measured achievements:

| Interaction | Budget to test |
| --- | --- |
| Routine completion | One meaningful outcome action; no compulsory narrative |
| Reporting an exception | Enter the new observation once; do not retype known context |
| Asking about a layout | One located question, with the relevant brief attached automatically |
| Cover or shift handover | No mandatory new prose; focus effort on review and acceptance |
| Being with the organiser | No required data entry during the conversation |
| Shared issue updates | One coherent item rather than repeated competing notifications |

Deliberately remove duplicate checklists, repeated client explanations, unnecessary chair scans, speculative “smart” prompts, separate logs for the same fault and reminders about other people's routine work. Use **Today, This room and People**, with a separate **Handover** action. **Help** appears in the relevant work context.

A feature earns its place by removing a remembered obligation, a search, an avoidable trip, an unnecessary interruption or an ambiguity. If it simply makes the same burden look attractive, revise or remove it.

## 11. Deliver the whole working arrangement

The service includes a reliable device, charged spare or clear charging point, usable pen and clipboard, readable room pack, consistent store labels, kit contents cards and an available human support route. Test touch targets, sunlight, dim areas, ordinary work gloves where relevant and actual carrying conditions with staff. A phone is not always the right tool at the instant of work.

Paper carries the same event identity, issued time, landmarks, quantities, owners and exceptions in legible greyscale. A named process replaces superseded packs; a concise change slip explains the difference. A QR code supplements the essentials. It cannot guarantee that a person holding old paper has seen the new plan.

Offline use keeps the authorised cached pack and last sync visible. Pending local notes are distinct from delivered information; urgent contact has the established alternative. Reconnection reconciles conflicting revisions and avoids duplicate requests. Recovery must preserve work after a restart, not just display a reassuring banner.

Introduce the workflow with experienced and new staff together, on a small real service slice with consent. Use their language, retain a practical fallback and provide quick help. Co-op's published rollout describes maintaining paper during adoption and using visible preparation status to reduce coordination calls; that is useful precedent, not proof of our result. [Co-op Digital rollout, 2017](https://digitalblog.coop.co.uk/2017/08/30/were-live-funeralcare-colleagues-have-started-to-use-the-digital-service-in-edinburgh/)

## 12. Earn the promised benefits

Baseline an opening, setup, interruption, change and close. Observe avoidable trips, rework, repeated entry, repeated explanations, unanswered requests, missed update promises and resumption effort. Check correctness and staff workload alongside time. Ask organisers whether they knew whom to approach and whether their concern was followed through; ask hallkeepers whether the system helped them use their judgement and leave work behind.

Set realistic benefit targets after that baseline. Proposed benefits are less chasing, fewer avoidable movements, clearer responsibility, more attentive hosting and a calmer finish. None is yet proven. Passing a prototype scenario establishes only that the interaction can be demonstrated; it does not establish live integration, safe venue operation, physical-device usability or enduring satisfaction.

Keep failed rehearsals. Test declined cover, absent managers, conflicting plans, stale paper, offline updates and a technically fixed issue with an unhappy client. Let staff remove burdensome features. The standard is a day that works better for the people living it, with beauty and pride present throughout.
