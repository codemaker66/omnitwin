### As built (rulings made in the build, 6 to 12 November)

The full Grand Hall wedding fixture did not fit A on one page at the studied type sizes. The room band stood about
340 pt tall, because Keep in view with the contacts beside a 2:1 room is much taller than the plan. The store list
took 330 pt, and the page ran 240 pt over. The studies fitted by summarising the store and leaving out the
planner's words. The build keeps every fact and changes the arrangement:

- **Two columns that each run on by themselves.** The left column (60%) holds the room, then Call on the day
  beside On duty (on the paper, as in the study, not on the brick wash), then the day in order. The right column
  holds Keep in view on brick wash, then the store. The wash marks care alone. The store starts under Keep in
  view, so neither column leaves a gap.
- **The planner's words** follow the day when that keeps the page no longer, or run full width below when they
  fit there. When page 1 has no room for them (the full wedding), they open the setup page, across it and before
  the first step, and page 1 says where they are: "On page 2, above the setup's steps." (see page 2's As built).
- **The day** sets a line's date and detail after its title ("Service door opens · Suppliers and band load-in")
  when the whole line fits; otherwise it keeps the stacked form.
- **Spacing:** care lines and store lines sit a little closer. The plan's caption reads "Drawn from the saved
  layout, looking down. Copper numbers: notes, with the setup. Brick rings: keep in view." Table numbers explain
  themselves. Panel text is balanced, so a short last word never sits alone, and no line starts with " · ".
- **Measured** (header, then each column's foot): the wedding's page 1 ends 14 pt above the footer band, and the
  setup opens page 2. The board meeting takes 2 pages and the short test sheet 1.

### As built

- **A setup that fits whole under the brief on page 1** (a short event) stays there, its steps full width with no
  rail: Keep in view is already on that page. A one-page sheet stays one page.
- **When the brief itself runs onto page 2** and fills less than 40% of it, the setup starts there after a rule,
  rather than leaving most of a page empty and starting on page 3.
- **The setup's own page opens with a head** for the crew handed it alone: the event, its date, room, guest count
  and layout, and the copy's approval line, with a draft's warning in brick. The blind review of the real page 2
  found these only in the footer (glance 6 and 7 against the study's 9) and both testers asked for this strip.
  With it, the page 2 re-test scored 8 / 8 / 8.5 (calm / glance / finish). Page 1 keeps the paper check's code.
- **Page 1's pointer names the page** ("On page 2, above the setup's steps.") when both columns end on page 1, so
  the setup's page is known. Both page 1 testers found the first wording, "Printed with the setup, before its
  first step.", vague.
- **The rail's contact** shows the name, role and phone a crew calls; the email stays with the contact on page 1.
- **Next (not in PR 1):** study D sets the chairs as their own tick line in Furniture ("120 Chiavari chairs · 110
  at the rounds · 10 at the top table"). Both page 2 re-testers asked for a chair total. The setup rule (the
  paper's own; the screen keeps its list) folds seated chairs into their tables, and a seated chair's note reaches
  no row, so "Notes on the plan" holds seat notes today. The chairs line would carry them where the crew sets the
  chairs.
- **Notes on the plan** that no setup line carries are listed after the last step under their numbers, so every
  number on the plan can still be looked up (the old legend's guarantee). The manifest normally carries each note
  under its item.
- **A setup line's height** is its item column (name, chairs, notes) or its place column, whichever runs longer.
  Notes no longer start below a long place.

### As built: the review of #415 (12 to 13 November)

  a rail of five steps left no room beside it) and a mono print keeps it; the rail stands a line taller only when a
  step is late.
- **The plan's names:** a name sits on its piece when it fits there across the page, as names always run (a screen
  turned against a side wall stands tall on the page, and offers a name only its short side); else along the row of
  pieces it stands in, end to end
  (the top table's three trestles), starting from its own piece's middle and never set off that piece, so a name at
  a row's end names the end piece; else just beside its piece, its letters (capital tops to descenders) 1.5 pt from

- **Rings** come from the planner's own access and allergy notes, never the sheet's counts ("1 wheelchair space").

### As built: Setup from, then Ready by (18 November 2026, before F)

Blake chose, by the question form: "Setup from + Ready by (Recommended)". The sheet printed `timing.setupBy` as its
finish line ("Ready by", "Room ready"), but both of that field's sources start a window: the room's earliest phase
before the booking (the Day Board's setup start) or the booking start less the room's changeover time. The crowded
day's Grand Hall breakfast (Setup phase 07:00, doors 08:00) printed "Room ready 07:00".

- **One rule, `sheetTimes()` in `@omnitwin/types`,** read by the PDF, the phone card, the print line and the Day
  Board's line (through the API's summary). Setup from is `timing.setupBy`; guests in is `timing.eventStart`, the
  booking's start; Ready by is the planner's typed final-touches time when given, else guests in. The frozen
  snapshot keeps its shape: `setupBy` keeps its historical name, documented at its definition as the window's start,
  so approved sheets print the new times from fields they already hold.
- **Guests in is the booking's start**, as the Day Board (doors) and the Diary ("Doors in") read it, which is what
  Blake's option named. The planner's When ribbon reads a booking the other way (the hire window, opening with a
  Setup phase inside it); that disagreement is older than this fix, is shared by the Day Board and the Diary, and is
  left for its own ruling. For a booking whose inner handles were dragged on the ribbon, every surface still reads
  the setup start as guests in.
- **The header has five facts:** Setup from (with "60 min to set the room"), Ready by ("when guests come in", or "15
  min before guests come in"), Guests in (day and zone), Guests, Room. Widths were measured in the sheet's fonts so
  the usual captions keep to one line (a time's numeral is 79 pt): 108, 126, 96 and 74 pt, the room the rest.
- **The day** opens with "Setup starts" (the header's label is "Setup from"; the day's lines are events, as
  "Event starts" is). The planner's final-touches time is the room's ready-by time, so it always folds into "Room
  ready" and lends it its reason. A room ready as guests come in is one moment and prints once, "Room ready, guests
  in", so a sheet without a planner's final time keeps its line count. A deadline is late only after Ready by.
- **A planner's final time after guests come in is kept and said, never moved:** the Ready by figure and its caption
  ("15 min after guests come in") in brick; in the day, Room ready after Event starts with "After guests come in" in
  brick; on the rail, Room ready marked late (with its weekday when it is on another day); on the phone card, the
  figure in brick and "The planner's ready time is 15 min after guests come in."; on the print line and the Day
  Board's line, ", 15 min after guests come in". Cost if wrong: a planner who meant a later finish (candles lit once
  guests are in) sees a warning on every surface.
- **A final time before setup starts** is shown as entered, with Setup from's caption in brick: "Not before the
  room's ready-by time".
- **Missing data:** no booking keeps "No booking holds this room yet" and "Confirm the time first"; a planner's final
  time without a booking is still Ready by, in brick, "The planner's time · no booking holds this room yet". No
  setup start shows Setup from "—" with "No changeover time for this room", and Ready by at guests in.
- A time on another day than guests come in carries its day ("Fri 13 Nov · 13 h to set the room"). Durations under
  two hours stay in minutes; longer ones read "2 h 30 min".
- **Page counts hold:** the wedding 3, the board meeting 2, a short sheet 1, the breakfast 1. The wedding's day gains
  "08:00 Setup starts", page 1's last free line (its day now ends 752 pt from the top), so page 1 no longer points to
  the planner's words; they open page 2 under its head, the existing fallback. The wedding fixture's setup start
  moved from 13:30 to 08:00: under the field's meaning 13:30 contradicted its own 10:00 structure deadline.
