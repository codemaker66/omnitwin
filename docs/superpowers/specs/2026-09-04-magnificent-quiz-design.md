## 1. Is Three.js the best way

No, except where the real Hall is on screen.

A note from the session that assembled this plan. Before the synthesis landed I had leaned the other way on the twelve tableaux: Three.js in orthographic mode, on the argument that Three is already the only road to Spark and is cached for planner visitors, so one renderer beats two. I changed my mind on the numbers below. The tableau is layered planes, ink that draws itself, a pointer light and real text — everything CSS and SVG do with subpixel text and the accessibility tree intact — and 213 KB of brotli to draw eight quads is a phone's first two seconds spent on nothing. The Lantern is not a second renderer in the sense that worried me: it is the existing ~2 KB `FlameCanvas` shader grown to five effects, on its own canvas, with a CSS fallback and a measured kill criterion (Card 0; section 13). Three and Spark stay exactly where the real Hall is on screen. Card 0 measures before anyone bets. The quiz today is DOM/CSS/React with no Three.js. The chosen stack is three layers under one React page and one DOM narrative:

The stage is DOM, CSS and SVG: full-bleed painted tableaux as layered planes (three to five `<img>`/AVIF layers with pointer or tilt parallax on `camera` 120/14 springs, 7 px amplitude), DOM `<button>` pokeables positioned in percentage coordinates from a per-scene manifest, SVG ink that draws with `stroke-dashoffset`, one pointer light on CSS variables (the `useCursorLight.ts` pattern), real text in real fonts. This is the Manuscript's substrate and it is the right one: text keeps subpixel antialiasing, zoom, selection and the accessibility tree; hit-testing is native; nothing needs a GPU to be a page.

The Lantern is a raw-WebGL2 compositor of at most 20 KB raw (assumption, measured in Card 0), grown from `pages/landing/FlameCanvas.tsx` (one ~2 KB fragment shader on its own canvas with a CSS fallback beneath). It runs only the five loud beats: the fire, the frost, the ink flood, weather (rain, snow, smoke, embers), and the seal's wax. It never shares a canvas with Spark, because any off-screen pass corrupts the splat sort (`.claude/gotchas/spark-render-target-effects.md`). It is capped at about 6 Mpx of fill per frame at DPR 1.5 on a Pixel 6a class device, and every `feTurbulence`/`feDisplacementMap` idea becomes either a Lantern shader or a mask rasterised once per scene at 1024 px, never a live SVG filter on a phone.

The Hall is Three.js 0.180, R3F and Spark 2.1, exactly as `RoomSplatScene.tsx` already mounts it, for the four ceremonies only (threshold, deliberation, weighing, reveal), on medium and high tiers, desktop-gated by a phone gate above the GPU classifier (section 9). The coarsest level of a room is one tile: Grand Hall 7.2 MB / 355,593 splats, Deacon Convener's Room 5.8 MB / 254,937, Reception 5.7 MB / 260,867 (`src/data/generated/trades-hall-splat-bundles.ts`). Phones and poster/low tiers get the same ceremonies on photographed plates with a painted depth mask in the Lantern.

Why not the alternatives, with numbers. Godot 4.x web: about 5 MB brotli of wasm before any asset, fifty times the whole quiz route (34 KB JS + 10 KB CSS brotli today); threaded builds need cross-origin isolation, and `packages/web/vercel.json` sends COOP with no COEP while `index.html` loads Google Fonts and Clerk cross-origin, so isolation is a platform change; single-threaded builds carry a documented iOS Safari audio crash; a canvas with no DOM fails constraint 5; GDScript sits outside `tsc`, ESLint and Vitest. Unity 6 web: 1.8 MB empty floor, 7–15 MB realistic, same iOS and gate problems, plus a licence. PlayCanvas: ~150 KB engine that is a peer of Three, not a replacement, because Spark renders only inside Three. Rive: `@rive-app/canvas-lite` 2.85 MB unpacked, an estimated 300–450 KB brotli of wasm (assumption, Card 0 measures), no text or audio in the lite build, wasm untestable under happy-dom, and vector output, which reads as an app; kept as a bounded bake-off for the Convener skin only, off by default. Lottie: After Effects vector motion with the same taste problem. PixiJS 8: 100–160 KB brotli (assumption) for a 2D scene graph with no accessibility tree; the honest fallback if the Lantern ever outgrows itself. Pre-rendered video: no renderer JS, but 25–30 MB per run, H.264 has no alpha so every reaction patch must match a compressed loop pixel-for-pixel on an OLED, iOS Low Power Mode blocks even muted autoplay, two live decoders plus a spring layer is unmeasured, and a £42–72k pipeline whose blind-test gate can fail after sixty artist-days. A Three.js board for the twelve scenes: 960 KB raw / 213 KB brotli of Three to draw eight textured quads that CSS draws for free. Spark behind every scene: 5.7–7.2 MB per scene, twelve scenes, an unmeasured iPhone that `device-tier.ts:40` classifies as a desktop, splats that cannot relight or burn, and the Living Hall precedent: `/living-hall` (`pages/living-hall/`, still routed at `router.tsx`) put a splat behind a document, was scored 0/10 by Blake as a homepage on 2026-07-11 and was replaced at the front door by `/fresh`; it survives as a landing route, and it is the shape this plan refuses to repeat behind twelve scenes.

Budgets, brotli unless stated. Quiz shell ≤ 90 KB JS (stage engine ~20, Lantern ~8, reaction layer ~10, audio engine ~8, springs/state/telemetry the rest) and ≤ 25 KB CSS; the 491,680-byte raw (measured in `dist/assets/quiz-run-CPBMNL2f.js`) / ~90 KB brotli `quiz-run-*.js` zod chunk that one `@omnitwin/types` import drags onto the critical path is split off and fetched lazily at post time. Fonts: self-hosted latin subsets, about 190 KB (assumption, measured with pyftsubset). Per scene: five to seven AVIF/WebP planes at 1600 px for phones (≤ 300 KB) and 2560 px for desktop (≤ 600 KB), one SVG ink layer 40–120 KB, an audio sprite ≤ 250 KB; prefetched one scene ahead during 40–60 s of reading. Three (0.21 MB brotli; 0.96 MB raw) plus Spark (0.71 MB brotli measured on 2026-09-04 with Node's brotli at quality 11 on `dist/assets/spark-Dc1t-seB.js`; 4.92 MB raw, 1.75 MB gzip — Vercel serves brotli) plus one 7.2 MB tile fetched from scene 9 on desktop medium/high only, about 11 s at 6 Mbps inside three scenes of reading. First scene on 4G, assuming 6 Mbps effective and 100 ms RTT: shell ~305 KB raw plus intro art ~150 KB plus the first voice line ~120 KB, about 575 KB, around 1.5 s of transfer and about 3 s to an interactive first page with the voice streaming.

Frames. Targets, not measurements, until Card 0: 60 fps on an iPhone 12 and a Pixel 6a class Android through page-turn, ink-draw and the fire, p95 frame ≤ 18 ms medium/high and ≤ 20 ms low during the ceremony and a five-second poke storm; zero long tasks over 50 ms after the first scene is interactive. Rules that make it plausible: only compositor properties in motion (transform, opacity, mask-position); at most 3,000 visible SVG path segments drawing at once; glow on a `::after` with static shadow and spring-driven opacity, never an animated box-shadow; no `filter` on any element over 25% of the viewport below high tier; the Lantern at DPR ≤ 1.5 with a 30-frame-over-20 ms tripwire that drops to DPR 1 and 100 particles.

Hit-testing is native buttons with an optional 64×64 alpha-mask refinement for odd shapes. Audio sync keeps the media-element clock for the voice and one `AudioContext` for everything else (section 6). Accessibility is by construction: the DOM narrative exists first, the canvas layers are `aria-hidden`, every pokeable is a named button, every sound has a caption.

Authoring cost per scene, assumption at UK freelance rates: sourcing 3 h, painting 2.5–4 days, plane separation 1 day, twelve pokeables with two or three states 1.5 days, sound 1 day, engineering 1.5 sessions, QA at ten viewports 0.5 session.

## 2. The story

The spine is the year, told in the Hall on the night before Deacons' Choosing Day. The Convener has watched the choosing since the Hall opened in 1794; he wears the Chain of 1887 (a chain of some kind since 1766); he claims no council vote, because he has had none since 1975. He does not narrate your year to you; he puts you back in it, present tense, one threshold at a time, as a Dungeon Master does. The twelve scenes keep their titles, worlds and vectors from `craft-quiz-model.ts`, so the memories, the reachability test and the band survive. The burgh is unnamed and stays unnamed, and it is a Lowland burgh with the name worn off: a river, a Cross, a Tolbooth, a Green, closes and wynds, ports rather than a curtain wall, because Glasgow never had one and a member will know it. The scene-1 text ("a wall on three sides") moves with the pivot: the water gate becomes the Water Port on the river, and "the wall" in scene 12 becomes the port's walk. Glasgow is named once, at the roll. The Hall is named throughout.

The law of the night is glossed at the threshold in his own mouth, so members hear a game and not an institutional error: the crafts choose their own, the way they always have; tonight, once a year, the wall is allowed one name. That makes his wound his and not the House's. The wound: forty years ago or two hundred, it is all one to the paint, he called a name into the wrong line of the book, and a man lived his life under it. He lets it slip at the end of each act and says it plainly in the deliberation preamble; one sentence of it goes into the ordinary near-miss line so every run closes the thread, not only the 32% that deliberate (`FORMAT-RESEARCH-2026-08-16.md:36`, latent-temperament respondents; 38.5% of dice paths in the 40,000-run measurement of section 0). He never names the man, the Craft, or his own Craft; one poke line has someone else ask and him decline, so the withholding is heard rather than hoped for.

Three pulls, all mechanical. The latch line: a slot of its own after the reply and any aside, at most fourteen words, naming the next place or object, no question, no forecast of the reader ("The frost is early this year. There is a pot in the square. Come and look at it."). Budgeted in `fit.mjs` at 375×667 with a four-line reply. It replaces the Q7 omen, which is deleted. Thursday: a real filmed actor, small and backlit, somewhere in all twelve tableaux, with twelve captioned lines of her own story that remember whether you found her last time; never voiced, never scored; at the reveal the three town-remembers memories are spoken as hers. The ledger: one gold stroke a scene on a strip that is on screen throughout; the year turns underneath it on the quarter days (Lammas at the port, Michaelmas at the fence, Hallowmas in the upstairs room, Yule in the square, Candlemas at the door, the March thaw at the leat, Beltane on the step, midsummer fire, Lammas again on the walk), and the fourteen arms in the dome stay dark for eleven scenes.

The three acts are Persona, Shadow and Self, never named on screen, marking a change of paper and light: Act I daylight and arrival (1–4), Act II dusk and candle (5–8), Act III the turn of the year with the Fire as its one night (9–12). Jung lives in the axes (the design doc found a3 is introversion/extraversion, a1 Senex/Puer, a4 Hero/Guardian, a5 Prometheus/Hestia) and in the reveal's title for the Craft, never in scoring.

The twelve beats, with form from brief 17 and tone (F funny, S sublime, K knife): 1 The River Gate, the bag, the port-keeper who is decent and doing a job, F2/F3, warm; latch: the river is low this year. 2 The Low River, the child, the three who did not move, F9 in miniature, K; latch: a fence in the middle of a field. 3 The Fence, the woman who says it has always been there, F11 inverted, F; latch: the well and what they will call you. 4 The Naming, the well, the book, Thursday, F; first slip of the sleeve. 5 The Other One, the rival who is worse and loved, F6, K. 6 The Ring, the old man's stock, F3 with a clock, K. 7 The Thin Winter, the empty pot, two days, F10, S and warm. 8 The Door You Were Told Of, eleven days and the smell of damp, F11, K with a laugh; second slip. 9 What the Burgh Is Built On, the leat through the low cottages, F5, S the terrible kind. 10 The Apprentice, the child at the door four mornings, F2 reversed, warm. 11 The Fire, one crossing of the room, F9, K. 12 The Wall, dawn, the bench with your name, the body that decides, F4 with you as the court, S.

The deliberation (top two within 0.15) is the hush: the Deacon Convener's Room at night on the real tile, two candles, two seals, no pokeables, identical staging for every pair, the authored scene or the composed one, the preamble carrying the wound. The weighing is a walk the length of the Grand Hall along the scanner's own stations while the ledger is read back in his voice over three beats of 1,600 ms (`CONVENER_DELIBERATION_BEAT_MS`), the dome still dark, touching does nothing and the caption says so: "Wheesht. ## 3. The voice

This is a pivot from Le Guin, recorded as such. Rothfuss supplies the scene: a storyteller who enjoys the telling, exact numbers where a lesser writer says "many", named things precise enough to feel like spells (the leat, the deid bell, a lock of four throws with a key to each throw), a frame narrator who knows the ending and chooses what to tell, one withheld fact per scene. Pratchett supplies the aside, the pokes and the reveal: the record's absurdity taken seriously (the box opened before the whole court and found empty, 1882; a fine paid in candle-wax; twelve bakers sent back to the Tolbooth until they signed, 1696; the Weavers' one-year deacon "for avoiding of all superioritie and tyrannie"; 296 Wrights admitted to steal a vote), institutions seen clear and loved anyway, nobody small the butt. The two never share a sentence. The "silence in three parts" device is banned outright; it is quotation, not register.

Ten rules a writer obeys and a reviewer checks, with the critique's corrections applied. 1 Shape: sentence lengths vary (shortest under 6, longest over 20, none over 30); no mandated comma sentence, the reviewer hears the shape rather than counts it. 2 One named thing per scene, understood from its sentence; Tier-2 words at most one per scene, glossed within twelve words. 3 Numbers exact or absent. 4 Laugh address: cost lines flat; body at most one; aside at most one; quiz-wide at least four of twelve bodies warm. 5 Load-bearing joke: strike it and the scene loses a fact. 6 The butt is a procedure, never a person, an option or the reader. 7 No funny option: rank the four by wit, no clear winner. 8 The devil has the best sentence. 9 The scene ends on the choice; the latch line ends the aside slot, imperative permitted, "you will" forbidden. 10 The withheld thing: the reviewer names it. Editor rules that survive the pivot: no proverbs written to be quoted; one quotable line per scene across body, reply and aside combined, and it must be about a procedure; no "never once"; no post-choice grading by cadence ("does a great deal of work", "you wrote it anyway" are lean and fail). Tic counts per everything a run can speak, memories and deliberations included: "four hundred miles" ≤ 2, "which is not the same as" ≤ 1, "That is the whole of the thing" 0, "and you knew it before" 0, "the way a … does" ≤ 2.

Hollywood Scots. Scots nouns and world in standard English and no eye-dialect at all: no ye, yer, dinna, canna, o', wi', nae, -it past tenses. Cadence carries Scotland, and the cadence is defined: the kirk-session minute-book crossed with the Authorised Version, long clause, short verdict, plus a syntax kit an American hears without decoding, capped at one per scene: "the now", "outwith", "I'm away to", "Is it yourself?", fronting ("See that man? He…"), "wait while", "I was wanting". Tier 1, free and guessable, ≤ 1 per 40 words: burgh, close, wynd, vennel, land (a tenement), kirk, bairn, wee, aye (never as filler), lass, lad, ken (≤ 1 per run), muck, midden, brae, tron. Tier 2, ≤ 1 per scene, glossed in its sentence: tolbooth, howff, siller, kist, deacon, mortcloth, causey, leat, stoup, dreich, thrawn, canny, douce, provost, unlaw, Martinmas, Lammas, Candlemas, Beltane. Tier 3, insider words only in reveal paragraphs and deliberation costs, ≤ 1 per paragraph, self-glossed: pendicle, cowan, cavell, Visitor, Goudie, essay, brod penny, litster, toorie. Struck from the earlier tiers as tartanry or false friends: loch, glen, bothy, gloaming, kailyard, "the morn". "Cry" is glossed once at the threshold ("At dawn the roll is called, cried, from the stair") and the weighing uses "call". Banned outright: och, lassie, bonnie, braw, hoots, Scotch (except the quoted 1947 bonnet advice), any Burns line, tartan as costume, bagpipes before the roll, "Highland" anything, kilts, 'tis, mayhap, and every psychology word. A Scots word never satisfies the lexicon test: "siller" is not a Hammermen hit, "iron" is; the register test in `craft-quiz-convener-script.test.ts` is replaced, not deleted, to permit Tier 1, cap Tier 2 at one and keep the §5.4 ban list. Frequency test: read aloud to someone from Ohio; more than one "what does that mean" fails; zero Tier-1 words is Le Guin and fails too.

The gate: brief 17's twelve boxes plus twelve from this section (shape, named thing, numbers, laugh address, load-bearing joke, butt, no funny option, devil's sentence, latch, withheld, tics, lore with every fact traced to a dossier line, the Maltmen and pruning-knife corrections applied, no council vote claimed). Script-checkable now: names, ban list, tiers, sentence length, latch length, tics. The rest run aloud in the writers' room with a stopwatch on the sixty-second defence.

Scene 4, The Naming (funny). Scene: "A burgh cannot hold two of anything for long, so most people here are called for what they do. There is a Reed, and a Kettle, and a woman called Thursday, and the one time I asked why, four people told me four different things. None of them agreed and all of them were certain. At the well they ask what you are to be called. They are not making conversation. The clerk has the book open, and the book has outlived everyone in it." Your own name (coopers): The one your mother used. It means nothing here, which is why it is still yours. A thing kept shut holds better. Cost: A name that says nothing has to be filled by you, alone, and slowly, and the book will not help. The work (bakers): Let them call you by what your hands do. Half the burgh is named that way, and it tells a hungry stranger which door has bread behind it. Cost: On the day you cannot do the work, you will not know what is left of you. Whatever they land on (maltmen): They will choose one anyway, out of some small thing you did on a Tuesday. Let them. The people with the cellar and the barley were twice told by Parliament they were no trade at all, and went on making malt, and are still here. Cost: You hand them the naming. Some of what they see in you, you will not like, and it will stick. Something you are not yet (dyers): Take the name of the thing you mean to become and spend forty years catching it. Wool takes the colour it is put in. So can a person. Cost: Every day until then, the name is a small lie you wear in public, and this burgh keeps count. Replies: "Your own. Nothing in it for them to take hold of. You will be filling it yourself, and slowly." / "The work, then. Short and true, and it tells a stranger where to bring a broken thing." / "You let them choose. I have seen that be trust and I have seen it be tiredness." / "A name you have not earned. Forty years to catch it, and it will be watching you the whole way." Aside: "The book, since you ask, is the same book. Thursday is in it under three spellings, and not one of them is Thursday. ## 4. The twelve worlds

One rule governs every tableau and is enforced by a lint: pokeables, captions and sound filenames never carry a word from `WORLD_LEXICON` of any of the scene's four option worlds unless the scene text already carries it, never name a Craft, and never show the iconic object of any option world (the boat pole in scene 2 is an option, so it is not a pokeable). Every scene has one saturated hue, the light source, and 90% of the frame in near-black and earth. Provenance tags: (P) photographed, (PD) public-domain plate with archive, (I) commissioned by the one hand, (F) filmed practical, (Pr) procedural, (C) captured room. The document genre is the frame the tableau sits in (paper, hand, margins); the tableau fills the phone screen.

What the phone loses is parallax, not the ceremony.

## 5. The interaction vocabulary and the choice ceremony

One primitive: `react(target, gesture)` composes a spring from `lib/springs.ts` (pokes on `placementBounce` 180/12, commits on a new `strike` 420/32, the camera and retreats on 120/14), one scheduled sound, one caption and an optional aside. Every pokeable is a pure reducer with the clock injected, states idle, touched, answered, tired, asleep, generalising `convener-state.ts`; a test proves the reducer imports nothing from `craft-quiz-model.ts`. Anticipation on fine pointers only (2 px lift on 120/14, the nearest light warming 4%); on touch the first tap is the anticipation. Reaction: one spring, one cue within the same rAF frame, one caption. Persistence: per-scene state in a Zustand slice `pokes: Record<sceneId, Record<propId, count>>`, cleared between runs; the things that persist for the run are listed in the persistence table below. Rarity is deterministic: stage n at count n or on a stated condition, identical for everyone; rare stages key on distinct props touched, not counts, so nobody is rewarded for drumming. Pokeables are inert while the scene line is being spoken (a poke yields only the caption "(he is still speaking)"), and duck to −9 dB under a reply. Every poke dispatches activity to the Convener so a minute on the board does not earn the doze. Haptics: `navigator.vibrate` 8 ms poke, 15 ms commit, Android only; iOS Safari has none and we say so.

The choice ceremony. Desktop: one tap commits. Phone: the held card. A tap lifts the option into an absolutely positioned overlay within the stage showing lead, body and cost line; the "Costs you" line is read through the live region; a second tap commits; tap-away or Escape puts it back. This is an instrument repair as much as a layout one: today the phone commits on bare leads with the cost hidden. The cost line itself goes to ≥ 12 px, alpha ≥ 0.9, contrast ≥ 4.5:1 at every viewport, replacing the 9–10.5 px uppercase at `TradesHouseCraftQuizPage.css:2064`. ## 6. Sound

Two clocks, one graph, the voice outside it. The voice stays the single `HTMLAudioElement` in `convener-voice.ts`, unlocked by a muted play inside BEGIN; the typewriter reads its `currentTime`. Everything else is one `AudioContext` (`latencyHint: "interactive"`) created in the same gesture with a one-sample silent buffer played for iOS. Four gain buses (ambience, music, sfx, ui) sum by construction to ≤ −3 dBFS; a `DynamicsCompressorNode` exists only as a crash guard for a stuck loop (knee 0, threshold −6, ratio 12) and is not a limiter, because it has no true-peak detection and Chromium applies makeup gain; the build gate renders the whole graph through an `OfflineAudioContext` and measures that, not the raw files. The voice ducks the others from its own events: ambience pre-duck on `play`, music duck and the quill's stop on `playing` (the `play` event fires before any sound exists), un-duck 3 dB on `waiting`, release suppressed when a `play` follows a `pause` within 250 ms (a src swap). Voice lines are prefetched one scene ahead with `fetch()` into Blob URLs keyed by manifest hash and set on the shared element, because `<link rel=prefetch as=audio>` is unwired today and WebKit ignores it anyway; a scene's blobs are evicted when the next scene's line ends. Interruptions: on `statechange`, `visibilitychange` and the next `pointerdown`, resume; if `sampleRate` changed (AirPods mid-run) tear down, rebuild and re-decode. Beds fade out on hidden and in on visible; random one-shots schedule only the next event.

Sprites and loops: one MP3 sprite per scene plus one global, decoded once; expected decoded sample counts stored per asset so per-browser encoder-delay differences are corrected at decode time; cues padded 50 ms and started 25 ms early; beds looped as two overlapping sources with an equal-power crossfade rather than `loopStart/loopEnd`; soaked on Safari, Chrome and Firefox. Memory: stems mono (poles panned in code), loops 32 s, one axis stem set resident at a time, per-scene sprites released on Continue, shared cues resident; decoded ceiling stated at ≤ 60 MB after the corrected constant (352.8 KB/s stereo, 176 mono) and measured on the phone pass.

Beds are the scene's place, weather and hour, never a Craft's workshop: six shared mono elements (water, wind, fire, room, voices, night) mixed per scene by gain, the room element being `useRoomTone` refactored to `buildToneGraph(context, output)` so it lives on the ambience bus (a Card 3 task, not free). Scene 1 opens on room plus water and lets wind and voices fade in as they land, so the first scene has about 400 KB of audio in flight, asserted by the e2e. The Fire's inverted duck is tiered: desktop and headphones as designed, a phone-speaker tier at −27 LUFS with a peaking cut near 3 kHz while the voice plays. The candle's gutter is a scene-seeded random walk that drives both the flame's CSS variable and the noise burst, never the pointer.

The tap vocabulary: material pokes (four round-robin variants, ±3% pitch, ±1.5 dB, under 400 ms, transient within 5 ms of file start); living things that reply and cool down; instruments of office that play full once per scene then shortened; modulators that change the bed for zero bytes. Four voices maximum; the third tap on one object within ten seconds at −6 dB and half length; a per-scene budget of 24 full-value plays after which the room goes quiet rather than dead. No note ever sounds on a material poke. Hover is silent. Commit is one press cue on the option button's pointerdown (and on Enter keydown), one lift cue on commit, pitch fixed and identical for all four seats; the cost line arrives in silence. Reply: quill, page-turn, ink dot. Deliberation: one clarsach note. Weighing: three ticks scheduled on the context clock and posted to the component so the dots render from the same schedule. Reveal: the 1887 chain settling (recorded), the winning Craft's dossier signature once at full length, the Hall's bell, twelve seconds of tutti, the room tone under the confession.

Score: clarsach, fiddle, smallpipes, tabor, bell; pipes exactly twice in a run. The recording day precedes the composer's brief so the real bells' strike notes fix the mode, not the reverse. The scene's axis is authored data in the model (six of the twelve "measured" axes were ties), and the second stem responds symmetrically to focus (any focus lifts the scene's pair together) so no per-option vector is audible. Loudness: voice normalised per section, not per line, with short-term max −12 LUFS and true peak −2 dBTP; music −20 LUFS alone; beds −30 (Fire −24); pokes peaks −12 dBFS.

The voice. ## 7. Art direction, pipeline and provenance

Direction. Warcraft's grammar, not its costume: a small figure against a thing too large for the frame, one saturated practical in a desaturated world, silhouettes that read at thumbnail size, materials that show age in the first second. The objects come from the dossiers; the camera comes from Turner and John Martin, who are the source of that grammar. Two registers, stated: the twelve tableaux are the past and take the 1710 test (nothing a Glasgow burgess of 1710 would not recognise; scene 3 is dated 1780–1840 and says so); the Hall scenes are the present, real and dated (the 1819 Chair, the 1887 chain, the 1888 window, the 1903 frieze), checked by the insider gate for accuracy, not period; the Convener is the hinge. Palette: keep the ivory and brass (`--craft-cream`, `--craft-gold`), retire the navy field, retire tartan as a surface entirely (broadcloth was the burgess's cloth; tartan at the Hall is a black-tie custom). A luminance floor per frame (10th-percentile luma and a midtone anchor on the hero silhouette) and a lit-room vignette variant, because members take this on phones under chandeliers. Type: display in a Scotch Roman or a Wilson revival with a Glasgow provenance (Miller Display or a licensed Alexander Wilson revival; licence cost assumption), text in EB Garamond; Cinzel retired as the fantasy-template tell; no blackletter, knotwork, glowing text or drop shadows; titles keep DOM text with one photographed laid-paper texture and a masked impression, printed only after the text freezes.

The sublime, assigned homes and nowhere else: vastness by scale contrast (1, 12, the reveal); obscurity that resolves (2, 6, 8, the deliberation at a fixed beat); power as one overwhelming practical (11, 9); light against dark (every frame, ceremonially the 1888 window); the count the mind cannot picture (the boards behind the deliberation, the medal's century of names, the fourteen arms at once). No motes because motes are pretty.

Pipeline. Period sources only where the reproduction licence is explicit: Rijksmuseum (CC0; Luyken's 1694 trades), the Met Open Access (CC0), Wellcome (CC-BY/PD), NLS maps and Slezer (CC-BY, assumption), Yale Center for British Art (PD); Glasgow City Archives and NGS as permission requests with fees. ## 8. Fairness and psychometrics

## 13. Risks and kill criteria

Phone performance is unmeasured for every tier but high; Card 0 exists to make it a number, and below 24 fps measured the Hall falls to plates and the Lantern to CSS. Kill: if the Manuscript-style page itself cannot hold 45 fps on the Pixel 6a with one Lantern beat, the Lantern is cut and the five beats become pre-baked masks. The AI-look gate on Card 7 is the programme's real gate: if the first tableau is flagged above chance by the lineup, no further art is commissioned until the pipeline is redesigned, for the price of one scene. The Convener shoot can read as costume drama; the painting is the recorded fallback. Thursday (the filmed figure in all twelve tableaux) is the one narrative device that rides on the practicals day: if that day cannot deliver her twelve plates through the lineup, she is cut from the tableaux and survives as the three spoken memories at the reveal, and no scene text depends on her being seen. The writing pivot can break the band or lean; the stage ships on the Le Guin text and the pivot follows. The illustrator is the critical path; engineering runs ahead on placeholder art and nothing waits on a scene. iOS audio: routing the voice through Web Audio was rejected for this reason; the remaining risks (interruption, route change) have handlers and a phone test with headphones paired mid-run. The no-scroll law against a stage with a held card, a ledger strip and six pokeables is the layout risk; `fit.mjs` runs on every card and the smallest viewports may carry four pokeables in view. Memory on Safari is bounded by the decoded ceiling and measured. The House may refuse the Chain or the bell; the weighing falls back to the captured room and the reveal bell to a licensed one, declared. The precedence order beyond three anchors is inference; it is not spoken until verified. Licences at NLS and NGS are assumptions; anything unverifiable is replaced, not argued. The experiment may show H1 false; the plan says so in advance and the quiz still ships on its other merits if the bias gates pass. A future contributor adding a drei `ContactShadows` or mounting the Lantern with Spark breaks the sort; the never-together test and the gotcha file are the defence. Kill criteria for the whole programme: a second failed lineup after redesign; a measured phone floor under 30 fps on the plain stage; any bias gate failing twice on the same scene.

## 14. What we deliberately do not do

We do not touch a vector, weight, threshold or test in the instrument; every scene remains data in the same shapes. We do not put Spark behind the twelve scenes; four soft rooms with ink drawn over them are not twelve themed screens, and the Living Hall was taken off the front door for exactly that shape. We do not ship a game engine or a second 3D runtime; the numbers in section 1 are recorded so nobody reruns the argument. We do not pre-render the twelve as video; 25–30 MB a run, no alpha and iOS Low Power Mode decide it. We do not let any generated pixel ship, including upscaled scans; the scrutiny is the expensive part and one miss costs the members' trust. We do not rig faces or cut figures out of six engravers' plates; drift is what a member's eye reads as generated. We do not sort by archetype, name a Craft before the roll, light a chain link or a shield mid-run, or let a pokeable stand next to an option. We do not use timers, streaks, scarcity or dice; rarity is authored and listed. We do not add motes and strings to the venue viewer; the critics were right about that surface, and the line we hold here is provenance and restraint: real plates, real rooms, one hue of light, sound that is the place and not a score, and a ceremony that is quiet until the arm is lit. We do not keep a narrator nobody can name the painter of. We do not throw away the quiz that exists: its instrument is the floor everyone stands on, and its DOM run is the screen reader's narrative under every stage we build above it.

## 4. Assets: formats, sizes, loading

Planes: AVIF with a WebP sibling, 1600 px wide for `coarse pointer` or width < 980, 2560 px otherwise, each plane sharing one alpha-carrying layer set; the poster is one 64 px LQIP as a data URI inside the manifest (about 1.5 KB) painted immediately. Budget per scene: ≤ 300 KB phone, ≤ 600 KB desktop, asserted by `tools/quiz-assets` and by the Vitest gate on the generated JSON. Loading order per scene is poster (from the manifest, zero requests) → coarse (a single 800 px composite AVIF, ≤ 60 KB, one request) → the sharp plane set, each `HTMLImageElement.decode()`d before it is swapped in, so no plane paints mid-decode; every request runs under one `AbortController` per scene that `prefetch.ts` cancels if the reader leaves before it lands. Ink: one SVG per scene, 40–120 KB, inlined into the DOM (not `<img>`) so `stroke-dashoffset` can be driven; the `segments` count from the manifest is the 3,000-visible-path budget. Masks for the Lantern: 1024 px single-channel PNG rasterised at build time, ≤ 40 KB each.

Audio: one MP3 sprite per scene (≤ 250 KB) plus one global sprite, decoded with `decodeAudioData` once and held as `AudioBuffer`s; the map carries `expectedSamples` so the loader can trim encoder delay by comparing `buffer.length` to expectation. Beds are six mono 32 s elements in the global sprite. Voice lines stay MP3 until Card 18 re-encodes to AAC-LC 64 kbps `.m4a`; the manifest's `file` field changes extension and nothing else. ## 5. Renderer integration

Three renderers, one page, one DOM narrative. The page renders, in DOM order: the progress region (`Question N of 12`, untouched), the narrative (the scene text, the option buttons, the reply panel, the aside, the latch), then the stage. Screen readers therefore hear scene → options → reply → things in the room, which is the plan's "DOM narrative first".

The no-scroll law with a full-bleed stage: the stage is fixed and so contributes nothing to `scrollHeight`; the narrative column is `height: 100dvh; overflow: clip` on its own wrapper (never on `body`, which the existing e2e measures); the held-card overlay and the reply panel are absolutely positioned inside that wrapper; retreating cards translate toward the chosen one, and because `overflow: clip` (unlike `hidden`) neither creates a scroll container nor lets a transformed child extend the document, `expectNoPageScroll` holds mid-spring. ## 6. Audio engine

The voice keeps its media element and its clock; `displayCharsSpokenBy` keeps reading `audio.currentTime`; nothing in `convener-voice.ts`'s alignment path changes. Everything else is `audio-engine.ts`: one `AudioContext({ latencyHint: "interactive" })` created inside the BEGIN gesture right after `unlockConvenerVoice()` at page line 666, a one-sample silent buffer started for iOS, four `GainNode` buses (ambience, music, sfx, ui) into a `DynamicsCompressorNode` (knee 0, threshold −6, ratio 12) into destination; bus gains are constants whose sum is asserted ≤ −3 dBFS by a unit test on the pure mix table. `ducker.ts` is a pure reducer over voice events (`play`, `playing`, `waiting`, `pause`, `ended`) producing target gains per bus with the 250 ms src-swap suppression; the adapter subscribes to the shared element's events and applies `setTargetAtTime`. `interruptions.ts` handles `statechange` → `resume()` on next `pointerdown`, `visibilitychange` → bed fade, and a `sampleRate` change → close, rebuild, re-decode from the retained encoded bytes. `sprite-loader.ts` keeps encoded `ArrayBuffer`s for the resident sprites so a rebuild never refetches. ## 7. State machine

The page's `QuizScreen` union becomes an explicit machine in `state/run-store.ts`, a Zustand store with a pure `transition(state, event, nowMs)` in `state/run-machine.ts` so every transition is unit-testable without React:

intro → threshold (BEGIN: seed drawn from `crypto.getRandomValues`, `positions` computed by `run-seed.ts`, arm assigned, prefs read, audio unlocked) → scene(n) with sub-states speaking → open → held (phone only) → committed → replying → latched → scene(n+1) … → deliberating (when `deliberate()` returns non-null) → weighing (three beats on the audio clock) → revealing (roll, hold, arm, ink) → result → intro (reset).

Rules the machine holds: pokes are accepted only in open/held/latched (inert while speaking, and the reducer returns the "(he is still speaking)" caption instead); `hung` is computed once on entering weighing from `hungBeforeDeliberation`; `answers[n]` stores `positions[n]` mapped back to the canonical index; `sceneMs[n]` is written on commit from the injected clock. Wall-clock reads (`durationMs`, `answerMs`) stay where they are in the page, and the `Date.now` grep test allowlists exactly those three lines by pattern. Motion state never enters any store: springs live in refs stepped by one rAF loop per mounted module (stage, ceremony, hall), each parking on `isSpringSettled`.

`pokes-store.ts` is `Record<sceneIndex, Record<propId, PokeableState>>` plus the three run-persistent facts (`initial`, `charAdvance`, `dogWhistled`), cleared on BEGIN; `distinctPropsTouched` is derived, never stored. `stage-store.ts` holds `sceneIndex`, `arm`, `effectiveTier`, `prefetch: Record<sceneIndex, "idle"|"inflight"|"ready"|"failed">`.

## 11. Not buildable as written, and what to do instead

The lazy zod chunk. The plan says the chunk "is split off and fetched lazily at post time". `@omnitwin/types` has only a root export, so a lazy import still costs 492 KB raw. Build the subpath export in Card 2 (section 4); the alternative, a hand-rolled validator in the web package, would duplicate the schema and drift, which `project_api_types_drift.md` already records as a standing risk.

`fit.mjs` on every card. `tmp/` is gitignored; `tmp/fit.mjs` is not in the repo and cannot be a gate. Card 0 promotes it to `scripts/quiz-fit.mjs` with a package script, pointed at `E2E_PORT` rather than the hard-coded 5192.

The OfflineAudioContext gate "in CI". happy-dom has no `OfflineAudioContext`, so it cannot be a Vitest gate; and CI's E2E job is recorded as never finishing (T-583, memory `project_ci_deploy_topology.md`). It is a Playwright spec run per file locally in every card's ship pass, and it joins CI only once T-583 is closed. The same applies to the mobile pull-to-refresh spec: Chromium emulation cannot trigger the browser's own pull-to-refresh; the e2e asserts the CSS guards and `scrollY` after a CDP touch drag, and the real gesture is verified on the Pixel 6a through `poke-budget.mjs`'s CDP connection.

`RoomSplatScene` for the ceremonies. It has no prop to hold the coarse level; it starts the finest level after 15 s. ## Appendix D — Sound per scene (from the sound specialist's spec, keyed to the scene text)

The rule that governs every bed: ambience is the scene's place, weather and hour, never a Craft's workshop. A bed that smelled of forge under scene 7 would tell a member which world is winning, which is the Q7 omen leak (anti-pattern X8) in another sense. The four worlds present in a scene are audible only in the four pokeables (§4), which is where the world is already allowed to show.

The beds are built from six shared element loops, each mono, 20–24 s, and mixed per scene by gain so twelve distinct rooms cost six files:

water (river, mid-distance), wind (exterior, over a wall), fire (a hearth at rest), room (the existing `useRoomTone` graph, procedural, zero bytes), voices (a market murmur with no intelligible words, recorded, not synthesised), night (crickets absent — Glasgow; use a very quiet wind-through-thatch with distant dog).

Per scene, keyed to the text in `craft-quiz-model.ts`:

1. **The River Gate.** Water 0.6, wind 0.3, voices 0.15. A rope creak one-shot every 20–40 s (random, scheduled on the context clock), gulls twice a minute at most, a far bell at scene entry only. Bright, outdoors, arrival.
2. **The Low River.** Water 0.35 but filtered to the shallow, quick trickle of a summer river (a second water sample, not a gain change), wind 0.2, one insect drone. No voices: three people stand and do not move, so the bed must be uncomfortably quiet. This is the scene where silence is the design.
3. **The Fence.** Wind 0.55 across pasture, a fence-wire tick when the wind gusts (the gust is an LFO on the wind gain; the tick fires when the gain crosses a threshold, so they are causally linked and the ear knows it). Sheep once, far. No water.
4. **The Naming.** Voices 0.35 close, a well: bucket chain and a drop of water into a deep shaft on entry, wind 0.1. Midday. The murmur ducks to 0.15 when he speaks the question so the town is listening.
5. **The Other One.** Room 0.4 (a shop interior), a wall clock ticking at 60 bpm from the far right, muffled street voices 0.1 through a window. The clock is the score's metronome for this scene (§6).
6. **The Ring.** Room 0.5 with the low-pass pulled down to 160 Hz (a closed upper room), a floorboard settle one-shot every 30–60 s, a candle: the `FlameCanvas` already has a guttering signal, and a filtered noise burst keyed to the same disturbance value gives a candle that sounds as it looks. Nothing outside.
7. **The Thin Winter.** Wind 0.5 with a winter filter (high-pass raised to 200 Hz, wind sounds thin), no water, no voices, a crow. A pot in a square: on entry, one metallic knock, then nothing goes into it. The empty pot is a pokeable that returns an empty ring.
8. **The Door You Were Told Of.** Room 0.45, a drip from behind the door at 7 s intervals, panned hard-ish left where the door sits, gaining 1 dB each time he finishes a line. The smell of damp made audible.
9. **What the Town Is Built On.** Water 0.5 but as a leat: a mill race, constant, plus the mill's wooden thunder at 0.2, low-passed, felt more than heard. Voices 0.1. This is the only scene whose water is a machine.
10. **The Apprentice.** Room 0.35, a cold morning outside the door (wind 0.2 high-passed), and the joint being cut: a slow saw stroke cycle, nine strokes, then a pause, then again, as texture on the bed. Never a hammer here.
11. **The Fire.** Fire 0.8 in a different register (the hearth sample is wrong; this needs a structural fire recording, roaring, with thatch crackle), wind 0.3, voices shouting at 0.25 with no words, and a low 45 Hz rumble under everything on desktop, high-passed away on phones. The one scene where the bed is loud and the ducking is inverted: he speaks OVER the fire at his normal level and the fire drops only 2 dB, because the fire should make his calm frightening.
12. **The Wall.** Water 0.3 (the river gate, heard from the wall side), wind 0.3, and the voices of scene 1 at 0.1 but pitched down two percent and with more people in them: the same town, a year older. The far bell from scene 1 returns at entry. A listener who has been paying attention hears the rhyme.

Threshold: the Hall. `useRoomTone` at its shipped values, plus the real Grand Hall's own room tone if the recording day (§9) happens, cross-faded in under it. Deliberation and weighing: every bed fades to the `room` element over 2.5 s; the world stops to let him think. Result: see §5.

Beds enter with a 1.8 s fade and cross-fade over the scene transition, never a hard cut. Between scenes the outgoing bed is faded before the incoming one is started so a phone never decodes two long loops at once.

## Appendix E — The insider pool: details a member recognises and a stranger enjoys (from the research fuel; every line traces to a dossier)

These are the raw material for the one insider line per scene (Card 8) and for the poke captions. Nothing here may be used as a scene backdrop, prop or sound that would identify an option's world (section 8's neutrality lint); they belong in asides, the reveal, the deliberation costs and the credits.

**hammermen**

- No man may be Deacon who has not first been Collector, and between the two he must 'lie fallow' a year; once elected, acceptance of office is compulsory.
- The Deacon Convener's common box (1644) had three outer keys — Tailors, Cordiners, the Maltmen's Visitor — and one inner key, held by the Hammermen: precedence made physical.
- The Legend of the Pears: at the Breakfast the new Deacon tells a shaggy-dog tale in which the word 'pears' must be evaded until the final sentence, because saying it was the signal for the whole company to scramble for pears heaped on salvers.
- The Gill Stoup proceeds are spent at the Collector's sole, unaudited discretion — 'one of the most jealously guarded privileges of his office' — for members in immediate need.
- The craft's grandfather clock (c.1757) was made by Robert Breakenrig, prosecuted as an unfreeman, who paid stallanger's dues all his life rather than join; the outsider's masterpiece is kept in the clerk's room.
- 1648: no master may convene 'vnles they haif ane honest hatt vpon yr. heid', forty shillings toties quoties, after members disgraced the craft 'by bringing vpon yr. heids old bonnets'.
- In Glasgow the goldsmiths stayed inside the Hammermen (unlike Edinburgh, where they split off and ranked first) — a Glasgow goldsmith is a hammerman.
- _Pitfalls:_ The James Watt persecution is a myth: Lumsden traced it through twenty works to one mild sentence of Joseph Black's and found 'absolutely no record'. Never stage it as fact; stage it, if at all, as the accusation the craft lived under. · Which side the Hammermen fought on at Langside (1568) is not in any consulted source, and even 'borne vs captured' for the banner is unresolved. The Falkirk-1746 banner on the modern website is uncorroborated by Lumsden or Muir. · No Glasgow evidence of St Eloi's feast being kept; the horse-leg re-shoeing legend is general hagiography, not Glasgow. The oath's wording is lost. · The chain of trades (links shaped as anchor, steam hammer, propeller, locomotive, light bulb) begins 1830/1850 and runs past the 1200–1850 window; use sparingly and date it. · The Rouping bonnet is a blue Kilmarnock bonnet with red toorie — that object belongs to the Bonnetmakers' world; do not let a Hammermen scene lean on it. · Hammermen–Tailors sit at cos 0.77; the split is A3 — does the mechanism work (material proof) versus does it suit the body and the time (fit). Write the forge as truth-of-the-thing, never as fit. · Synthesis flag: the craft has a real Keep streak (boxes, keymasters, the 380-folio Decree 'lodged in the box'); do not write Hammermen-serving options as pure Make.

**tailors**

- 'At the far hand': in 1743 a stranger paid £12 sterling to enter where a freeman's son paid 21 pounds Scots — and from 1734 a son could enter 'albeit he be neither a working tailor nor cappable of making an essay'.
- The mortcloths were hired to 32 parishes from Airdrie to Strathblane — 235 hires in 1731 alone — income from grief paid out again as pensions.
- In 1743–44 charity (£484) consumed most of a £616 income: monthly pensioners, supplicants at every court, 'verbal orders' for anyone who came to beg.
- Term limits of 1719: one year as Deacon, then three out; no man both Deacon and Collector (1710); a fine for merely accusing a Deacon of pocketing a fine (1717).
- The Officer was clothed by the trade — accounts buy him shirts, German serge breeches, 'Hat, Wig, Shoes & Stockings as usual' — and carried the Box and candles to every meeting.
- 1705: Benjamin McIntyre mocked Deacon Robert Hogg as 'the dearest hog in the Mercat' and lost his vote for seven years, pardoned only after publicly craving the Deacon's pardon.
- 1760: ordered to receive a mail packet on a Sunday, the craft minuted its 'detestation and abhorrence of the above profanation of the Lord's Day'.
- _Pitfalls:_ The 1924 blazon and motto could not be recovered; the tent-between-two-robes device is the London Merchant Taylors', tagged extrapolated in the dossier. Use St Anne, shears, the key — never a fabricated shield. · The 22 May 1546 four-craft mutual-defence band is unverified (search excerpt only). · 'Whip the cat' (the travelling tailor) is general Scots usage, not the Glasgow minutes. · Edinburgh's Tailors' Hall and the National Covenant (1638) are kin lore, not Glasgow. · Women in this craft are a documented exclusion (mantuamakers tolerated for dues, forbidden to train other women; a 1744 fine on teaching the trade to women). It belongs to the Shadow, handled plainly, not as colour. · Hammermen–Tailors cos 0.77: this is the Perfection pole, and its truth is fit — to the body and to the time (essays changed in 1713 because fashion moved). Keep the forge's truth-of-the-thing out of Tailors scenes.

**cordiners**

- The Goudie: an office peculiar to the Glasgow Cordiners, most junior of the Master Court, wielder of the Goudie Box at entertainments; the Deacon's Goudie's legendary duty was 'to ensure that his Deacon returned home safely'.
- The Deacon wears the chain at every formal occasion but one: when dancing, a special Dancing Medal is worn instead.
- 1657: the shoemakers complained the barkers and tanners were starving them of leather and outvoting them; the ruling forced the masters to be 'half shoemakers and half Barkers and Tanners' forever after.
- The craft's first historian claimed recognition from 27 June 1460; the petitioners' names are identical to the second charter of 27 June 1569 — same date, same names — so the 1460 claim is treated as a copying error.
- The oak charter box of 1708 ('Queen Anne oak, two locks, brass plate'), exhibited at the 1938 Empire Exhibition, was 'mislaid' and never found; the Deacon's chain was stolen in the early 1990s; today's is the third.
- 1589/90: cordiner Johnne Neill was made Burgess free of charge on condition he supply 'six good and sufficient footballs for the town', or pay 20 shillings.
- Cobblers worked only 'old leather' and were forbidden by law to make new shoes; the dignity line ran through the craft's own trade.
- _Pitfalls:_ The motto 'God is our Hope' rests on the closing line of the craft's 2022 history only — no independent source spells the blazon or motto; treat as craft-reported. · No description of the specific Glasgow cordiner essay-piece survives; do not invent which shoe. · The King Crispin processions were run by journeymen's St Crispin societies (500 Glasgow journeymen 'distinguished by the name of St Crispin's Society', 1779–80), adjacent to but not the Incorporation. Do not have the Master Court crown the king. · Wallace's armour is a tartanry magnet; the dossier says 'reputed' armour, and the source is an 1821 newspaper recalling 1790. Keep 'reputed'. · Deloney's The Gentle Craft (1597) is English chapbook literature; the proverb is kin lore, Scotland-wide, not a Glasgow minute. · Royal Charter is given as 1918 and 1919 in the same history; the craft and the House say 1919. · Cathedral pew-ends (1957), Body and Sole (2018) and the 2004 Supplementary Charter are outside the 1200–1850 window.

**maltmen**

- The chief is the Visitor, never a Deacon: the title was imposed in 1555 when Marie de Guise's regency suppressed elected deaconships; every other craft took 'Deacon' back, the Maltmen never did.
- There was no essay-piece. The entrant swore 'to mak Malt weill and skilfullie' with a named cautioner bound for him; the trial was perpetual — the Visitor's monthly 'visitin and sichtin the haill kills in toun'.
- 1621: a stranger 'not marieand ane Burges manis dochter, being ane maidin undeflorit' paid forty merks; marrying in halved the price. Far Hand and Near Hand survive on today's application forms.
- The last official 'visiting of the kilns' was 10 October 1815 by Visitor Robert Tennent, Junior — the brewing dynasty runs through the book.
- Nothing may be lent to a Master Court member 'in any shape or form whatever'; the Barrowfield Mortification (2,000 merks from John Orr, 1730) is the craft's only endowed fund.
- Whoever 'railes or miscalles ye Visitour … either be word or deid, privatelie or publiklie' pays ten pounds (1637).
- The dinner every new freeman owed (£6 13s 4d) was commuted in 1642 to ten merks into the Box 'for the common good' — 'Ressaivit for his denner and mortclaith' sit side by side in the accounts.
- _Pitfalls:_ Lore-judge correction (brief §6): the Maltmen never renamed themselves. The craft's name did not change; 'Visitor' was imposed on it and kept; 'Oversman' was a one-year dodge for the chief in 1670–71. The synthesis line 'twice quietly renamed their chief' overstates — the dossier documents only the Oversman episode; nothing is recorded for a rename in 1567. · No motto exists in any source; the 1851 medal inscription ('Established in remote antiquity. Sanctioned by the Letter of Guildry … Ratified by Parliament, 1672') serves. No patron saint is recorded; pre-1601 records burned. Lean on the absence — the Box, the book, the oath. · The correct 1605 designation was 'Maltmen and Mealmen'; the craft 'kept by the Malt and threw aside the Meal'. The Skippers merger and first Deacon Convener Duncan Semphill are a Trades House claim. · This is an ale-and-malt world, not a whisky world; the licensed-trade/Best Bar identity and the 1874 Bengal refusal are post-1850. The world-lexicon keys on barley, malt, ale, beer, brew, cheer, cellar. · Bakers–Maltmen is the most dangerous pair in the fleet. Give the Maltmen the shared table, the levee, the graveside — the hearth — never the mill queue or the scale. · The 'only Incorporation admitting no women' statement is a modern museum line; it is not colour and not for copy.

**weavers**

- 'Overmeikle wark': the deacon may take surplus work from an overloaded loom and deal it out among 'honest men' of the craft, fee and all (1599).
- Deacon for one year only, 'for avoiding of all superioritie and tyrannie' (1595, re-enacted 1611 and 1618); whoever canvassed for votes would never be a master again and would 'mak his publict repentatioun on the pillar in the kirk as ane persoune seditious' (1603).
- Suburb treaties instead of wars: Gorbals 1605 'for the speciall luife favour and kyndnes' borne to Provost Elphinstoune; Calton 1725 'for mantaining mutual love and good neighbourhood', with their own oversman, essays judged jointly, fines split between two poor funds.
- Coming 'mid gait, or half gait' to a brother's burial counted as absent (1611).
- The Collector's Box of 1763: 'The Weavers Art it is renowned so that rich nor poor without it cannot go'.
- A new deacon paid for his own 'heidwasching' (inaugural carousal) 'furt of his awin purs' (1616); in 1701 the freemen doubled their own dues for seven years because the poor were 'like to starve for want of bread'.
- The motto deliberately reverses London's: Glasgow weaves Trust with Truth — truth is the warp.
- _Pitfalls:_ The patron is genuinely blank. St Severus/Serf is a Dundee extrapolation; never name a saint. The blank is the canonical fact. · The 1528 Seal of Cause does not survive; every '1528' claim rests on the 1681 recital. The blazon is read from the crest image, not the Lyon Register. Minute books 1683–1793 are lost. · Purlie Wilson, Baird and Hardie (1820), the Calton dead (1787) and the Sma' Shot are the wider weaving world — journeymen, suburbs, Paisley — not the Incorporation. The masters were inside the walls when the muskets levelled; that is the Shadow, and it must not be borrowed as the craft's own martyrdom. · The Newfoundland-dog power loom (1773) is contested by the Kinloch hand-crank claim (1793); if used, keep it as the tale it is. · Tartanry: plaiding is striped homespun, not clan tartan; the weaver-poet is a 19th-century romance. The world-lexicon keys on loom, thread, warp, weft, pattern, draft. · Masons–Weavers cos 0.79, the closest pair in the fleet. The Weavers are the Steady pole, not the Living pole: write the web that renews through other hands (transmission), never the stone that outlasts, and never confuse patience with permanence.

**bakers**

- Mill-day: twenty-six shares, each carrying a day's grinding in turn; in 1790 Deacon Galloway and the miller were fined 25s each for grinding 100 bolls out of turn.
- The essay was standardised in 1783 as a batch of loaves 'to prevent partiality'; before that one intake was examined on beef-steak, veal, pigeon, pork, mutton and rabbit pies, apple, plum and pear tarts, frouches, custards and soufflés. Essays ended 16 August 1872.
- The oath de fideli: to 'not pack nor peel with unfreeman nor cover unfreeman's goods with his own', £20 sterling penalty; members bound to grind at the trade's own mills in rotation, drought excepted by the Deacon's leave.
- The 1719 Deacon's Box is painted 'Alma Ceres terrae cunctis alimenta ministrat' — the goddess of grain over a Presbyterian box — and was once guarded by rival 'Boxmasters of the New Box' and 'of the Old Box'.
- The Act of 1731 punished not the man who baked light but the man who sold full-weight penny loaves too cheap, 'for the great prejudice of these members'.
- The Auld Horn's silver ribbons carry every Deacon's name since 1776; when it filled in 1869 a new horn took up 'the record … O' Deacons' names sin' syne'.
- The Old Deacons' Club (1815), 'the House of Lords of the Incorporation', gavel of Cathedral roof-oak; of its mysteries 'it would be unwise to speak, still less to write'. Apprentice headwashing was abolished in 1769.
- _Pitfalls:_ No Glasgow patron survives; St Obert is extrapolated from Perth, Edinburgh and Dundee. The Deacon's Chair oak from Culross (St Mungo's birthplace) is the one sacral relic, and it is 1905. · The original charter burned in 1652; the craft proved its rights by 'use and wont' for 150 years — write the litigation as memory-without-parchment, not as a charter-reading. · The anchors-and-water reading of the arms is interpretation; the patent glosses nothing. · The 1641 ban on buns is the council's statute, not the craft's; the 1730 act against strangers' gingerbread is the craft's own. · The 1766 whisky petition is about barm for bread; do not let it become a drink story — that is the Maltmen's world. · Bakers–Maltmen is the fleet's worst pair: the Bakers get the mill queue, the scale and the shared resource (bench-system), never the shared table. Bakers–Fleshers splits on A4: the Regent's order is the bold well-timed act; the Fleshers keep the calm sough. · 'Praise God for all' is shared with the London Bakers' Company; the 1886 mill fire, Bilsland's 230,000 loaves and the Ure dynasty are post-1850.

**skinners**

- Oldest charter in Glasgow (1516), seventh in precedence, four seats in the House to the Hammermen's six — a tension the craft has traded on for five centuries, in silence, while its motto hands even that glory upward.
- The newest member served as Officer, unpaid: warning members to meetings and funerals, carrying the Box and candles, attending the Deacon in 'banquets, processions, and the conveying of distinguished gentlemen in or out of town', executing poindings, searching skins at market. Gabriell Conyinghame served three separate spells 1655–71; James Younge some 27 years to 1736.
- 1516 fines were paid in light: a pound of wax candles for resetting another master's apprentice; half a pound of wax and forfeiture for 'false stuff'.
- By the 1857 Rules a member's son entered for £3; a stranger paid £50, had to be under fifty, and had to produce a medical certificate.
- Glovers gave the craft its name and almost none of its members — only two glovers admitted in the first half of the 17th century.
- The deacons of 1608 took office by uplifted hand, 'to ye glorie of God and to ye weale of ye craftis' — the motto's sentiment three centuries before the 1924 grant, petitioned for by an architect, a shipowner and a writer.
- The same St Christopher dedication was used by Edinburgh's skinners at St Giles from 1450–51: a Scotland-wide skinners' allegiance.
- _Pitfalls:_ This is the thinnest dossier in the fleet: Lumsden's definitive 1937 history is an unreadable image-only scan. The essay (a dressed skin or cut gloves) is extrapolated — never specify a Glasgow essay-piece as fact — and the process is a general manual, not a Glasgow record. · No Skinners-v-Cordiners demarcation case was found; the bark-pit frontier is trade-structure inference. Do not invent a leather feud. The Fleshers buyer-seller friction is likewise structural, not minuted. · One BHO abstract renders the 1516 altar as St Mungo; the fuller source printing the petition says St Christopher. Follow Christopher; know the discrepancy. St Bartholomew (flayed) is not Glasgow. · The Napoleonic battalion, £200 gifts (1799, 1854), the 1857 Rules, the 1924 arms and the Earl of Snowdon are outside or at the edge of the window. · Tartanry: fur-trapper and Hudson's Bay imagery is wrong — these are sheep and lamb skins on a Glasgow burn. · Coopers–Skinners cos 0.69, both Keepers: the Skinners guard persons at the hearth (widows, the Officer's service, the charter's 'cherite'); the Coopers guard contents at the bench. The world-lexicon for this craft keys on hide, leather, spoiled, discard, charter, oldest, charity, worth keeping — the instrument already leans on the institution as much as the yard; keep both in every home answer.

**wrights**

- 'A penny win ye' — on signing the roll the entrant shakes hands with every member of the Master Court, each saying 'I wish you a penny won': a blessing of honest earnings.
- 'Pendicle' (a member who never made his essay) is the craft's deadliest insult; after Robert Dickie's slander in 1673 the court banned the word on pain of £5 Scots.
- The Gold Box inside the Deacon's Box: 'the art of opening it is supposed to be a secret to all who have not been at an opening'; each Deacon deposits one memento a year, from Cathedral oak to a Riverside Museum ribbon.
- The 1792 essay spec: a window 'not less than two and one-half feet square… six lozens and beeded', a bound shutter four feet high, a knife box fifteen inches 'bevelled both ways', a footstool sixteen inches square, 'all to be made from rough wood in the Essay Room'.
- The Deacon's consort is the 'Queen of the Wrights' and wears the original 1936 badge; the Master Court bell (2017) 'once a year makes the journey to London'; families trace membership 'seven or eight generations' at the near hand.
- _Pitfalls:_ The 1057 Malcolm III charter is origin-myth, not archive fact; the craft's own 1900 history and Bryce 2025 begin the documented life far later. · No source names the Wrights' pre-Reformation altar saint; St Joseph is generic Christendom colour [extrapolated]; the 1924 arms carry St Kentigern, and the saint's face on the seal is a portrait of Deacon Samuel Stevenson. · The Langside flag 'is said to have been carried' — provenance unproven; do not stage it as fact. · 'Five pounds in lieu' of the essay is the modern rule; pendicle admission ended 1876 — do not put a fee-instead-of-essay in a seventeenth-century scene. · Never show a Wright judging stone or a Mason judging timber: the 1600 divorce was filed because 'the said masons could not judge upon our work, nor we upon theirs perfectly'. · Jaffray the Wright drew the first Trades Hall plans ('the one with a spire was preferred') but Adam's design won; do not have a Wright design the Hall. · Masons–Wrights sit at cos 0.71: Wright scenes must load Make (A5+), not another quality-vs-mercy trade (00-SYNTHESIS Part 3).

**coopers**

- The 1569 charter abolished the new member's 'dinner and banquet' and turned the money into the poor-fund for 'decayed brethren' — feast into fund, altar-wax into widow's coal.
- Ninth in precedence by the 1776 Decreet despite holding the eighth-oldest charter (1569 beats the Wrights' 1600 and the Maltmen's 1605).
- The deacon roll's only two blanks answer each other: 1645–46 'Not given (plague?)' and 2020–21 'No image due to COVID'.
- The Hammermen's 1939 relic-box inventory lists, as item 14, 'Facsimile of Patent of Arms of Incorporation of Coopers' — one craft keeps a copy of another's honours.
- In 1665 every cooper 'entered and to be entered' had to subscribe the act that no man buys rungs 'abrod aither in burghe or landwart but what sail be a commoune bargane to the haill tred' — one man's sharp dealing became every apprentice's signature.
- _Pitfalls:_ No verified Glasgow motto or blazon: 'Love as Brethren' is the London Company's, not Glasgow's; the 26 June 1924 matriculation is confirmed but the wording is unrecovered. · The essay is [extrapolated] — documented only for Aberdeen's coopers (salmon salt, firlot and peck, deacon-issued gauges). · The trussing-in/blackening is [comparative]: Speyside and trade-generic, never presented as Glasgow-Coopers fact. · In the burgh records 'couparis' can mean dealers (horse-couper); the 1622 Renfrew herring dispute is about fish-dealers, not barrel-makers. · Whisky cooperage is post-period; in the 1200–1850 register the cargo is herring, salmon, ale, tobacco hogsheads and sugar puncheons. · The 1798 figure — 84,782 herring barrels a year, mostly Clyde-shipped, feeding enslaved people on West Indian plantations, with no minute of protest — is documented and must not be prettified. · Coopers–Skinners (cos 0.69) is a census near-tie: Cooper scenes must guard contents at the bench, never persons at the hearth (00-SYNTHESIS 2.3 item 6).

**fleshers**

- 'Blawing of muttoun' — inflating a carcass with air to fake plumpness — the craft's signature fraud, banned by name 'vnder the pane of xls'.
- 'Jock Tamson's bairns': the 1870 'guid-ganging plea' in which operatives tried to keep school funds from pendicles and the Court of Session 'affirmed the unity of the Craft'.
- Widows were admitted to their husbands' market stalls from the first — 'their admission to trade was of grace, not of right' — which is why women's names stand on the earliest lists.
- Sons entered at 'half upsett', the ancestor of today's 'Near Hand' money; each freeman might take one apprentice in seven years, 'buikit' and paying £5 upset.
- 'Keep a calm sough' — the Clerk's phrase for the craft that 'carried on with its task while thrones shook. Its Minutes reveal no panic in times of crisis.'
- _Pitfalls:_ MEDIUM dossier — among the three thinnest in the fleet; one rich but partisan spine (the Tory-voiced 1935 Clerk's Note) and burgh records via Glasghu Facies. · No verified motto: 'Men must eat though Empires fall' is the 1935 Clerk's creed, not heraldry; Aberdeen's 'Virtute Vivo' and London's Psalm 8 are kin, not Glasgow. · No patron saint and no altar — born 1580, post-Reformation, policed by the sermon bell (the 'no altar because post-Reformation' inference is the dossier's). · No essay is recorded — an inference from silence; the flesher's test was the wholesomeness of what he sold, enforced by inspection. · The blazon is read from the Grand Hall carving, not the Lyon Register; the deed of 1580 is lost (one council line survives) and early minute books were destroyed after Dominie Mitchell's excerpting. · They fight through courts (1802, the Lords, 1870), never riots — the synthesis flag keeps A4 Bold as market-quick bluntness, not swagger; do not write a riotous flesher. · Rangers FC's first match on Fleshers' Haugh (May 1872) is insider gold but outside the 1200–1850 register. · Fleshers–Bakers (cos 0.61) share the same Jacobite winter with opposite virtues — the steady scale vs the bold well-timed act; keep that seam.

**masons**

- Rapping the Shed closes every meeting as the working day once closed — three slow, seven rapid, three slow — to 'Stop, Stop, Stop / All the Masons in the Shed / Stop, Stop, Stop', prefaced 'By command of the Deacon and in accordance with ancient custom'.
- In 1999 the Master Court asked the Lord Lyon about 1057 and no record was found; members are now formally instructed to say 'legend states'. The Lodge of Glasgow St John swallowed rank '3 bis' (3½) rather than let the legend go, and the mark 'I / M / MLVII' has been cut into every stone project since 2015.
- The Besir ('the Deacon to Be, Sir') must supply a cake and sing the Besir Song — written in 1987 by a Past Deacon of the Wrights — then host the Besir's Party in the Steps Bar.
- The Queen of the Masons is ceremonially chained with the Deacon's own chain at the Choosing Lunch while he is de-chained, and closes that lunch by rapping the shed.
- The rigging-stone penny (1603): whoever hewed the roof-ridge stone paid twelvepence in the pound 'upon the aged, impotent, and decriped persons, freemen of the Craft' — topping-out made holy. New Master Court members are 'New Laid Stanes' and must wait 'a year and a day'.
- _Pitfalls:_ The 1057 charter surfaced c.1800 as 'an old musty paper' and its witness list includes an anachronistic 'Andrew Hamilton, Bishop of Glasgow' — the 1551 Provost's name; treat as legend, never as scene fact. · The Incorporation states plainly it 'is not part of Freemasonry': no aprons, all-seeing eyes or lodge gothic; the operative Lodge 3 bis link is real but is not the Incorporation. · St Thomas (the 1551 charter text) versus St Stephen (the 2016 booklet) altar discrepancy — the primary charter says St Thomas. · Naismith's bed rests on a Wikipedia-sourced legend; the 1579 story is 'semi-legendary' in the historians' view and told as fact only by the craft. · Kippen Jock (1826), the 'perished the pack' verdicts and 60s fines for failing to inform on a cowan (1612) are the shadow; do not write the Masons as gentle. · Masons–Weavers is the closest pair in the fleet (cos 0.79) and Masons–Wrights 0.71: a Mason scene must trade stone-that-outlasts against pattern-that-renews (A1), or endure/keep against make/join (A5) — never another quality-vs-mercy trade (00-SYNTHESIS Part 3).

**gardeners**

- Through the 1600s the head of the craft was styled 'visitor' as often as deacon — the man who goes round and looks — and the August judging walk of Let Glasgow Flourish (founded 1983) is the same visitation four centuries on.
- The motto 'Gardening — the First of Arts' is an answer to a specific insult: article 23 of the Letter of Guildry (1605) forbids a guild brother to 'tapp… pears, apples… onions, kail… and such like small things, which is not agreeable to the honour of the calling of a gild brother'.
- The 1924 blazon says 'the moon in her increment' — waxing — the only increasing thing on any of the fourteen shields; London's gardeners chose the same Eden and wrote the curse ('In the sweat of thy brows') under it.
- Near hand £75 and far hand £125 are still on the price list, 'still exactly two-fifths cheaper'; in the same 18th-century ledger a duke's gardener paid £60 Scots and 'Gilbert Wilson (entry money £3 scots, quarterly, as being very poor, old and infirm)'.
- 'Stallenger… till he be admitted Burgess' — a man licensed to a market stall until he could afford to belong; and until 22 January 1745 the craft submitted 'a leet of two' and the Town Council picked its deacon.
- _Pitfalls:_ The pruning knife belongs to the Order of Free Gardeners (Haddington 1676; Adam/Noah/Solomon degrees; compass, square and pruning knife), NOT the Incorporation — the lore judge already caught this error once. · Never conflate the Incorporation with the Free Gardeners, even though both used Adam in the same city for seventy years. · No patron saint and probably never one; St Fiacre (spade, basket, 30 Aug/1 Sep) is European colour only — 'not to be attributed to Glasgow'. · Do not map the Foull Moor; do not settle 1646 vs 1649; do not name the plague-dead Deacon (the Woddrow identification is inference). · The Gardeners took essays ('John Muir, Weaver… produced his essay', 1745) but nobody knows what a gardener's essay was — 'still do not invent one'. · The Necropolis is Merchants House ground (George Mylne), recorded as a negative finding; no Incorporation involvement. · The 'kailyard' here is a larder, not the sentimental fiction school; and the narrative pp. 1–178 of the 1903 history remain unread — the craft's own account of charter, lands and yards is missing. · Axis: Gardeners are the Living pole with A5 now −1 (Keeper); the Gardeners–Barbers seam (cos ≈ 0.55) needs slow-tended permanence against renewal-of-persons.

**barbers**

- The 1656 Seal of Cause contains a clerical error dating the royal patent to 'ane thousand five hundred ffyftie-nyne' (1559) — a year when James VI had not yet been born.
- The skeleton clause of 1708: when the joint stock was divided the surgeons kept 'the Bookes, Skeletone and all rarities now in the hall', and the Barbers formally renounced all claim to the skeleton, forever.
- The essay was two wigs from set pairings — 'A Grizel cut Bob wig and a Queue wig… A Bag wig and a Scratch wig… A Campaign wig and a Spencer wig… A Foxtail wig and a Drop lock wig'; the last essay was made in 1864.
- The painted motto in the Hall — 'This elegant art flourished in the days of Joseph, son of Jacob the patriarch, governor of Egypt' — claims descent from the man who had to be shaved before he could stand before Pharaoh; the arms say 'In the Presence of God'.
- Ranked in 1724 'immediatly before the Bonnetmakers To witt between the Gardeners and Bonnetmakers… in all time comeing'; a Gorbals barber selling a wig in Glasgow 'shall have liberty to Shave the Buyers head and face if required' (1732); pensioners still receive shortbread.
- _Pitfalls:_ Two mottoes must be kept distinct: 'In the Presence of God' (arms) and the Latin Joseph line (the old oil painting); do not merge them. · No patron saint — post-Reformation founding; Cosmas and Damian are British barber-surgeon colour, not Glasgow record. · The original 1599 Royal Charter was never found in the chest (1835); only an 1804 notarised copy survives. · Tennent's 1899 Records are openly partisan against the Surgeons ('threw up the sponge', 'self-excision' are his voice). · Not Sweeney Todd: the craft's whole meaning is being safe to touch — razor at the neck, lancet at the vein — and its shadow is the gossip's treason, not the murderer's. · The ledger enrols Black journeymen ('Sandy Barber' and 'Glasgow', 1741) and 'a negro boy belonging to Matthew Orr' (1769) without comment, then petitions against the slave trade (1792): do not sanitise either fact. · Sabbath hypocrisy is documented (masters fining apprentices ten pounds while pocketing gentlemen's Sunday fees) — use it, do not soften it. · Barbers sit at three census boundaries (Skinners, Maltmen, Gardeners); their scenes must pull decisively toward Living-renewal-of-persons (00-SYNTHESIS 2.4).

**dyers**

- Every new member takes the oath and is presented with a blue bonnet 'with a diced border in red and white and crowned with a bright red tuft or toorie', then introduced to the Master Court wearing it — bought from the Bonnet Fund (1935), and pointedly Stewarton bonnets for Glasgow members.
- Two mottoes: 'Give Glory to God' (the official one) and 'Concordia Corroborat' carved beneath clasped hands on the Charter Chest.
- Kilmarnock bonnet or Stewarton bonnet? Official craft advice to Field Marshal Montgomery in 1947: call it 'a Scotch Bonnet'. The Duke of Gloucester in 1925: 'I am somewhat relieved to find that you do not expect me to knit or even dye one.'
- On entry each craftsman declared the indelible mark by which his bonnets could be known — 'twa blakis in the ye lip of ye neck for his mark'; forty shillings 'toties quoties' for repeating anything said in court.
- The 1877–78 Deacon's medal reads 'The Incorporation of Dyers and Bonnetmakers' — reversed — and the Town Clerk had to be shown the 1597 parchment before accepting the true name. A recent Deacon's blessing to new members: the hope that neither they nor their dependants 'would ever receive one single penny from the Incorporation'.
- _Pitfalls:_ The blue bonnet is the fleet's largest tartanry trap; the antidote is in the record — a rain-proof felted crown for ploughmen, 'the emblem of liberty', not Highland dress. The '45 requisition and the white cockade are documented; Brigadoon is not. · Kin lore is never Glasgow fact: Stewarton 'sichters', wet caps, forfeited-and-burned bonnets, Dundee's Lockit Book, 'idlesetts' — all labelled Ayrshire/Dundee in the dossier. · The formal blazon text is unpublished; 'the dicing recalls' the Stewart fesse-checky is the craft's own legend (Deacon Ogg, 1934), not heraldic fact. · No patron saint; chartered 1597, post-Reformation; courts met in kirkyards, not at an altar. · Turkey red and Sir Archibald Orr Ewing (1871) are Victorian — beyond the 1850 register. · Helen Wylie worked the craft in 1598 but no woman was a member until 2003 (first woman Deacon 2010) — do not invent women members in period. · Never call them 'the Dyers' alone; the craft's own shadow is 'survival by dilution' — a craft of bonnetmakers containing no bonnetmakers. · The Bold pole (A4 +3) is 'defiant endurance, not swagger' — the lone 1809 stand, the 1816 'Spartan severity', the anthem's cocked bonnet; write nerve, not bravado (00-SYNTHESIS 2.3 flag 4).
