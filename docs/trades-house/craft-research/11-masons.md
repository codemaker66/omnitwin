# Incorporation of Masons

> Craft #11 of the 14 Incorporations of the Trades House of Glasgow. Researched 2026-08-05 for the craft-sorting quiz programme. Every claim is cited inline; anything inferred from general Scottish guild practice rather than Glasgow masons' evidence is tagged **[extrapolated]**.

## Essence (the craft's soul in one paragraph)

The Masons are the craft that measures truth in stone. Everything about them runs on proof: the apprentice proves seven years, the candidate proves his "essay" before three of the "perfiteast men of craft," the Saturday inspection proves every man's wall, and when Glasgow doubted Mungo Naismith's flat arch he proved it by sleeping under it. They believe they are the oldest thing in Glasgow that is not the Cathedral itself — and they believe that partly because they built the Cathedral, and partly because they cherish a royal charter of 1057 that the Lord Lyon cannot find any record of. That double nature is the whole craft: patient, exact, charitable to its own aged and broken, jealous of its standards to the point of cruelty toward the unfree "cowan" — and so in love with permanence that it would rather carve a legend into its mark (MLVII) than let the story die. A mason closes every gathering the way a foreman once closed the working day: three slow raps, seven quick, three slow. Stop. Stop. Stop. The stone will still be there tomorrow.

## History

- **The legend of 1057.** The Incorporation's own tradition holds that Malcolm III ("Canmore"), in the year he took the throne from Macbeth, granted Glasgow's operative masons a royal charter — dated at "our Court at Fordie, the 5th day of October, 1057" — to protect the city from "unskilled and insufficient workmen" at work on the Cathedral, and to give the masons a perpetual lodge ([Cruikshank, *Sketch of the Incorporation of Masons*, 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862); [Trades House — Masons](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-masons/)). The document surfaced only at the beginning of the 19th century as "an old musty paper" in the charter-chest of the St John's Lodge (Crawford, *Sketch of the Trades House*, as cited in [Bryce, *Customs and Traditions*, 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)). In 1999 the Master Court asked the Lord Lyon's Court, which could find no record of it; the Incorporation now formally instructs members to say "legend states" when citing 1057 ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)). The charter's witness list even includes an anachronistic "Andrew Hamilton, Bishop of Glasgow" — the same name as the 1551 Provost ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
- **Documented founding: the Seal of Cause, 14 October 1551.** Provost Andrew Hamilton of Ceuchtnock and bailies John Muire and John Hall granted a Seal of Cause to "ye heid men and mesters of ye masounes, [wrights,] cowpers, sclaters, sawars, and quarrours" of Glasgow, because "leile and trew" craftsmen "of perfite cunyng" were being "put to pouyrtie" by incomers "warkand insufficient wark" ([Cruikshank 1879, full transcript](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862); date confirmed at [Trades House Digital Library](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/incorporations.html)). The 1551 statutes required: burgess-ship and examination by "thre of yer perfiteast men of craft"; a 20-shilling upset to St Thomas's altar in the Cathedral; 10 shillings per apprentice entry; seven-year minimum apprenticeships; one apprentice at a time; and a weekly inspection every Saturday evening of "all menis wark."
- **The mother-craft era.** The 1551 charter made the Masons the head of a whole federation of building trades. The Coopers hived off in 1569 and the Wrights obtained separate letters of deaconry in 1600 — the Wrights' own application declaring that until then they "and most of the other crafts in Glasgow" had been "under the jurisdiction and authority of the masonic incorporation and lodge" ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862); dates at [Trades House Digital Library](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/incorporations.html)). Quarriers remained brethren "bearing burden with the Masons" (minute of 11 Dec 1600).
- **1605, Letter of Guildry.** The Masons became one of the 14 Incorporations under the Trades House; they elect four members to the House and by old right send two "lyners" to the Dean of Guild Court, the body that once passed every building plan in Glasgow ([Trades House — Masons](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-masons/); [Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)). In the House's customary listing of the crafts the Masons stand eleventh of fourteen ([Trades House crafts listing](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-masons/)).
- **Records.** The oldest surviving minute book (the Sederunt Book) runs 10 September 1600 to 5 July 1681 — a near-complete series that few of the other Incorporations can match, since the Trades House's and Town Council's own early-1600s books are lost ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)). The Incorporation's records are catalogued by [The National Archives (F150989)](https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/c/F150989), and its membership roll is published from 1574 ([Bryce, *Members 1574–2013*](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/final_book.pdf)).
- **Early meetings** were held in the "Hie Kirk" (Glasgow Cathedral) itself, in St Enoch's kirkyard, the Blackfriars kirk and the Trades' Hospital — the craft literally convened inside the buildings its forebears raised ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
- **1846–47: the fall of privilege.** The Act abolishing exclusive burgh trading privileges ended the Masons' legal monopoly; the craft converted itself into the charitable, educational and convivial body it remains ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862); [Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
- **Arms matriculated 19 February 1917** by Lord Lyon Sir James Balfour Paul ([Patent of Arms excerpt](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/excert_from_laws_of_masons_001.pdf); [Trades House Digital Library](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/incorporations.html)).
- **Today** the Incorporation supports stonemasonry and allied trades (bricklaying, plastering, tiling, plumbing, roof slating), funds bursaries and competitions, pays quarterly pensions to beneficiaries, and counts King Charles III among its members. It states plainly that it "is not part of Freemasonry" ([Incorporation of Masons of Glasgow](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/); [Trades House — Masons](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-masons/)).

## Legends & Episodes

- **The crafts save the Cathedral (1579).** As told in the History of the Trades' House and repeated by Cruikshank: when Principal Andrew Melville of the College "assembled the people by tuck of drum to pull down the Cathedral," the Crafts ran to arms and warned that "if any person presumed to pull down a single stone of the church, he should that moment be buried under it." Summoned before the Council in Edinburgh, the leaders were vindicated by the thirteen-year-old James VI: "too many churches had been already destroyed" ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)). Glasgow Cathedral is the only mainland medieval cathedral in Scotland to survive the Reformation roofed and whole — the crafts' proudest corporate memory, whatever embellishment the telling has gathered. *(Historians treat the details as semi-legendary; the Incorporation tells it as its own.)*
- **Mungo Naismith sleeps under his arch (c. 1756).** The Incorporation's freeman Mungo Naismith, master mason of St Andrew's in the Square (built 1739–1756 to Allan Dreghorn's design), raised over its portico the first flat arch of its kind in Scotland. When the centering came down the town swore it would fall — so Naismith had a bed set up between the columns and slept the night beneath it, watched by a crowd ([St Andrew's in the Square — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Andrew%27s_in_the_Square); Naismith and his grandson appear on the Incorporation's rolls in [Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
- **The pear eating that bought a deaconship.** Craft legend: a prospective Deacon with a house and orchard — "possibly on the banks of the Molendinar Burn" — feasted the voting members on pears and drink the night before the election, handing each a few pears and a sprig of white heather: wear the heather tomorrow if I have your vote. He could count his majority across the hall before a hand was raised. He won — and the Masons have held a Pear Eating on the eve of every Deacon's Choosing since ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf); [Pear Eating 2014 report](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/pear-eating-18th-september-2014.html)).
- **The forged(?) charter and the fight for "Number One of the West" (1849–1850).** Armed with the 1057 charter, the operative Lodge of Glasgow St John petitioned the Grand Lodge of Scotland for precedence "next to the Grand Lodge" in the west. Grand Lodge offered placement as **No. 3 bis** ("3½") — pointedly "without admitting the antiquity of the charter of 1057." The lodge swallowed it, and the Incorporation's Deacon is to this day invited to be installed Depute Master of Lodge 3 bis ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862); [Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
- **The Tolbooth ground stone (15 March 1626).** The Town Council minuted the laying of the Tolbooth's foundation stone; every mason named on the job matches the Incorporation's entry books — the whole civic skyline of old Glasgow was Incorporation work ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
- **"Because we cannot writt."** On the 1628 national charter to Sir William St Clair of Rosslyn, "The Ludge of Glasgow — John Boyd, deakin; Rob. Boyd, ane of the mestres" signed alongside masters of Edinburgh, Stirling and Dundee, several with "my hand att the pen, led by the notar... because I cannot writt" — men who could raise a cathedral vault but not sign their names ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
- **The deacon who walked himself to death (1828).** Thomas Lang, elected Deacon while sick, rose from his bed to walk with the Grand Lodge at the laying of the foundation stone of Hutchesons' Bridge, "after which he went home to his bed, and died soon after" ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
- **Famous members.** David Hamilton, "the father of Glasgow architecture" (Hutchesons' Hospital, the Royal Exchange), appears on the 1824 roll as "David Hamilton, architect" and served as Deacon in 1808 ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)). Sir John Train MP was Deacon in 1923 and Deacon Convener 1927–28; his dress sword is still Incorporation regalia ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)). HRH Prince George joined in 1928 ([Trades House Digital Library](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/incorporations.html)), and the present King is a member ([Trades House — Masons](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-masons/)).
- **The wind that broke Kippen Jock (1826).** Builder John Taylor ("Kippen Jock") had a four-storey tenement in Portland Street ready for its roof when a storm brought much of it down; he never recovered financially and ended his days labouring for other men — the craft's own cautionary tale about pride, weather and margins ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).

## Patron, Arms & Motto

- **Arms (matriculated 19 Feb 1917):** *Azure, on a chevron between two cotises and accompanied by three towers, two in chief and one in base, all Argent, the last masoned Sable, a pair of compasses proper* ([Patent of Arms, Lyon Register, 19 February 1917](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/excert_from_laws_of_masons_001.pdf)). Three silver towers on blue, the chevron (itself a builder's truss-form), and the open compasses — the universal mark of the measuring, designing mason. The pattern descends from the medieval London Masons' arms of 1472/3 (chevron, compasses, three castles) ([Worshipful Company of Masons arms](https://www.heraldry-wiki.com/heraldrywiki/index.php/Worshipful_Company_of_Masons)).
- **Motto:** **"IN THE LORD IS ALL OUR TRUST"** — in the escrol beneath the shield ([Patent of Arms 1917](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/excert_from_laws_of_masons_001.pdf)). It is English, not Latin — the old vernacular prayer of the operative mason lodges (the same line closes early masonic invocations) **[extrapolated: the line's wider operative-lodge pedigree is general masonic history, not a Glasgow-specific record]**. For men who worked under falling stone and on high scaffolds, trust in the Lord was not decorative.
- **Patron altar: St Thomas.** The 1551 Seal of Cause directs every upset and fine to "Sanct Thomas's altar" in Glasgow Cathedral, "for ye upholding of devigne service... and of ye vestments and ornaments yairof," with a pound of wax as the penalty of disobedience ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)). Glasgow Cathedral held relics of St Thomas of Canterbury alongside St Kentigern's ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)), while masons across Christendom honoured St Thomas the Apostle, the legendary builder of a heavenly palace — which Thomas the altar honoured is not stated in the charter. *(Note: the Incorporation's own 2016 booklet says the weekly penny upheld "the Altar of St. Stephen" — a discrepancy with the 1551 text; the primary charter says St Thomas ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf) vs [Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).)* The associated lodge took **St John** as patron (Lodge of Glasgow St John).
- **The Incorporation's Mark (adopted 2015):** a capital **I** above a capital **M** above the Roman numerals **MLVII** (1057), now cut into every stone project the craft undertakes — first incised on Roddy McDowall's one-and-a-half-life-size sculpture of St Mungo, carved by a sculptor the Incorporation had funded through his training ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)). A legend the Lord Lyon cannot verify, chiselled permanently into stone: the craft in one glyph.

## Rituals & Customs

- **Entry (17th c.):** the candidate "has given his **essay**, made his **oath of fidelity** to the Craft, paid his **upset** or freedom fine, and the expenses of the **banquet** to the Craft" (standard minute form; the 1620 act forbade giving the banquet *before* the essay was judged — candidates had been softening their examiners with dinner first) ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)). Essays were still being "wrought in the Deacon's yard" by candidates in the 1840s ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
- **Discipline (1551):** breaking the rules cost a pound of wax to St Thomas's altar; disobeying the Deacon added punishment by the magistrates "with all rigour," exclusion from work until a fresh upset was paid, and penance — the offender "shall pass to his curate at the Greyfriars and recounsell his oath and faith" ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
- **The Saturday visitation:** every Saturday evening, "two or thre of ye worthyest and maist perfite craftsmen," with the Deacon or an officer, would "pass and sersh and vesy all menis wark gif it be sufficient and gude" — faulty work "correkit and forbiddyn" ([1551 Seal of Cause, in Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
- **Rapping the Shed.** Every Masons' meeting still closes as the working day once did, the senior Deacon present striking the table in the old foreman's rhythm — three slow, seven rapid, three slow — to the words "Stop, Stop, Stop / All the Masons in the Shed / Stop, Stop, Stop," prefaced: "By command of the Deacon and in accordance with ancient custom, I close the meeting thus" ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf); [Trades House Digital Library video listing](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/incorporations.html)).
- **The Pear Eating** (September, eve of Choosing Day): the last event of the whole Trades House year — accounts approved, then "rustic fare" (pies, sausage rolls), songs and recitations in the Saloon; every guest leaves with a small basket of pears and a sprig of white heather; a senior Deacon recites the origin legend aloud so the young learn it ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
- **The Besir.** On Pear Eating night the Deacon-to-be ("the Deacon to Be, Sir") supplies a cake and must sing the Besir Song (written 1987 by John Laird, Past Deacon of the Wrights — verses running from Charles II in 1683 to Robert Adam fighting the building regulations over the Trades Hall staircase in 1793), then hosts the Besir's Party in the Steps Bar ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
- **Deacon's Choosing** (September): meeting in the Grand Hall with the regalia laid out — the Deacon's Bell (1789), the Mell (mallet, 1912), the Trowel, the Staff, the Box Keys, the chains — followed by the Choosing Dinner where "The Auld Hoose" is sung and student prize-winners are toasted ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
- **Queen of the Masons.** The Deacon's partner is styled "Queen of the Masons," wears the Queen's Jewel, and at the Choosing Lunch is ceremonially chained with the Deacon's own chain of office while the Deacon is de-chained; the Queen closes that lunch by rapping the shed ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
- **The Box Opening.** The craft's oak Box of 1684 needs two keys, held by the Late Deacon and Late Collector so no one man can rob the craft (rule of 8 Jan 1658: never opened without the Deacon, his four Masters and two brethren present). Once a year, at a black-tie ceremony, the key-keepers open it "with feigned difficulty" and the Deacon deposits mementoes of his year ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf); [Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
- **New Laid Stanes:** new Master Court members, who must stand and speak at the Pear Eating. A member may only join the Court after "a year and a day" — an ancient rule against packing the electorate ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
- **Pension Paying Luncheons** (quarterly, the Western Club): the Collector announces "the pensions have been paid" to the craft's beneficiaries and the Past Deacons cheer; the incoming Collector attends the fourth as the jokingly-ignored "Stranger in the Midst" ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
- **Calendar:** Candlemas Court (February, with social evening and swearing-in of members), Lammas Court (August, elections), Choosing Day and Pear Eating (September) — the old Scottish quarter-days still governing the craft's year ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
- **Mortcloths:** the Incorporation lent its funeral palls free to contributors; others paid 12s for the "meikle clayt" and less for the "lyttll claithe" (1620) ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
- **Rigging-stone charity (1603):** whoever hewed a rigging stone (roof-ridge stone — the last stone of a house) paid twelvepence in the pound "to be bestowed upon the aged, impotent, and decriped persons, freemen of the Craft" — completion of a roof triggering alms, the topping-out made holy ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).

## Sensory World

- **Stone:** blond Glasgow sandstone — "very fine white rock" from the Buchanan Street quarries, "quite a contrast to the coarse Cowcaddens rock"; quarries worked by four-man crane companies, five cranes to a pit, paid by the piece ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)). Freestone dust, wet lime, the sour smell of slaked mortar. **[extrapolated: smells inferred from the materials named in the records]**
- **The shed:** the timber lodge at the building site where stones were cut in the dry; the day ended when the foreman rapped on it with "a rule, metal tool or even a piece of masonry" ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
- **Tools:** mell and chisel, compasses, square and rule, trowel, plumb — the compasses and towers on the arms; the Deacon's ceremonial Bell (1789), Mell and Trowel still laid out at every Choosing ([Patent of Arms 1917](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/excert_from_laws_of_masons_001.pdf); [Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
- **Sounds:** the 3–7–3 rap; iron on stone; the creak of quarry cranes; the "tuck of drum" that once threatened the Cathedral; a pianist and "The Auld Hoose" at the Choosing Dinner ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862); [Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
- **Marks:** each mason's private mark cut into every finished bed of stone — still legible on Glasgow Cathedral, sheltered behind shafts and lintels where the weather cannot erase them ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
- **Taste:** pears and white heather in September; pies and sausage rolls; the Besir's cake; a "small libation" in the Steps Bar; the entry banquet whose cost was minuted in 1668 — of £33 paid, £14 banked and "the rest being allowed to be spent, as use is" ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf); [Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
- **The body:** seven years' apprenticeship plus "two for meat and fee"; taskers who could "put a great quantity of work through their hands"; brothers who all died before 35; a deacon who walked one procession too many ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).

## Values

1. **Proof over promise.** No one enters on reputation: examination by "thre of yer perfiteast men" (1551), the essay before the banquet (1620), the Saturday inspection of all work, Naismith sleeping under his own arch. The craft's deepest reflex is *show me* ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
2. **Permanence.** They build for centuries and think in them: the 1057 legend, the 1684 Box still in use, records unbroken from 1600, the MLVII mark cut into new stone in 2015, a self-image of "approaching the end of their first millennium" ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf); [Trades House Digital Library](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/masons.html)).
3. **Guardianship of standards.** The 1551 charter exists *because* "insufficient wark" was ruining true craftsmen; cowans were barred from hewing so much as a window (1600); the master answered personally for "ye fynenes of ye work" ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
4. **Rank earned by service.** The fixed ladder — apprentice, journeyman, freeman, New Laid Stane, Collector, Late Collector, Deacon — cannot be skipped or bought (the year-and-a-day rule exists precisely to stop vote-packing); even the Deacon "must abide by the ruling of the Master Court" ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
5. **Care for the fallen.** Rigging-stone pennies for the "aged, impotent, and decriped" (1603), mortcloths for burials, twice-yearly grocery visits to pensioners, and the quarterly cheer when "the pensions have been paid" ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862); [Trades House — Masons](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-masons/); [Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
6. **Memory as duty.** The 2016 customs book exists because Past Deacons feared traditions "were not being passed on completely from Deacon to Deacon"; the pear-eating legend is recited annually on purpose; the Box receives its deposit every year ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
7. **Reverence — work done under God.** Fees to St Thomas's altar, wax fines, penance at Greyfriars, the motto "In the Lord is all our trust," and arms taken up to defend the Cathedral itself ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862); [Patent of Arms 1917](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/excert_from_laws_of_masons_001.pdf)).

## Shadow

Every mason virtue has a dark twin, and the records show them all. *Guardianship of standards* curdles into **monopoly and cruelty**: the cowan — the unfree builder, often just a poorer man — barred under £10 penalty from hewing a door; freemen fined 60 shillings merely for *failing to inform* on an unfreeman (1612); entry fines racked to £66 13s 4d for outsiders while freemen's sons paid £6 (1654 vs 1636) — a wall of privilege dressed as quality control, held until Parliament demolished it in 1846 ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862); Cruikshank himself jokes that Adam Smith "would have found as little favour" with the old craft as with its descendants). *Permanence* curdles into **antiquity-vanity**: a craft so hungry to be oldest that it staked its public dignity on a musty paper with an impossible witness list, fought the Grand Lodge for "Number One of the West," and accepted the faintly absurd rank "3 bis" rather than let the legend go ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862); [Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)). *Proof over promise* curdles into **pitilessness toward failure** — Kippen Jock's storm, the asylum, the "perished the pack" verdicts passed on broken men in the craft's own biographies. And *reverence for stone* curdles into **violence**: the proudest story the Masons tell is a threat to bury a man alive under the Cathedral he meant to pull down.

## Rivals & Kin

- **The Wrights — the child that left home.** Bound together in the 1551 Seal of Cause under mason jurisdiction, the Wrights obtained their own deaconry in 1600, their application admitting they had been "under the jurisdiction and authority of the masonic incorporation and lodge" ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)). The rivalry mellowed into the closest kinship in the House: the two crafts jointly administer the Lady McDonald Fund, invite each other's Deacons to the Box Opening and Pear Eating, and a Wright wrote the Masons' own Besir Song ([Trades House — Masons](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-masons/); [Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
- **The Coopers** likewise sat under the 1551 charter before incorporating separately in 1569; Masons, Wrights and Coopers still jointly claim the Malcolm III foundation ([Trades House — Masons](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-masons/); [Trades House Digital Library](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/incorporations.html)).
- **Slaters, sawyers, quarriers** stayed within or under the Masons — quarriers formally warned to Masons' courts "as brethren... bearing burden with the Masons" (1600); a slater entered as freeman *qua* slater in 1659 ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)). The modern craft still gathers the allied trades: bricklayers, plasterers, tilers, slaters, plumbers ([Incorporation website](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/)).
- **The cowans — the eternal outsiders.** Unfree builders of dry-stone dykes, forbidden to "work, hew windows or doors, or sell stones" in the burgh (1600); in 1622 James Ritchie was hauled up merely for *feeing* a cowan ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)). The word passed from Glasgow's building sites into worldwide masonic vocabulary.
- **The Grand Lodge of Scotland and Mother Kilwinning** — rivals in antiquity, who never admitted the 1057 charter and left the Glasgow lodge at "3 bis" ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
- **The Merchants House / Dean of Guild Court** — the old constitutional counterweight: the Court was majority-merchant, with the Masons and Wrights each supplying two craft "lyners" to line out and judge buildings ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).
- **London kin:** standing reciprocal hospitality with the Worshipful Company of Masons and the Worshipful Company of Plaisterers of London, whose Masters are feasted at the Choosing Dinner and given free membership ([Bryce 2016](https://www.incorporationofmasonsofglasgow.org/uploads/1/7/2/0/17207928/memberst_traditions.pdf)).

## Lexicon

Period and craft words a Scots narrator could use (all attested in the cited records):

1. **Cowan** — an unfree, uninitiated builder; the mason's shadow-rival ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
2. **Essay** — the trial-piece of craft a candidate must work before admission (1620).
3. **Upset** — the entry fine paid on becoming a freeman.
4. **Far-hand / rear-hand** — entrants unrelated to freemen vs freemen's sons and sons-in-law (fee scales differed fourfold).
5. **Rap the shed** — signal the end of work; now, close the meeting (3–7–3).
6. **Besir** — the Deacon "to be, sir," on Pear Eating night.
7. **New Laid Stane** — a newly elected Master Court member.
8. **Rigging stane** — roof-ridge stone; hewing one triggered the charity penny (1603).
9. **Vesy** (visie) — to inspect work ("pass and sersh and vesy all menis wark," 1551).
10. **Cunning** — skill, know-how ("found of cunning or knowledge," 1057 legend text).
11. **Insufficient wark** — botched work, the founding grievance of 1551.
12. **Tasker** — a mason paid by the piece, not the day ([Cruikshank 1879](https://archive.org/details/cu31924030291862)).
13. **Mell / mall** — the mason's mallet; the Deacon's ceremonial one dates from 1912.
14. **Meikle clayt** — the "big cloth," the Incorporation's grand mortcloth (1620).
15. **Sederunt** — the sitting/attendance of a court; the Sederunt Book, 1600–1681.
16. **Quarter accounts** — members' quarterly dues; unpaid, "no vote... nor be heard" (1609).
17. **Lyner** — the craft's man on the Dean of Guild Court, who "lined" (approved the lines of) buildings.
18. **Deacon — and the Queen of the Masons** — head of craft, and the style of the Deacon's lady.
19. **Prenteis / booking** — apprentice; the clerk's formal enrolment of servants and apprentices.
20. **"Buried under it"** — the crafts' 1579 warning over the Cathedral; usable as a proverb of the craft's temper.

## Question Seeds

Moral dilemmas rooted in this craft's specific lore, for the sorting quiz. Each seed names the value it reveals.

1. **The musty paper.** In the charter-chest you find the parchment your craft's whole antiquity rests on — and you, who can read stone, can see it is too young. Announced, your craft becomes merely one guild among fourteen; kept silent, your brethren keep their millennium. The Lyon Court will not rule either way. Do you speak? — *Choosing to expose reveals **proof over promise**; choosing silence reveals **memory as duty** (the legend as load-bearing wall). Rooted in the 1057 charter, Crawford's account of its discovery, and the 1999 Lord Lyon inquiry.*
2. **The drum at the Cathedral door.** Melville's men are assembling with a drum to pull down the Hie Kirk your grandfathers built and your own courts still meet in. The magistrates counsel patience; your brethren are running for their mells. Do you stand in the door and say a man will be buried under the first stone he casts down? — *Taking arms reveals **reverence/guardianship of the built past above civil peace**; standing back with the magistrates reveals **rank and order**. Rooted in the 1579 episode.*
3. **The cowan in winter.** An unfree man — skilled enough, hungry, outside the craft — is laying stone within the burgh at half rate. The Acts fine you 60 shillings if you know and do not reveal his name to the Deacon. His family eats from that wall. Do you inform? — *Informing reveals **guardianship of standards** (the wall of the craft protects everyone inside it); refusing reveals **care for the fallen** turned outward, against the craft's own law. Rooted in the cowan acts of 1600 and the informer statute of 1612.*
4. **Naismith's bed.** You have built a thing no one in Scotland has seen before, and the town swears it will fall. Words will not settle it. Do you have your bed carried out and sleep under your own arch before the crowd — staking your body on your work — or let time prove it and be called a coward by masons and a fool by none? — *Sleeping under it reveals **proof over promise** at its purest: the maker as guarantor. Declining reveals **permanence** — the quiet faith that the stone needs no theatre. Rooted in the St Andrew's in the Square portico legend, c. 1756.*
5. **Pears and white heather.** The night before the election, the man who would be Deacon feeds you from his orchard and asks you to wear white heather tomorrow so he may count his votes across the hall. You owe him nothing but this evening. Wear it? — *Wearing it reveals **loyalty made visible** (allegiance declared and countable, the craft's open-ledger honesty); refusing reveals **rank earned by service** — a deaconship should be built like a wall, course upon course, not bought with fruit. Rooted in the Pear Eating origin legend.*
6. **The essay and the three masters.** Your essay is judged by three of the "perfiteast men of craft" — who are also the three builders you will underbid next season, and they have failed it on a fault you cannot find. The law of 1551 makes them judges; the magistrates could overrule them, at the price of shaming the craft's right to govern itself. Cut a new essay in silence, or appeal? — *Cutting again reveals **rank earned by service** and submission to the craft's collective judgment; appealing reveals **proof over promise** turned against the craft itself — the standard matters more than the guild. Rooted in the 1551 examination statute and the 1620 essay act.*
