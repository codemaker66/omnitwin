# The Trades House of Glasgow

> Not a fifteenth craft but the body that binds the fourteen: constituted by the Letter of Guildry, sealed 6 February 1605, as the Deacon Convener's Council of the Craft rank of Glasgow's Guildry ([British History Online — Letter of Guildry appendix](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/dcv-dcxxii); [Trades House — Who we are](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/who-we-are/)). This dossier anchors the sorting-quiz narrator — a portrait hanging in the Trades Hall — in the institution's own verified lore.

## Essence (the institution's soul in one paragraph)

The Trades House is fourteen quarrels bound into one body. It was not born of brotherhood but of arbitration — merchants and craftsmen had fought for the town since the Reformation removed the Church from its ruling place, and the Crown's arbiters ended the war with a deed both sides could live with and neither loved ([Guildry, scienceonstreets/Strathclyde, via search excerpt](https://scienceonstreets.phys.strath.ac.uk/new/Guildry.html); [British History Online](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/dcv-dcxxii)). Its arms are fourteen arrows in a bundle and its motto is *Union is Strength* — "an arrow on its own is weak, but fourteen together are strong" ([Trades House Virtual Museum — Entrance Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/entrance-hall.html)) — yet the same fourteen sued each other over the order of their chairs for half a century until the Court of Session fixed the ranking in 1777 ([Hammermen of Glasgow — Early history](https://hammermenofglasgow.org/our-history/)). It accepted second place behind the merchants forever, and never forgot it. It kept the poor's box before everything: a Crafts Hospital funded from 1607, a free school from 1806, and today more than £750,000 a year given away from funds exceeding £18 million ([Records of the Trades House 1605–1678](https://archive.org/stream/recordsoftradesh00glas/recordsoftradesh00glas_djvu.txt); [Trades House — What we do](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/what-we-do/)). And it built itself a hall by the finest architect in Britain so that the union of the trades would outlast the trades themselves — which it has.

## History

**Before 1605 — crafts without a Guildry.** Glasgow's individual crafts are older than the House: the Skinners' members roll runs from 1516, the Masons' from 1574, the Hammermen sought to "regulate the trade in metalworking within the Burgh of Glasgow" from 1536 ([Trades House Virtual Museum — home/navigation](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/); [Hammermen](https://hammermenofglasgow.org/our-history/)). "Prior to 1605, there was no Guild of Merchants and no Convenery of Trades, though there were isolated craft associations, most of them legally recognised with Charters and Seals of Cause" ([Records of the Trades House 1605–1678, via search excerpt of the full text](https://archive.org/stream/recordsoftradesh00glas/recordsoftradesh00glas_djvu.txt)). Two royal mercies were remembered ever after in stained glass: King James IV "decided that half the Town Council of Glasgow should be craftsmen," and "Mary Queen of Scots restored the right to elect Deacons in 1556" ([Trades House Virtual Museum — Saloon Stained Glass Window](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/saloon-stained-glass-window.html)). After the Reformation of 1560 removed the Church from its ruling position, "the merchants and the craftsmen fought for control of its administration, with tension and confusion only resolved when the Crown stepped in" ([Guildry — Strathclyde, via search excerpt](https://scienceonstreets.phys.strath.ac.uk/new/Guildry.html)).

**The Letter of Guildry, 6 February 1605.** The "animosities between merchants and craftsmen were resolved by submitting points of difference" — "privileges, places, ranks, and prerogatives" — to arbiters: Sir George Elphinstone of Blythswood, then Provost; Mr David Weems, the Parson of Glasgow; and the city ministers John Bell and Robert Scott ([Chronicles of the Maltmen Craft in Glasgow 1605–1879, via search excerpt](https://archive.org/stream/chroniclesofmalt00incoiala/chroniclesofmalt00incoiala_djvu.txt); the British History Online transcript names Elphinstone, "Master David Wemis" and "Master John Bell" as the oversmen — [BHO appendix](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/dcv-dcxxii)). Their award "is termed the 'Letter of Guildry,' and contains the Constitutions of the present Merchants' House, Trades' House, and Incorporation of Maltmen": "there shall be in all time coming ane Dean of Gild, ane Deacon Conveener, and ane Visitor of Maltmen" ([Chronicles of the Maltmen, via search excerpt](https://archive.org/stream/chroniclesofmalt00incoiala/chroniclesofmalt00incoiala_djvu.txt)). The deed was approved by the town council and recorded in the burgh court books; it fixed entry money (a burgess becoming guild brother, 13s 4d; a guild brother's son, 20s; an "outland" merchant or craftsman, £30 Scots; an apprentice after service, 10 merks), required masters to testify on oath that an apprentice's service was truly complete, and directed hospital revenues to "decayit brethrene" ([BHO appendix](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/dcv-dcxxii)). And it contained the clause the crafts would quote for four centuries — that merchant and craft guild brethren, "everie ane of thame, as ane body of the commoun weill, sall rank thame selfis togidder, but distinctioun" — together, without distinction ([BHO appendix](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/dcv-dcxxii)).

**The House constituted, 1604–1605.** The Deacon Convener's office is headed "from 1604" in the House's own records, and the first name on the Grand Hall boards is "Duncan Sempill, Skipper 1604" — the crafts had convened under him before the Letter was sealed the following February ([Trades House Digital Library — Deacon Convenors](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/deacon-convenors.html); [Virtual Museum — Grand Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/grand-hall.html)). Sempill was "a Skipper, whose craft along with the Mealmen was merged with that of the Maltmen" — the only Trade Skipper on any board ([Records of the Trades House, via search excerpt](https://archive.org/stream/recordsoftradesh00glas/recordsoftradesh00glas_djvu.txt); [Virtual Museum — Grand Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/grand-hall.html)). In October 1605 the Convener's Council comprised the deacons of the hammermen, tailors, cordiners, baxters, weavers, skinners, wrights, coopers, fleshers, masons, bonnetmakers and chirurgeons, plus "a visitor for maltmen and meilmen"; that month four craftsmen — Duncan Sempill, "M. Peter Lowe," John Anderson and John Muir — were chosen to sit on the merchant-dominated Dean of Guild's Council ([Records 1605–1678, full-text extraction](https://archive.org/stream/recordsoftradesh00glas/recordsoftradesh00glas_djvu.txt)). The House's business from the first was the whole craft rank's business: settling demarcation ("ane setled ordo' betuix ye saidis masonis and wreichtis" over slate-work, May 1607), disciplining non-guild-brethren who would "never [be] promottit to bruik ony office," and negotiating with the Dean of Guild "how ye dett lyand wpone ye toun may be payit" ([Records 1605–1678](https://archive.org/stream/recordsoftradesh00glas/recordsoftradesh00glas_djvu.txt)).

**The Crafts Hospital and almshouse.** From 1607 the records document the crafts' great joint enterprise: a hospital for poor and disabled craftsmen, funded by pledges craft by craft (James Lichtbodie promised "the sowme of fiftie pundis money"; the maltmen and mealmen gave "sevin scoir xij li" yearly toward "the poore of the said craftis hospitall") ([Records 1605–1678](https://archive.org/stream/recordsoftradesh00glas/recordsoftradesh00glas_djvu.txt)). The "Crafts Hospital" or "Trades Almshouse" stood "between Rottenrow and St Nicholas Hospital and Peter Lowe who founded the Faculty of Physicians and Surgeons of Glasgow was the first surgeon of this institution in 1609" ([Glasgow Royal Infirmary endocrinology history blog, via search excerpt](https://endocrinologyatglasgowroyalinfirmary.wordpress.com/2018/05/04/a-royal-infirmary-appears/)). Before Glassford Street, "the Crafts met in an Almshouse on the corner of Cathedral Street and the High Street, where the Barony Hall of Strathclyde University sits today" ([Trades House — Trades Hall](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/trades-hall/), corner detail via search excerpt of the same official material).

**Precedence settled by law, 1724–1777.** "In the eighteenth century, the fourteen crafts established their order of precedence. The Incorporation of Hammermen were awarded the premier position in 1777 by the Court of Session" ([Hammermen](https://hammermenofglasgow.org/our-history/)). The fights were real and specific: in 1724 the Barbers claimed a seat above the Masons before the whole House and lost, ranked "immediatly before the Bonnetmakers To witt between the Gardeners and Bonnetmakers... in all time comeing," a ruling confirmed by the Court of Session in 1777 (Records of the Incorporation of Barbers, 1899 — see the companion dossier [13-barbers.md](./13-barbers.md) where this is cited in full).

**Decline of privilege, endurance of purpose.** The 1846 Act abolishing the Scottish incorporations' exclusive trading privileges left the crafts and the House as charitable and civic bodies (cited from the Barbers' Records in [13-barbers.md](./13-barbers.md); the House's own summary is that it "was established to help protect and support the Crafts people of the City" and is today "a charity" — [Who we are](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/who-we-are/)). The Local Government (Scotland) Act 1973 "abolished all non-elected posts" on the Council, ending four centuries of ex officio civic office; the Dean of Guild sat "until the 16th May, 1975," and both the Lord Dean and the Deacon Convener were thereafter invited to attend "in an honorary non-voting capacity" ([Merchants House — History](https://www.merchantshouse.org.uk/history/); [Who we are](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/who-we-are/)). The Deacon Convener's council seat "is retained to this day, but no longer has speaking or voting rights" ([Who we are](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/who-we-are/)).

## The Deacon Convener

- **Rank.** "The Deacon Convener is the third citizen of Glasgow after the Lord Provost and the Lord Dean of Guild" ([Trades House Digital Library — Deacon Convenors](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/deacon-convenors.html)).
- **Function.** "The title 'Deacon Convener' was coined because s/he 'convenes' the House Trustees" ([Who we are](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/who-we-are/)). Under the Letter of Guildry the Convener was elected annually, "eight days after bailies' selection," and "could not serve more than two consecutive years" ([BHO appendix](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/dcv-dcxxii)).
- **Regalia.** "The custom of the Deacon Convener wearing a chain came into being in 1766"; the first chain arrived "on 15th September 1787"; the present chain was "Purchased in 1887 for £208 and made in Glasgow... 18ct gold," with a 22ct-gold medal bearing "the names of the Deacon Conveners from 1885/6 to 1985/6" ([Virtual Museum — Deacon Conveners' Artefacts](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/deacon-conveners-artefacts.html)). Dress in the 1880s was a formal suit with a Court sword; the modern dress is "silk jacket, striped trousers, and small Court buttons" ([Digital Library — Deacon Convenors](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/deacon-convenors.html)). The Late Deacon Conveners' chain carries "links shields depicting the unregistered coats of arms of the 14 Incorporations" ([Deacon Conveners' Artefacts](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/deacon-conveners-artefacts.html)).
- **Chair.** The Deacon Convener's Chair, designed by David Hamilton in 1819, carries "fourteen silver shields each engraved with the coats of arms" of the Incorporations ([Visitors Guide to Scotland — Glasgow's Trades Hall](https://www.visitorsguidetoscotland.com/2024/12/glasgows-trades-hall/)).
- **Governance today.** "The Platform — similar to a corporate board — comprises seven members serving consecutive one-year roles, with one new member elected annually" ([Who we are](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/who-we-are/)).

## Shared rituals & customs

- **Deacons' Choosing Day.** The Hall itself was "first used in September 1794 on Deacons' Choosing Day, when the Incorporated Trades held their elections" — the election of the fourteen deacons is the institution's oldest shared rite, and the building was inaugurated with it ([TheGlasgowStory — The Trades Hall](https://theglasgowstory.com/story/?id=TGSCF06)).
- **The chain and the dancing medal.** The Deacon Convener's chain (1766 custom, 1887 chain) has a gentler twin: at balls "the chain is heavy and could cause damage to their partner," so deacons wear a "Dancing Medal" — "a smaller version of the Deacon's Medal" on a ribbon. The Masons' dancing medal is the Sir John Train Medallion, given by the Deacon of 1923 who became Deacon Convener 1927–28 ([Virtual Museum — Deacons' Dancing Medals](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/deacons-dancing-medals.html)).
- **The bell.** In the belfry above the Hall hangs a bell by "Thomas Mears of London," 1794, inscribed "Trades Hall of Glasgow" — in "an area that very few members of the Trades House have had the pleasure to visit," reached through a roof of prefabricated beams still bearing their carpenters' joint numbers ([Virtual Museum — Belfry](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/belfry.html)).
- **Banners and colours.** The Hammermen keep "two banners from the Battle of Langside in 1568 also flown at Falkirk 1746" ([Hammermen](https://hammermenofglasgow.org/our-history/)). When invasion threatened in 1803 the House "agreed to raise a Battalion of 600 craftsmen" and "voted £500" for clothing and supplies, the officers "to be chosen by the craftsmen themselves"; its Regimental Colour bore "the Trades House 14 Arrows in place of the Crown," and the colours survive in the Trades Hall today ([Virtual Museum — Trades House Military Heritage](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/trades-house-military-heritage.html)).
- **Dinners.** Formal dinners fill the Grand Hall with "Deacons and guests wear[ing] tartan at black-tie dinners" ([Virtual Museum — Entrance Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/entrance-hall.html)); the clock above the main door is "probably the most useful item in the Grand Hall" because hosts watch it through the speeches ([Virtual Museum — Grand Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/grand-hall.html)).
- **The school.** The Trades Free School, established 1806: "sixty boys took their places, sitting on oak benches" in the purpose-built schoolroom above the Saloon; girls admitted 1838; 216 pupils by 1844; instruction in "reading, writing, arithmetic, geography, church music and the principles of religion," with silver medals for prizes; closed 1876 after the Education Act of 1872 "placed the responsibility for education on the state" ([Virtual Museum — Craftsmen's Gallery](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/craftsmans-gallery.html)).
- **Memory as furniture.** Names are the House's liturgy: Deacon Convener boards in the Grand Hall from "Duncan Sempill, Skipper 1604"; mahogany panels in the Saloon recording "names and donations left in mortification" in merks and pounds Scots; a 22ct medal engraved with a century of Conveners; the library's "photographic records of the Deacons and Visitor over the years" ([Grand Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/grand-hall.html); [Saloon](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/saloon.html); [Deacon Conveners' Artefacts](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/deacon-conveners-artefacts.html); [Library](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/library.html)).
- **The "Chain Gang."** The library was renovated by "the 1994 Chain Gang in celebration of 200 years of the Trades Halls" — the House's own name for a cohort of chain-wearing office-bearers ([Virtual Museum — Library](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/library.html)). [extrapolated: that "Chain Gang" denotes past/serving chain-holders is inferred from the name; the museum page does not define it.]

## The Trades Hall, room by room

**Commission and construction.** Three architects submitted plans in 1791: John Craig and James Jaffray were paid £31 10s each for theirs, Robert Adam 50 guineas — "Robert Adam had a greater name as an architect hence he received 50 guineas" ([Virtual Museum — Trades Hall of Glasgow](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/trades-hall-of-glasgow.html)). Built 1791–1794; Adam died in 1792 and "his younger brothers, James and William Adam... saw the building finished," with the wright John Brown as principal contractor ([Virtual Museum](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/trades-hall-of-glasgow.html); [TheGlasgowStory](https://theglasgowstory.com/story/?id=TGSCF06)). It cost "£7,927" to complete and furnish ([Trades House — Trades Hall](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/trades-hall/)). It is "the only major Adam work surviving in Glasgow, and the oldest building (apart from the medieval cathedral) still used for its original purpose" ([Trades House — Trades Hall](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/trades-hall/)); Category A listed since 1966 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_Trades_Hall)). Later hands: David Hamilton (north pavilion raised 1806 for the school; south pavilion extended 1837 to create the Saloon; Grand Hall ceiling 1840), James Sellars (c. 1888), John Keppie (1916), Walter Underwood (ceilings 1953/1955); a public trust took the building for preservation in 1977, and a 2003 restoration reinstated "three large venetian-style windows in the Glassford Street facade" ([TheGlasgowStory](https://theglasgowstory.com/story/?id=TGSCF06); [Virtual Museum — Grand Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/grand-hall.html); [Saloon](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/saloon.html)).

- **Entrance Hall.** A ceramic mosaic floor in which "the name of the building can be clearly seen"; the Arms of the Trades House with "fourteen arrows representing each of the fourteen Incorporations" and the motto "Union is Strength" on a red field distinguishing it from the city's arms; two long carved oak benches "made by Belgium woodcarvers who were refugees in Glasgow during the First World War," carved with "old crests of the Incorporations," donated by Alexander Walker, former Deacon of the Cordiners, in 1937. Adam "envisaged a cross-vaulted ceiling" here; "the present ceiling probably dates from the 1930s" ([Virtual Museum — Entrance Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/entrance-hall.html)).
- **Stair to the Grand Hall.** "A typical Adam double staircase plan," rebuilt by Sellars c. 1888, "one of the few remaining areas which retains original details"; plasterwork of "the fourteen sheaves of arrows," which "appear more frequently throughout the building than the Arms of The Trades House"; a "magnificent stained glass window with the crests of the fourteen Incorporated Trades as they were in 1888"; busts of Deacon Conveners James McLennan (1893–94) and William MacLean (1827–28); portraits of clerk George Crawfurd (1831–75) and of James Buchanan — who rose "from humble beginnings as a blacksmith" to wealth trading with Jamaica and left "a £10,000 legacy for craftsmen's children's education" — with Mrs Buchanan beside him. "Above the entrance to the Grand Hall is a marble tablet commemorating the opening of the Trades Halls in 1794" ([Virtual Museum — Stair to Grand Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/stair-to-grand-hall.html)).
- **Grand Hall.** Originally "the entire first floor"; five windows ("three large arches windows and two smaller rectangular"), the Trades House coat of arms above the central one; a seven-metre dome with the arms of the fourteen Incorporations; ceiling replaced 1840 (Hamilton) and 1953 (Underwood — traffic had made the old one unstable), the present one of waxed, fireproofed avodire — "rare West African avodire wood"; mahogany panelling by Sellars c. 1888; one of the two surviving Robert Adam fireplaces; the Deacon Convener boards; the watched clock ([Virtual Museum — Grand Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/grand-hall.html); [Trades Hall — Rooms](https://www.tradeshallglasgow.co.uk/rooms/)). Around the perimeter runs the frieze: "painted by Belgium artists shortly after the Boer War," still incomplete in October 1903, "64 metres (210 feet) long," depicting "the fourteen Incorporated Crafts in a historical romanticized style" with 161 people — only four of them women, found among the Barbers, Cordiners and Fleshers — plus "one cow, one donkey, two sheep and two pigs" ([Virtual Museum — Frieze in the Grand Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/frieze-in-the-grand-hall.html); the venue's marketing calls it a "breathtaking gold frieze" — [Trades Hall — Rooms](https://www.tradeshallglasgow.co.uk/rooms/)). Capacity today: 250 theatre/reception, 180 dinner, 210 m² ([Trades Hall — Rooms](https://www.tradeshallglasgow.co.uk/rooms/)).
- **The hidden mural.** Beneath woodchip wallpaper in the Grand Hall sleeps "a series of large painted panels depicting allegorical figures representing Charity, Science, Justice, Plenty, Commerce and Peace, separated by smaller panels with images of birds," painted September–December 1855 by Campbell Tait Bowie (1825–1896), apprentice of D. R. Hay and collaborator of Alexander "Greek" Thomson. Discovered during 2003 renovations; "due to ongoing Hall commitments at the time, it was not possible to undertake a full investigation," so it was covered in conservation materials and papered over again — "one of the finest surviving examples of the work of this important artist," waiting ([Virtual Museum — Hidden Mural](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/hidden-mural.html)).
- **Saloon.** Added 1837–38 by Hamilton; refurbished 1916 by Keppie with "mahogany panelling which records donations to the Trades House and the Incorporations" in merks and pounds Scots; the second Adam fireplace moved here from the Grand Hall in 1916, topped by an onyx clock commissioned for the 1953 Coronation; ceiling replaced 1955 by Underwood after "a minor earthquake in 1932"; electroliers "purchased from the Grosvenor Restaurant in Gordon Street after it was destroyed by fire in 1970." Serves as Council Chamber for Trades House meetings ([Virtual Museum — Saloon](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/saloon.html); [TheGlasgowStory](https://theglasgowstory.com/story/?id=TGSCF06)). Its stained glass, 1915, by Glasgow's Guthrie and Wells, was given by Deacon Convener William Beattie, Late Deacon Convener A. Graham Service and Collector Jackson Millar: Mary Queen of Scots (left), Beattie himself "dressed as a Burgess of 1604" (centre), and King James IV (right), each with arms ([Virtual Museum — Saloon Stained Glass Window](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/saloon-stained-glass-window.html)).
- **Reception Room, Robert Adam Room, Galleries.** The Reception Room offers "a vibrant and contemporary ambiance" (150 m²); the "newly refurbished" Robert Adam Room has wooden floors, chandeliers and a bar; the North and South Galleries on the second floor are twins for smaller meetings ([Trades Hall — Rooms](https://www.tradeshallglasgow.co.uk/rooms/)).
- **Craftsmen's Gallery.** "A corridor and museum" added 1808 above the Saloon, originally the Trades Free School's schoolroom; now home to the Deacon Convener's historic "Hat and shoes with silver buckles," ceremonial maces gifted by the trades, a pupil's prize tapestry of the Finding of Moses, and "The Bakers Chair which is made from reclaimed timber from Glasgow Cathedral and Culross Abbey" ([Virtual Museum — Craftsmen's Gallery](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/craftsmans-gallery.html)).
- **Library.** Renovated by the 1994 Chain Gang; "an extensive collection of books about the history of the Trades House, Hall, Crafts and also the City of Glasgow" (reference only — "books may not be removed"); "photographic records of the Deacons and Visitor over the years"; "a copy in oils of the 'Four Philosophers' by Peter Paul Rubens"; and "the chairs used by the Deacons and Visitor of the fourteen Incorporations" ([Virtual Museum — Library](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/library.html)).
- **Belfry.** The 1794 Mears bell, its pulley wheel, joint-numbered roof beams — see Rituals above ([Virtual Museum — Belfry](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/belfry.html)).
- **Portraits worth a narrator's envy.** The collection includes Frédéric Chopin, "who visited Glasgow in 1848" ([Visitors Guide to Scotland](https://www.visitorsguidetoscotland.com/2024/12/glasgows-trades-hall/)).

## Relations with the Merchants House

One arbitration, two houses. The Letter of Guildry "regulated both the city's craftsmen (in the form of the Trades' House), and its merchants (in the form of Merchants' House)" ([Guildry — Strathclyde, via search excerpt](https://scienceonstreets.phys.strath.ac.uk/new/Guildry.html)); it "still forms the basic constitution of the Merchants House" as well ([Merchants House — History](https://www.merchantshouse.org.uk/history/)). The settlement's asymmetry is permanent and precise: the Dean of Guild (always a merchant) is the second citizen of Glasgow, the Deacon Convener (always a craftsman) the third ([Digital Library — Deacon Convenors](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/deacon-convenors.html); [Merchants House](https://www.merchantshouse.org.uk/history/)). Yet the same deed made them "ane body of the commoun weill... but distinctioun" ([BHO appendix](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/dcv-dcxxii)), and the structures interlock: from October 1605 the crafts elected four of their own onto the Dean of Guild's council of "four merchants and four craftsmen" ([Records 1605–1678](https://archive.org/stream/recordsoftradesh00glas/recordsoftradesh00glas_djvu.txt); [BHO appendix](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/dcv-dcxxii)). The two houses aged in parallel: both lost their council seats to the 1973 Act (the Dean sitting ex officio "until the 16th May, 1975"), both were re-invited "in an honorary non-voting capacity," and both are now charities — the Merchants House (SC008900) supporting "charities in the West of Scotland" as the Trades House supports its own causes ([Merchants House — History](https://www.merchantshouse.org.uk/history/)).

## The modern charitable role

- **Scale.** "Funds in excess of £18 million"; "donations of more than £750,000 are awarded annually to deserving causes and individuals across the West of Scotland" ([What we do](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/what-we-do/)); the House and its fourteen crafts together "make donations of more than £750,000 annually" ([official material via search excerpt](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/what-we-do/)).
- **The funds.** Commonweal Fund (grants to "charities and organisations within the city of Glasgow and surrounding areas," committee meeting March and September), MacFarlane Trust, Drapers' Fund ("children, aged up to and including 17 years of age and their carers in the wider Glasgow area where there is great and urgent need"), Relief Fund, Education Fund ([Charitable — Commonweal](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/charitable-commonweal/) and [Drapers](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/charitable-drapers/), via search excerpts; [What we do](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/what-we-do/)).
- **Education and craft.** Glasgow Modern Apprentice Awards; Craftex (craft-skills showcase); the Citizenship Award, "open to all schools - pre-5, primary, secondary and special needs," recognising work by schools "in, for and with their communities"; Robert Burns Festival; School Craft Competition; the Trades House Lecture; "Determined to Make Movies" ([What we do](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/what-we-do/); [Education — Citizenship](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/education-citizenship/), via search excerpt).

## Values

1. **Union is strength.** Fourteen arrows on a red field; "an arrow on its own is weak, but fourteen together are strong." The sheaves of arrows recur through the building more often than the full arms — the bundle, not the badge, is the theology ([Entrance Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/entrance-hall.html); [Stair to Grand Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/stair-to-grand-hall.html)).
2. **Order accepted as the price of peace.** Born of arbitration, not victory: rank "but distinctioun" with the merchants, precedence fixed by the Court of Session, second and third citizen forever — the House's genius is living inside a settlement it did not entirely win ([BHO appendix](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/dcv-dcxxii); [Hammermen](https://hammermenofglasgow.org/our-history/); [Digital Library — Deacon Convenors](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/deacon-convenors.html)).
3. **The poor's box before everything.** Hospital pledges from 1607, "decayit brethrene" in the founding deed, a free school for craftsmen's children, £750,000+ a year now ([Records 1605–1678](https://archive.org/stream/recordsoftradesh00glas/recordsoftradesh00glas_djvu.txt); [BHO appendix](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/dcv-dcxxii); [What we do](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/what-we-do/)).
4. **Memory kept in oak and gold.** Boards of names from 1604, donation panels in merks and Scots pounds, a medal engraved with a century of Conveners, photograph albums of every Deacon — the institution's sacrament is the recorded name ([Grand Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/grand-hall.html); [Saloon](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/saloon.html); [Library](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/library.html)).
5. **Craft made visible and warranted.** A hall by the best architect money could flatter, a 64-metre frieze of the trades at work, colours bearing the arrows in place of the Crown — the work itself displayed as the argument for the institution ([Trades Hall of Glasgow](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/trades-hall-of-glasgow.html); [Frieze](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/frieze-in-the-grand-hall.html); [Military Heritage](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/trades-house-military-heritage.html)).
6. **Lawful tenacity.** Every fight in the record — demarcation of masons and wrights, precedence, privileges — ends in an order, an act, a minute, a ruling obeyed; the House's weapon is the settled "ordo'", not the riot ([Records 1605–1678](https://archive.org/stream/recordsoftradesh00glas/recordsoftradesh00glas_djvu.txt)).
7. **Service to the commonweal.** The founding deed's own phrase — "ane body of the commoun weill" — is still the name on the cheque-book: the Commonweal Fund ([BHO appendix](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/dcv-dcxxii); [Charitable — Commonweal](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/charitable-commonweal/)).

## Shadow

Each virtue, curdled. **Union** curdles into rank-obsession: the body that preached fourteen-as-one spent the eighteenth century suing itself over the order of chairs, and needed the Court of Session to say who sat first ([Hammermen](https://hammermenofglasgow.org/our-history/)). **Order accepted** curdles into permanent second place worn as identity — third citizen, a council seat with "not a voice nor a vote," dignity as presence without power ([Digital Library — Deacon Convenors](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/deacon-convenors.html); [Who we are](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/who-we-are/)). **The poor's box** curdles into gatekeeping — the founding deed prices entry by blood and origin (a son 20s, an "outland" man £30) ([BHO appendix](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/dcv-dcxxii)), and the frieze that glorifies the crafts finds room for 161 figures but only four women ([Frieze](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/frieze-in-the-grand-hall.html)). **Memory** curdles into embalming: an institution that panels its walls with the names of donors can end by loving the names more than the living trades — the school closed in 1876, the trading privileges died in 1846, and what remains risks becoming a museum of itself that dines beneath a mural it keeps wallpapered over ([Craftsmen's Gallery](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/craftsmans-gallery.html); [Hidden Mural](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/hidden-mural.html)). **Craft made visible** curdles into spectacle — a "gold frieze" sold as wedding backdrop ([Trades Hall — Rooms](https://www.tradeshallglasgow.co.uk/rooms/)). And **wealth given** can have unexamined roots: the stair hangs the portrait of a blacksmith who made his educational legacy "trading with Jamaica" in the age of the plantations ([Stair to Grand Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/stair-to-grand-hall.html)) [extrapolated: the source states only "trading with Jamaica"; the plantation-economy context of that trade is historical inference, not stated by the museum].

## The narrator's anchor — a portrait in the Hall

Verified fixtures a portrait-narrator can truthfully claim as neighbours and vantage points:

- Hanging on the **Adam double staircase**: George Crawfurd (clerk for forty-four years, 1831–75), James Buchanan (blacksmith → Jamaica trader → £10,000 for craftsmen's children) and Mrs Buchanan; busts of Conveners McLennan and MacLean; the 1888 stained glass of all fourteen crests colouring the light ([Stair to Grand Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/stair-to-grand-hall.html)).

## Question Seeds

Dilemmas built from the House's actual record — each tests an institutional value or its shadow. (These frame the *narrator's* sensibility; the fourteen craft dossiers carry the per-craft dilemmas.)

1. **The Order of Chairs.** Two of your fourteen crafts are at the Court of Session's door over who processes first, and the lawyers will eat a year of the poor's box either way. You can impose a ruling tonight — wronging one craft by fiat to keep the money — or let the law settle it justly, slowly, expensively, and admit before all Glasgow that the bundle of arrows cannot bind itself. — *Imposing reveals **union** shading into control; the courts reveal **lawful tenacity** — and its price, paid by the poor ([Hammermen, 1777](https://hammermenofglasgow.org/our-history/)).*
2. **The Mural Under the Wallpaper.** Bowie's allegories — Charity, Justice, Peace — sleep under the woodchip, and restoring them means closing the Grand Hall for a season: the Hall whose lettings fund the pensioners. Beauty owed to the dead craftsman, or bread owed to the living poor? You cannot have both this year. — *Restoring reveals **memory** and **craft made visible**; keeping the wallpaper reveals **the poor's box before everything** — and asks when thrift becomes the burial of your own soul ([Hidden Mural](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/hidden-mural.html)).*
3. **Second Citizen Forever.** The 1605 deed calls merchant and craftsman "ane body... but distinctioun" — and in the same breath makes the Dean of Guild second citizen and your Convener third, in all time coming. Peace on those terms has held four hundred years. A young deacon moves that the House refuse the third place at the next civic procession until parity is granted. — *Voting him down reveals **order accepted as the price of peace**; voting with him reveals the clause's promise taken literally — and risks the war the arbiters ended ([BHO](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/dcv-dcxxii); [Deacon Convenors](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/deacon-convenors.html)).*
4. **Buchanan's Legacy.** The blacksmith's £10,000 educates craftsmen's children to this day, and his portrait hangs on the stair — wealth made "trading with Jamaica" in the plantation age. A member moves to take the portrait down; another answers that the children his money schooled are the House's truest monument. The portrait beside his can hear the whole debate. — *Keeping it with the history told reveals **memory** at its most honest; removing it reveals conscience against continuity; keeping it silent reveals memory curdled into embalming ([Stair to Grand Hall](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/stair-to-grand-hall.html)) [extrapolated: plantation-context inference tagged in Shadow].*
5. **The State Takes Your School.** It is 1876. The Education Act has made the Trades Free School redundant — the state now does, for every child, what you did for sixty boys on oak benches. Half the House wants to fight to keep the school as identity; half wants to close it and find the next wound no one else is dressing. — *Closing reveals **service to the commonweal** over self-perpetuation; fighting reveals the institution's love of its own reflection ([Craftsmen's Gallery](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/craftsmans-gallery.html)).*
6. **The Seat Without a Voice.** Since 1975 your Convener sits in the City Chambers by invitation, honorary, "not a voice nor a vote." A motion proposes ending the attendance: better absent with pride than present as furniture. The old Conveners on the Grand Hall boards seem to watch the vote. — *Keeping the seat reveals **order accepted** — witness as duty; leaving it reveals dignity that mistakes ceremony for humiliation, or names it truly ([Merchants House — History](https://www.merchantshouse.org.uk/history/); [Deacon Convenors](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/deacon-convenors.html)).*

### Honesty notes

- **1604 vs 1605.** The House's own boards and records head the Deacon Convener line "from 1604" (Duncan Sempill), while the Letter of Guildry is dated 6 February 1605; both are reported, not harmonised.
- **The arbiters.** BHO's transcript names three oversmen (Elphinstone, Wemis, Bell); the Maltmen's Chronicles name four (adding Robert Scott). Both counts are given with their sources.
- **Saloon window discrepancy.** Museum: 1915, Guthrie and Wells, King James IV. Visitors Guide: 1916, James VI. The museum (the House's own) is followed; the conflict is flagged.
- **Precedence table.** Only three anchor points are directly sourced (Hammermen 1st, Barbers 13th, Bonnetmakers last-adjacent); the full 1–14 mapping is tagged as an inference from the House's invariant listing order.
- **Lumsden's *Traditions* PDF** (electricscotland) could not be text-extracted this session (scanned images); its facts enter only via the companion Barbers dossier where they were previously extracted and cited.
- **Leaflet figures** (£926,319; 810/81/235; SC040548) are reproduced only as *unverified* per the repo's own source record; the citable public figure is the official "more than £750,000 annually."
- **No verified details found** for: the modern installation ceremony/kirking of the Deacon Convener (Edinburgh's equivalents are documented, Glasgow's are not, in the sources consulted); when the 1794 Mears bell is actually rung; the material of the 1903 frieze (painted; "silk" is not confirmed by the museum and the venue's "gold frieze" is marketing language); the fate of the Trades Hospital building. These are left absent rather than invented.
