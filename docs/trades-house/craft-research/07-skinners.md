# Incorporation of Skinners & Glovers

> Research dossier for the Trades House "sorting hat" quiz. Seventh craft in the customary order of Glasgow's fourteen Incorporated Trades; claimant to the title of oldest. Facts are cited inline; anything extended from general Scottish guild practice rather than Glasgow Skinner evidence is tagged **[extrapolated]**.

## Essence (the craft's soul in one paragraph)

The Skinners are the craft that turns death into warmth. They took the beast at the point where the flesher left it — a wet hide, heavy with blood and hair — and by lime and bark and patience made it into fur-lined caps, white-tawed gloves, bindings for books, warmth against a Scottish winter. They are Glasgow's eldest incorporated craft, chartered on 28 May 1516, and their founding document is not really about skins at all: it is two crafts promising to "unyte ourself in cherite togidder" — to bind themselves together in charity — under the altar of St Christopher, the giant who carried travellers across the river. Everything essential is already there: primacy worn quietly, judgment of quality by sworn masters, the weekly penny for the altar and the poor, and a motto that hands the credit away — *To God only be all Glory*. A skinner's work is slow, foul-smelling, and invisible in the finished glove; the craft's soul is exactly that: patient transformation, rigorously judged, credited to someone else.

## History (founding, seal of cause date, key episodes — cite sources inline)

- **28 May 1516 — the Seal of Cause.** The provost, bailies and council of Glasgow, with consent of the burgh's feudal superior Archbishop James Beaton (then also Chancellor of Scotland), granted a seal of cause to the crafts of skinners and furriers — "so far as can be ascertained, the first example of such procedure in Glasgow" ([Renwick & Lindsay, *History of Glasgow*, ch. XLIII](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow48.htm); [Trades House of Glasgow](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-skinners-glovers/)). On this the Incorporation "claims to be the oldest of the fourteen Incorporated Trades of Glasgow" ([Trades House](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-skinners-glovers/)).
- **The petition itself** came from the "kirkmaisters and the laif of the maisteris of skynner craft and furrier craft," being "tua craftis and unyte ourself in cherite togidder," presented to the town "sittand in jugement, counsalie gaderit," and framed "according to the lawis and consuetis of grete townis of honour of uther realmes and provinces" ([Renwick & Lindsay](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow48.htm)).
- **Its terms:** no member to set up booth unless found qualified by the council and sworn masters; upset of 5s for a freeman's son, 10s for an unfreeman's son, toward divine service at the altar; no master to reset another master's apprentice (penalty: a pound of wax candles to the altar); a weekly penny from each booth-holding master for the altar's adornment and the priest's sustenance; no false stuff to be sold, on pain of half a pound of wax and forfeiture of the goods ([Renwick & Lindsay](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow48.htm); [British History Online, Glasgow charters preface](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/xlviii-lxx)). The kirkmasters had power, "along with an officer of the town, to poind and distrain for the foresaid duties" ([BHO abstracts](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/pp3-27)). The original seal of cause survives in the Incorporation's possession, with a notarial copy made 8 December 1707 ([Renwick & Lindsay](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow48.htm); [BHO](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/pp3-27)).
- **A charter born in a year of war.** The seal was granted months after the insurrections of 1515–16, in which John Mure of Caldwell seized the Archbishop's castle in Glasgow (February 1515–16) and the Regent Albany marched on the city to retake it. The oldest craft charter in Glasgow was signed while its granting archbishop was fortifying his palace with a fifteen-foot wall ([Renwick & Lindsay, ch. XLIII](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow48.htm)).
- **Furriers and glovers.** The furriers were united with the skinners from the founding petition of 1516; Trades House sources note the association "since at least 1533" ([Trades House](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-skinners-glovers/)). Glove-making sat inside the craft's monopoly, but glovers were always few: only two glovers were admitted to the skinners' craft in the first half of the 17th century ([TheGlasgowStory](https://www.theglasgowstory.com/story/?id=TGSBD)). The modern style is "Incorporation of Skinners & Glovers of Glasgow."
- **1557 — the first extant minute book** begins (1557–1581), bound in vellum; the second minute book (1582–1586) is bound in a leaf cut from a manuscript of circa 1450 ([Wikimedia Commons category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:History_of_The_Skinners_Furriers_and_Glovers_of_Glasgow)).
- **6 February 1605 — the Letter of Guildry** reorganised Glasgow's burgh government and created the Trades House; the Skinners' funds were thereafter to be applied "to charitable, benevolent, and any other good and pious uses… but primarily to the support of indigent members, widows of members, and orphans" (quoted from the Incorporation's own [1857 Rules](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/skinner_s_rules_and_regulations__1857.pdf)). Deacon Alexander Cauldwell of the skinners sat in the first recorded Trades House convocations of 1605 ([Records of the Trades House of Glasgow 1605–1678](https://archive.org/details/recordsoftradesh00glas)).
- **1 April 1607** — Deacon Cauldwell "for himself and ye brethrein of his craft" promised twenty merks toward building the crafts' hospital ([Records of the Trades House](https://archive.org/details/recordsoftradesh00glas)).
- **From about 1780** the craft turned outward to national affairs: objecting to parliamentary bills, helping raise a battalion of Volunteers for the Napoleonic Wars, sending £200 for famine relief in Scotland in 1799, £200 in 1854 for widows of soldiers and sailors in the Crimean War, and large sums in both World Wars ([Trades House Digital Library](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/skinners.html)).
- **5 December 1857** — the Court of Session approved the Incorporation's revised Rules under the Act abolishing exclusive trading privileges in burghs (1846): the formal end of the trade monopoly and the completed conversion into a charitable and benevolent society ([1857 Rules](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/skinner_s_rules_and_regulations__1857.pdf)).
- **14 February 1924** — arms matriculated by Sir James Balfour Paul, Lord Lyon King of Arms ([grant of arms, Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Grant_of_arms_by_the_Lord_Lyon_King-of-Arms,_14th_February,_1924.jpg)).
- **Today** the craft is a registered charity (SC010473) whose income goes "primarily to the relief of pensioner members, their widows and unmarried daughters," with four elected representatives in the Trades House ([Trades House](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-skinners-glovers/); [TotalGiving](https://www.totalgiving.co.uk/charity/incorporation-of-skinners-and-glovers-of-glasgow)).

## Legends & Episodes (the memorable stuff a storyteller would steal)

- **The charter signed under siege.** Weeks after armed men stormed the Archbishop's castle and the Regent's artillery rolled through Glasgow, the skinners quietly secured the first craft charter in the city's history from that same embattled archbishop's council ([Renwick & Lindsay](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow48.htm)). The eldest craft was born reading the political weather correctly.
- **The minute book bound in a dead book's skin.** The craft's second minute book (1582–1586) is wrapped in a leaf of a churchman's manuscript written around 1450 — after the Reformation, the skinners, of all people, re-used old holy parchment (which is itself prepared skin) to clothe their own records ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:History_of_The_Skinners_Furriers_and_Glovers_of_Glasgow)). Skin outlives what is written on it.
- **Skinners' Green.** West of the Molendinar Burn where it meets the Clyde lay a stretch of common ground "long used by the Skinners of Glasgow for drying their wool and skins and latterly known as Skinners Green"; title-deeds of the district are full of lime-holes and bark-holes, and the green was probably already in craft use by 1503 — before the charter itself ([Renwick & Lindsay, ch. XLI](https://electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow46.htm)). By the late 16th century the tanneries were "alleged to be polluting the Molendinar" ([TheGlasgowStory](https://www.theglasgowstory.com/story/?id=TGSAE)) — the craft that served St Christopher, patron of river-crossings, stood accused of fouling the river.
- **The stranger's brod penny.** From 1587 the craft paid its Officer with the "custom pennies in the market" — the tax on outsider tradesmen bringing skins to sell in Glasgow, "also known as 'strangers' brod pennies'". The men of Gorbals, though, had cut a deal: they paid the Incorporation for the privilege of setting up stalls right next to the skinners ([Bryce, *List of Officers 1568–1851*](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/list_of_skinner_s_officers_from_1568_to_1851.pdf)). Even the competition paid rent to stand beside them.
- **Deacon Cauldwell's promise.** On 1 April 1607, before the assembled deacons, Alexander Cauldwell of the skinners pledged twenty merks of craft money to build the crafts' hospital — charity minuted as solemnly as any contract ([Records of the Trades House](https://archive.org/details/recordsoftradesh00glas)). In the 1609 hospital reckoning the skinners stood at £10 — equal with the weavers and ahead of the coopers, bakers and masons ([same](https://archive.org/details/recordsoftradesh00glas)).
- **The oath by uplifted hand.** In the early Trades House, deacons including the skinners' took office "be eleua'un of everie ane of y[air] handis" and protested they would "do y[air] office to ye glorie of God and to ye weale of ye craftis of yis bur' and citie" ([Records of the Trades House](https://archive.org/details/recordsoftradesh00glas)) — the motto's sentiment, three centuries before the motto was matriculated.
- **The battalion and the £200.** A craft of skin-dressers helped raise a Volunteer battalion in the Napoleonic Wars, and twice sent £200 across the country — to the famine-struck in 1799 and to Crimean widows in 1854 ([Trades House Digital Library](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/skinners.html)).
- **The roll of four centuries.** The Incorporation holds an unbroken roll of members from 1516 to 1936 ([Trades House Virtual Museum](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/skinners-members-from-1516-to-1936.html)), and its officer lists read like a Glasgow ballad: Jaips and Luifes, Gemmills and Cauldwells, Auchinclosses and Tassies — including one Gabriell Conyinghame who served as Officer across three separate spells between 1655 and 1671, and James Younge, Officer for some 27 years to 1736 ([Bryce](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/list_of_skinner_s_officers_from_1568_to_1851.pdf)).
- **A royal-adjacent postscript.** Modern membership rolls have included the Earl of Snowdon ([Trades House Digital Library](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/skinners.html)); the crafts still keep their adorned pews at the annual Glasgow Cathedral service ([Trades House](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-skinners-glovers/)).

## Patron, Arms & Motto (heraldry, patron saint, motto with translation, symbolism)

- **Patron saint: St Christopher.** The 1516 petition's declared purpose was "augmentation of divine service at the altar of St. Christopher, their patron," within the metropolitan kirk (Glasgow Cathedral); the chaplainry at that altar, on the south side of the nave, had been founded about two years earlier by Provost John Schaw with his spouse Marion Crawfurd ([Renwick & Lindsay](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow48.htm)). The same dedication was used by the Edinburgh skinners at St Giles from 1450–51 ([same, footnote](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow48.htm)) — a Scotland-wide skinners' allegiance. Note: one BHO abstract summary renders the altar as "of St Mungo"; the full narrative source quoted above, which prints the petition's wording, says St Christopher — this dossier follows the fuller source. St Christopher, the giant ferryman who carried the Christ-child over the river, suits a craft of burdens, rivers and hides borne on backs. St Bartholomew — flayed alive, patron of skinners elsewhere in Britain — does not appear in the Glasgow evidence.
- **Feast day:** "the solemnity of the feast of their altar on the Sunday next after St. James' day" (St James = 25 July; so a high summer feast) ([BHO preface](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/xlviii-lxx)).
- **Arms (matriculated 14 February 1924):** *Per fess Azure and Ermine, a pale counter-changed, three rams salient Argent, two and one, armed and unguled Or; on a chief Or, an ancient hand-bell Sable between two chapeaux Gules turned up of the second.* Granted by Sir James Balfour Paul, Lord Lyon, on the petition of Deacon Robert Miller (architect), Collector Thomas Guthrie Brownlie (shipowner) and Clerk William James Mitchell (writer) ([grant of arms document, read in full](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Grant_of_arms_by_the_Lord_Lyon_King-of-Arms,_14th_February,_1924.jpg)).
- **Symbolism:** the **rams salient** are the source animals, leaping alive — the craft honours the beast it uses; the **ermine** quarters are the fur trade itself, fur rendered as heraldry; the **chapeaux gules turned up ermine** (crimson caps of maintenance with ermine brims) are simultaneously the furrier's product and the ancient badge of honourable dignity; and the **ancient hand-bell sable** echoes St Mungo's bell of the city's own arms — the dead-bell rung through Glasgow's streets for departed souls, tying the craft to the city and to remembrance of the dead. **[extrapolated]** (the grant document does not state readings; the bell/city association and cap symbolism follow standard Glasgow and heraldic usage).
- **Motto: "To God only be all Glory"** — already-English, no translation needed; it is the old Reformation doxology *Soli Deo Gloria* in Scots-English dress, and the same motto borne by the Skinners' Company of London ([Worshipful Company of Skinners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worshipful_Company_of_Skinners)). The craft that lines other men's honour in fur claims none for itself.

## Rituals & Customs (oaths, essays/masterpieces required of entrants, feast days, precedence, relics, insignia)

- **Admission:** no man might "carry on business as a skinner unless he was a burgess of the city and his standard of work good enough to pass the masters of the Craft" ([Trades House](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-skinners-glovers/)). Sworn masters judged the work; the council admitted the man. A formal **essay** (masterpiece) such as a dressed skin or a cut pair of gloves was the standard proof in Scottish skinner/glover crafts — **[extrapolated]** from general Scottish craft practice; the 1516 charter requires only that the entrant be "found qualified."
- **The upset:** entry money graded by blood — 5s for a freeman's son, 10s for an unfreeman's in 1516 ([Renwick & Lindsay](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow48.htm)); by 1857, £3 for a member's son or son-in-law under 25, but £50 for a stranger, who also had to be under fifty and produce a medical certificate ([1857 Rules](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/skinner_s_rules_and_regulations__1857.pdf)).
- **The Officer's servitude:** the newest member served as Officer — unpaid at first — warning members to meetings and funerals, carrying "the Box and candles," attending the Deacon in "banquets, processions, and the conveying of distinguished gentlemen in or out of town," executing poindings, and acting as a recognised **searcher of skins** in the market ([Bryce](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/list_of_skinner_s_officers_from_1568_to_1851.pdf)). Once salaried (after 1696) he was also clothed by the craft: coat, hat, shoes, stockings, and "sometimes a wig" ([same](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/list_of_skinner_s_officers_from_1568_to_1851.pdf)).
- **The Box:** the craft's chest of money and papers, with two elected "keepers of the keys of the box" ([1857 Rules](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/skinner_s_rules_and_regulations__1857.pdf)) — no one man could open it alone.
- **The quarterly courts** kept the old ritual calendar into the Victorian age: "the Candlemas, Whitsunday, Lammas, and Martinmas Courts," with each member paying two shillings of quarter-dues at Lammas ([1857 Rules](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/skinner_s_rules_and_regulations__1857.pdf)).
- **Feast and worship:** the pre-Reformation feast on the Sunday after St James' day at their altar ([BHO](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/xlviii-lxx)); today, the annual Cathedral service where "the Skinners and the other Crafts each have their pew adorned with their coats of arms" ([Trades House](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-skinners-glovers/)).
- **Precedence:** seventh in the customary order of the fourteen (Hammermen, Tailors, Cordiners, Maltmen, Weavers, Bakers, **Skinners**, Wrights, Coopers, Fleshers, Masons, Gardeners, Barbers, Bonnetmakers & Dyers — [Glasgow Trades Hall, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_Trades_Hall)) — yet first in age, a tension the craft has traded on for five centuries. The rationale of the precedence order is lost ([Trades House Digital Library](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/skinners.html) and related House sources).
- **Relics and insignia:** the original 1516 Seal of Cause, still in the Incorporation's possession ([Renwick & Lindsay](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow48.htm)); minute books from 1557 in their vellum bindings ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:History_of_The_Skinners_Furriers_and_Glovers_of_Glasgow)); a Deacon's chain worn "at appropriate dinners and functions" ([Virtual Museum, chains](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/deacons-etc-chains.html)); the coat of arms among the fourteen around the Grand Hall dome of the Trades Hall ([Virtual Museum](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/coats-of-arms-in-the-grand-hall.html)).
- **The deacon's oath** in the early Trades House was taken by show of uplifted hands, promising office "to the glory of God and the weal of the crafts" ([Records of the Trades House](https://archive.org/details/recordsoftradesh00glas)).

## Sensory World (materials, tools, sounds, smells, the physical texture of the work)

- **Materials:** sheepskins and lambskins above all (rams on the arms), plus goat, deer, rabbit, fox and imported peltry for the furriers; wool as a by-product dried on the green; lime, oak bark, alum and salt for curing. Evidence: the craft dried "wool and skins" on Skinners' Green; the district's deeds are thick with "lime-holes and bark-holes… required in the tanning of hides" ([Renwick & Lindsay, ch. XLI](https://electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow46.htm)); limeholes were "pits in which liquid prepared from lime was used to remove the hair from animal skins" ([TheGlasgowStory](https://www.theglasgowstory.com/story/?id=TGSAE)).
- **The work:** soaking hides in the burn; liming until the hair slips; scraping flesh and hair from the skin over a beam with a curved two-handled knife; tanning in bark pits for months, or tawing white leather with alum for gloves; softening by hand, staking, paring, cutting **[extrapolated** — standard skinner/glover process; Glasgow evidence gives the pits, the green and the products, not a process manual**]**.
- **Sounds and smells:** the scrape of the fleshing knife, the slap of wet hides, the Molendinar running through the yards — and above all the stink: lime, rotting flesh-side scraps, bark liquor. Their own neighbours alleged the tanneries were fouling the burn ([TheGlasgowStory](https://www.theglasgowstory.com/story/?id=TGSAE)). Against that, the finished trade was all softness: ermine trim, fur-lined chapeaux, kid gloves.
- **Places:** Skinners' Green at the Clyde-mouth of the Molendinar, in craft use by about 1503 ([Renwick & Lindsay](https://electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow46.htm)); the market where the Officer searched skins and levied the brod pennies, with the Gorbals men's stalls hard alongside ([Bryce](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/list_of_skinner_s_officers_from_1568_to_1851.pdf)); the candlelit box-openings — the Officer carried "the Box and candles to the meeting place" ([same](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/list_of_skinner_s_officers_from_1568_to_1851.pdf)).
- **Texture:** the paradox of the craft is tactile — hands cracked by lime produce the softest thing a body can wear.

## Values (5-7 core values, each with WHY rooted in evidence)

1. **Charity as constitution, not afterthought.** The founding petition literally describes the union as binding "in cherite togidder" ([Renwick & Lindsay](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow48.htm)); poor-relief provisions for members, widows and children followed the charter almost at once ([Trades House](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-skinners-glovers/)); today the craft's functions are "entirely charitable" ([same](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-skinners-glovers/)).
2. **Quiet primacy — being first without shouting.** Oldest of the fourteen (1516), yet only seventh in precedence and holding four seats to the Hammermen's six ([Trades House](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-skinners-glovers/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_Trades_Hall)). The craft's identity rests on seniority carried without ceremony — and its motto surrenders even that glory upward.
3. **Judgment of the hand.** No booth without being "found qualified" by sworn masters (1516); "false stuff" forfeited and fined in wax to the altar ([Renwick & Lindsay](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow48.htm)); the Officer as sworn "searcher of skins" in the open market ([Bryce](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/list_of_skinner_s_officers_from_1568_to_1851.pdf)). Quality was policed publicly, and bad work was paid for in candle-wax — light bought with shame.
4. **Patience with slow transformation.** Hides lay in lime-holes and bark-holes for months before they were leather ([Renwick & Lindsay](https://electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow46.htm)); the craft has kept a member roll continuously from 1516 to 1936 ([Virtual Museum](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/skinners-members-from-1516-to-1936.html)). Nothing in this craft is quick; its whole genius is waiting well.
5. **Service before standing.** Every new member served as Officer — unpaid, carrying box and candles, warning funerals — before enjoying full craft life ([Bryce](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/list_of_skinner_s_officers_from_1568_to_1851.pdf)). Rank in the Skinners began at the bottom, on your feet, in other people's service.
6. **Ordered hospitality to the outsider.** Strangers were neither barred nor freely admitted: they paid the brod penny at market, and the Gorbals men bought the right to trade alongside ([Bryce](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/list_of_skinner_s_officers_from_1568_to_1851.pdf)); a stranger could join for £50 where a son paid £3 ([1857 Rules](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/skinner_s_rules_and_regulations__1857.pdf)). The door is open — at a price that protects the family inside.
7. **Glory deflected.** "To God only be all Glory" ([1924 grant](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Grant_of_arms_by_the_Lord_Lyon_King-of-Arms,_14th_February,_1924.jpg)); the 1608 deacons' oath "to ye glorie of God and to ye weale of ye craftis" ([Records](https://archive.org/details/recordsoftradesh00glas)). The skinner's product is worn as someone else's magnificence; the craft made that anonymity a theology.

## Shadow (the craft's virtue curdled into failure)

The Skinners' shadow is **the keeper who outlives the craft**. Quiet primacy curdles into precedence-obsession: the oldest charter becomes a relic to be polished rather than a trade to be practised — by the nineteenth century the Incorporation was a fund-administering society under Court of Session rules ([1857 Rules](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/skinner_s_rules_and_regulations__1857.pdf)), and glove-making had withered inside it centuries earlier (two glovers admitted in fifty years — [TheGlasgowStory](https://www.theglasgowstory.com/story/?id=TGSBD)). Ordered hospitality curdles into gatekeeping: £3 for a son, £50 for a stranger is charity that quietly becomes bloodline privilege. The judgment of the hand curdles into monopoly enforcement — the sworn searcher poinding a poor outland packman's skins for an unpaid penny. Patience curdles into inertia; deflected glory curdles into false modesty that refuses scrutiny ("it is all for God" as a way of never answering for the state of the yard). And the tannery that served the ferryman-saint of clean crossings ended up accused of poisoning the Molendinar itself ([TheGlasgowStory](https://www.theglasgowstory.com/story/?id=TGSAE)) — the craft's deepest failure mode: doing foul work upstream and calling it someone else's water.

## Rivals & Kin (which crafts it clashed with, which it married into, demarcation disputes)

- **Kin by charter: the Furriers** — "tua craftis and unyte ourself in cherite togidder" from 1516 ([Renwick & Lindsay](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow48.htm)); associated since at least 1533 per House tradition ([Trades House](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-skinners-glovers/)). **The Glovers** were absorbed into name and monopoly, but barely into membership (two admissions in the first half of the 17th century — [TheGlasgowStory](https://www.theglasgowstory.com/story/?id=TGSBD)): a marriage of style more than numbers.
- **Frontier with the Cordiners (shoemakers-tanners).** Both crafts ran bark-pits; the cordiners' incorporation (1558) expressly included the barkers ([TheGlasgowStory](https://www.theglasgowstory.com/story/?id=TGSAE)). The natural demarcation — skinners dress skins with the hair/wool on and taw light leathers; cordiners bark-tan heavy sole leather — is the classic Scottish fault line **[extrapolated** — no specific Glasgow skinner-v-cordiner lawsuit surfaced in the sources consulted**]**.
- **Upstream suppliers: the Fleshers.** Every skin began on a flesher's booth; the two crafts were bound in a permanent buyer-seller embrace at the market, with all the friction that implies **[extrapolated** from trade structure; both sat together in the Trades House from 1605 ([Records](https://archive.org/details/recordsoftradesh00glas))**]**.
- **The Gorbals men** — the licensed rivals across the river, unfree of the burgh, who paid the Incorporation "for the privilege of setting up stalls next to the Skinners" instead of the strangers' brod penny ([Bryce](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/list_of_skinner_s_officers_from_1568_to_1851.pdf)): competition converted into rent.
- **Within the House,** the skinners appear throughout the early records as one of the steady middle crafts — joint sponsors of the 1605 act barring non-guild-brethren from office, contributors ranked with the weavers at £10 in the 1609 hospital list ([Records](https://archive.org/details/recordsoftradesh00glas)) — arbiters more often than combatants (the recorded House quarrels of the era are masons-v-wrights, with the skinners' deacon sitting in judgment).
- **Cross-country kinship:** Edinburgh's skinners shared the same patron, St Christopher, from 1450–51 ([Renwick & Lindsay footnote](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow48.htm)); London's Skinners' Company shares the ermine-and-motto tradition ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worshipful_Company_of_Skinners)).

## Lexicon (period/craft-specific words and phrases a Scots narrator could use)

All from the cited Glasgow sources unless marked *(general Scots craft usage)*.

1. **Seal of Cause** — a burgh charter erecting a craft; the Skinners' is Glasgow's first (1516).
2. **Kirkmaister** — pre-1516 craft official "having charge of altarage arrangements," sometimes acting as deacon.
3. **"Tua craftis unyte in cherite togidder"** — the founding phrase; two crafts united in charity.
4. **Dekin / dykin** — deacon, head of the craft (spelled both ways in the 1605–09 records).
5. **Upset** — the fee to set up booth as a master; 5s a freeman's son, 10s an unfreeman's.
6. **A pound of wax** — the fine for resetting another man's prentice, paid in candles to the altar.
7. **False stuff** — bad or fraudulent skins; forfeited on discovery.
8. **Brod penny / strangers' brod pennies** — the market toll on outsider skin-sellers, granted to the craft Officer from 1587.
9. **Searcher of skins** — the sworn inspector of hides in the market.
10. **Poind** — to impound a culprit's goods by decree of deacon and masters.
11. **Imbuikit prenteis** — "booked in" as apprentice, entered in the craft's book (1605 records: "Johnne Govane is imbuikit prenteis to Patrik Gemill elder, skynner").
12. **The Box** — the craft's locked chest of funds; two key-keepers; carried with candles to meetings by the Officer.
13. **Quarter dues** — the member's quarterly payment; collected at the Candlemas, Whitsunday, Lammas and Martinmas Courts.
14. **Lime-holes and bark-holes** — the pits of the tanning yards along the Molendinar.
15. **Skinners' Green** — the drying ground for wool and skins where the burn meets the Clyde.
16. **Peltry** *(general Scots/trade usage)* — furs and skins as merchandise.
17. **Tawing** *(general craft usage)* — dressing white glove-leather with alum rather than bark.
18. **Chapeau turned up ermine** — the fur-brimmed cap of maintenance on the craft's arms.
19. **Merk** — two-thirds of a pound Scots; Deacon Cauldwell's hospital pledge was "twentie merkis."
20. **"To the glorie of God and the weale of the craftis"** — the deacons' sworn formula, 1608.

## Question Seeds (moral dilemmas rooted in THIS craft's specific lore)

1. **The false-stuff fire.** You are a sworn searcher of skins. On your own brother's booth you find false stuff — badly limed skins that will crack within a winter, dressed to look sound. The 1516 charter is plain: forfeit the goods, fine him in wax candles that will burn in front of the whole craft at the altar. He has five bairns and a bad year. Do you seize, or pass by his stall a little quickly? — *Choosing seizure reveals* ***judgment of the hand*** *(truth of the work above blood); choosing to pass reveals charity curdling toward nepotism — the Shadow.*
2. **The packman's penny.** A starving outland packman spreads lambskins at the edge of the market without paying the strangers' brod penny — the very penny that pays your craft's Officer and, through the box, its widows. The law lets you poind every skin he owns. The men of Gorbals, who pay handsomely for their stalls, are watching to see if the rules bind everyone. What do you do? — *Enforcing reveals* ***ordered hospitality*** *(the door has a price so the house can stand); waiving reveals* ***charity as constitution*** *— and tests whether you'll pay his penny yourself from your own pouch, the third road the craft's founders would have recognised.*
3. **The archbishop's seal.** It is May 1516. Armed men besieged the Archbishop's castle three months ago; his party may not survive the year. He offers your two weak crafts the first seal of cause in Glasgow's history — if you bind yourselves publicly to his authority now. Wait for calmer times and another craft may be chartered first, forever. Take the parchment from a hand that may fall? — *Taking it reveals* ***quiet primacy*** *(the future of the brethren outweighs the risk of the patron); refusing reveals purity of allegiance over the craft's survival — noble, and possibly fatal.*
4. **The book flayed for a binding.** The Reformation has emptied the Cathedral's altars — including St Christopher's, which your weekly pennies dressed for forty years. A leaf of a beautiful mass-book, written on fine vellum about 1450, comes to your hand. Your minute book needs a cover, and vellum is your own trade's stuff. Do you cut the holy page to bind your accounts, or bury what can no longer be prayed from? — *Cutting reveals* ***patience with slow transformation*** *(everything, even the sacred past, is material for what must endure); refusing reveals* ***glory deflected*** *— some things belong to God only, not to use.*
5. **The burn that carried the saint.** Your yard's lime-holes drain to the Molendinar; the town alleges the tanneries are killing the burn and the salmon below. Moving the pits beyond the ports would ruin three of the poorer masters this year — masters whose widows the box would then keep for thirty. As deacon, do you defend the yards or order them moved? — *Ordering the move reveals stewardship — the ferryman-saint's craft refusing to foul the crossing; defending the yards reveals the Shadow: doing foul work upstream and calling it someone else's water.*
6. **The Officer's wig.** You are newest of the craft, and so its servant: carrying the box and candles, warning funerals in the rain, unpaid, forbidden even to keep an apprentice for two years. A wealthy stranger offers £50 to enter the craft — and hints at more for you personally if you'll carry his petition warmly to the Master Court. His money would swell the widows' fund; his haste would leapfrog the sons who waited. — *Carrying it coldly and honestly reveals* ***service before standing*** *(the servant of the craft cannot be bought); refusing him outright reveals the guard-dog virtue; carrying it warmly reveals the gate becoming a market.*
