# Incorporation of Fleshers

## Essence (the craft's soul in one paragraph)

The Fleshers are the craft that stands between the living beast and the fed city — bloody to the elbow so that everyone else may eat with clean hands. Their charter is not about artistry but about *trust under appetite*: honest scales, unblown mutton, bull beef marked as bull beef, offal off the High Street. Born post-Reformation with no altar and no saint, they answered instead to the market bell and the Kirk clock, and their 1935 Clerk gave them their unofficial creed: "Men must eat though Empires fall" — the Incorporation "pursued its fleshing unperturbed while the guns rumble" ([A Note upon the History of the Incorporation of Fleshers, The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)). Nothing of the beast is wasted — hide, tallow, dung, even the market's blood had a price — and nothing of the fraternity is wasted either: the one obscure charity clause in their charter became, in the Clerk's words, "the key-stone of the arch" when the trade monopoly died. Steady, loyal, litigious, unsentimental, and quietly kind: a craft with a strong stomach and a warm heart, in that order.

## History (founding, seal of cause date, key episodes)

- **6 October 1580 — Seal of Cause.** Upon the petition of eleven fleshers — Matthew Watson, John Watson elder, Thomas Herriot, John Brown, Robert Brown, John Dawson, John Friebairn, James Muir, William Muir, Andrew Carmichael and John Wann — "the Charter of the Fleshers' Craft of Glasgow passed the Common Seal of the Burgh" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)). The original deed is lost; all that survives in the Council record of that day is one line: "The Fleschouris lettir of dekynheid was selit witht the commoun sele" ([British History Online, Glasgow charters, abstract no. 368](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/pp28-55)).
- **Purpose.** The charter's stated grievance was that "through the great increase and multitude of people, the said Craft of Fleshers … was unfunded, abused and heavily damnified in default of good rule." It provided for a Deacon and four Masters, meat inspection, punishment of selling malpractices, market dues on unfreemen, rules on tallow and skins, and "against the throwing out of bags, paunches and tripes upon the High Street" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf); [Trades House of Glasgow — Fleshers](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-fleshers/)).
- **1605 — the Letter of Guildry / Decreet Arbitral** settled the dispute between Merchants and Trades "in true brotherly fashion" and created the Trades House; the Fleshers took their place among the fourteen incorporated crafts, electing four members annually to the House ([Trades House of Glasgow](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-fleshers/); [Wikipedia — Trades House of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trades_House_of_Glasgow)). In the crafts' order of precedence the Fleshers stand **tenth of fourteen** (the Hammermen won the premier place at the Court of Session in 1777; the logic of the sequence "has been lost in time") ([Hammermen of Glasgow — Early history](https://hammermenofglasgow.org/our-history/); [Trades House Digital Library — Incorporations](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/incorporations.html)).
- **17th century.** The Trades House records (ed. Harry Lumsden, 1910) show the Fleshers contributing to the crafts' hospital, the burgh minister, the college (university) building fund, the town's plea against Dumbarton, and "expenses of the riding to parliament" ([Records of the Trades House of Glasgow, A.D. 1605–1678](https://archive.org/details/recordsoftradesh00glas)).
- **1744–1755.** The Magistrates erected a public slaughterhouse (1744) and covered flesh markets in King Street (1755), which the fleshers used for fees and duties ([Fleshers of Glasgow v. Magistrates of Glasgow, SCOS Archive](https://archives.law.virginia.edu/scos/explore/cases/fleshers-glasgow-v-magistrates-glasgow)).
- **15 June 1802 — the Incorporation beats the Council in court.** When the Magistrates unilaterally raised beef and mutton market dues by a third (1799), the Fleshers sued and won: "Magistrates of a royal burgh have no right to impose a tax upon the inhabitants without the consent of the Legislature" ([SCOS Archive](https://archives.law.virginia.edu/scos/explore/cases/fleshers-glasgow-v-magistrates-glasgow)).
- **1802 — the Fleshers' school.** The market's "dung money" was applied to a dominie who taught members' children at reduced fees in the Incorporation's own hall in King Street ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **1846.** The trading monopoly of the Fleshers, with those of all Scottish incorporations, was abolished ([Trades House of Glasgow](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-fleshers/)). The charity clause — "help and relief of their poor decayed brethern" — became the craft's whole purpose.
- **1870 — the "guid-ganging plea."** Operative (trade) members sued to keep school funds for their own children against the "pendicles" (non-trade members); the Court of Session sided with the pendicles and "affirmed the unity of the Craft. All were 'Jock Tamson's bairns'" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **1870s.** The building of St Enoch Station forced the railway's purchase of the Incorporation's King Street property, including the old hall — "a loss from the point of view sentiment, but the golden salve set the Incorporation on its feet financially" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **25 February 1924.** The Incorporation's arms were matriculated with the Lord Lyon ([Trades House Digital Library — Incorporations](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/incorporations.html)).
- **1980 — the '400 Club'** founded for the craft's 400th anniversary; today the Incorporation runs "a substantial fund for the benefit of needy pensioners" and keeps its links with the meat industry ([Trades House of Glasgow](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-fleshers/)).

## Legends & Episodes

- **The lost charter and the one surviving line.** The founding deed itself vanished; the craft's birth certificate is a single sentence in the Council minutes — a guild whose origin is a rumor in someone else's book ([BHO abstract no. 368](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-charters/1175-1649/no1/pp28-55)).
- **Censured for killing during the sermon (1583).** "The Fleshers were censured for killing flesh in time of the Preaching on the Week days" — the town's hunger set against the Kirk's stillness. In 1619 the Session added: "No Fleshers to slay flesh between light and light on Sundays" ([Kirk Session annals printed in M'Ure/Glasghu Facies, 1872 ed.](https://archive.org/details/glasghufaciesav00murgoog)).
- **Blowing mutton.** Burgh statute: "na breiding of flesche nor blawing of muttoun be, vnder the pane of xls." — inflating a carcass with air to make it look plump was the craft's signature fraud, banned by name. Likewise "scoring of mutton" (only "ane skore on ilk schulder") and exposing "bull beef" unmarked ([M'Ure/Glasghu Facies](https://archive.org/details/glasghufaciesav00murgoog); [The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **The feet left hanging on the hides.** The tanners complained the fleshers "keip the feit hinging at the hyd" until after sale — the feet made a hide "appeir to be ane thrie part weight moir nor it is," then in cutting them off the fleshers took "ane large piece aff the hyd away at everie foot" — "ane double deception." The Council ordained the feet cut "immediately after the killing of the bestiall" ([burgh records printed in Glasghu Facies](https://archive.org/details/glasghufaciesav00murgoog)).
- **The deacon put in ward (13 May 1667).** Andrew Watsoune, flesher, a freeman's son, complained the Deacon refused to admit him. The Trades House ordered the Deacon and his masters to produce "thair lre of deaconhead and yr book" — they had refused to render accounts "thes thrie zeirs bygaine" and "flatlie denyed" in judgment, so "they wer all charged to remaine in ward" until they obeyed ([Records of the Trades House of Glasgow](https://archive.org/details/recordsoftradesh00glas)).
- **The dynasties.** The deacons' roll from 1604 reads like a saga of three clans: Watsons rule the early 1600s (the first deacon was a Watson, and two of the original eleven), Browns/Brooms the mid-century, and Gilmours dominate the 18th century for decades at a stretch ([Past Deacons from 1604, Trades House Virtual Museum](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/incorporation_of_fleshers_~_2018.pdf); [The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **Bonnie Prince Charlie camps on the Fleshers' grazing (25 Dec 1745 – 3 Jan 1746).** Provost Haugh on Glasgow Green became "Fleshers' Haugh" because members of the Incorporation rented it to pasture their cattle. On that same haugh the Young Chevalier reviewed his ragged Highland army and squeezed hostile Whig Glasgow for clothes and shoes ([Glasghu Facies](https://archive.org/details/glasghufaciesav00murgoog); [Wikipedia — Glasgow Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_Green)).
- **The loyalty fund.** During the American War of Independence the Incorporation raised money to help suppress "this 'un-natural rebellion against the King's authority'" — Tory and Royalist to the bone, "never abating loyalty to the King although not standing for any encroachment upon the liberty of the subject" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **The Deacon on a hired charger.** When the Circuit Judges entered Glasgow the Deacon rode in the civic procession "mounted upon a charger, hired for the occasion, or possibly diverted pro tem its accustomed task of drawing a delivery van" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **The dung-and-blood fight in the House of Lords.** When the City's Markets Bill proposed to annex the market's dung and blood — which were the Incorporation's revenue — the Fleshers appealed to the House of Lords, which "declined to pass the Markets Bill without provision for the redemption of the Incorporation's rights at a fair price" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **Dominie Mitchell and the destroyed books.** The schoolmaster ("passing rich on forty pounds a year") was set to excerpt the important minutes — after which the disordered early record books "were then, unfortunately, destroyed." The craft's memory survives only through its school ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **The wandering bell.** The craft's great bell, presented in 1820 — Deacon's bell or school bell — was lost for decades, then "found recently by a Glasgow citizen in an antique shop in Canada" and restored to the Clerk's office on a stand given by Deacon Simpson ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **Rangers FC is born on the Fleshers' field (May 1872).** Four teenagers played the new club's first match against Callander (0–0) on Fleshers' Haugh, and Rangers played there three years before moving to Burnbank ([Football's Square Mile — Flesher's Haugh](https://www.footballssquaremile.com/fleshershaugh); [Wikipedia — Glasgow Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_Green)).
- **Deacon Milne's watch and ward.** In the Great War, Deacon William Milne — burgess and holder of burgage land in Old Wynd — answered the medieval duty of "watch and ward" as a special constable, though the feudal obligation had long been abolished: "the spirit of the man rose above any legal quibble" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).

## Patron, Arms & Motto

- **Arms.** As carved and painted around the Grand Hall dome of the Trades Hall (and matriculated with the Lord Lyon on **25 February 1924**): a blue shield bearing **three bulls' heads** in silver (two above, one below), **two pole-axes crossed in saltire** between them, and on a silver chief a **red boar's head** ([photograph, Coats of Arms in the Grand Hall, Trades House Virtual Museum](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/coats-of-arms-in-the-grand-hall.html); matriculation date from [Trades House Digital Library — Incorporations](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/incorporations.html)). I have not found the formal blazon text online; the description above is from the Grand Hall carving itself.
- **Symbolism.** The pole-axe is the slaughterman's tool of the merciful single blow; the bulls' heads are the beasts themselves, the boar's head the festive board — the whole shield is the animal, the instrument and the feast. The device belongs to the same heraldic family as the London Worshipful Company of Butchers (pole-axes in saltire, bulls' heads, boar's head on a chief) and the Freemen Fleshers of Edinburgh, whose 2017 grant is "two pole-axes saltireways Or between three bulls' heads couped … on a chief … a boar's head couped between two garbs" ([Heraldry Wiki — Freemen Fleshers of Edinburgh](https://www.heraldry-wiki.com/heraldrywiki/index.php?title=Freemen_Fleshers_of_Edinburgh); [DrawShield — Butchers' Company](https://drawshield.net/reference/public-arms/b/butchers.html)).
- **Motto.** No motto for the Glasgow Fleshers could be verified in any source consulted; the Grand Hall carving shows no motto scroll. Kin crafts have them — Aberdeen's Fleshers use "Virtute Vivo" (I live by virtue) with a lamb crest ([Doric Columns — Aberdeen Fleshers](https://doriccolumns.wordpress.com/7-incorporated-trades/incorporated-trades-arms/fleshers-1534/)), and London's Butchers "Omnia subjecisti sub pedibus, oves et boves" (Thou hast put all things under his feet, all sheep and oxen — Psalm 8). Treat any Glasgow motto as **unconfirmed**.
- **Patron saint.** None documented. The Fleshers sealed their cause in 1580, twenty years *after* the Reformation — unlike the older Glasgow crafts they never kept an altar in the Cathedral, and their earliest discipline is Kirk Session discipline (sermon-time and Sabbath slaughter bans). A craft born without a saint, policed by the sermon bell. [extrapolated: the inference "no altar because post-Reformation" is mine; the absence of any patron in the sources is a fact.]

## Rituals & Customs

- **Entry.** Apprenticeship was "the method of recruitment contemplated": each freeman might take **one apprentice in seven years**; the apprentice was "buikit" (booked) in the records, served his "sewin zeiris" by indenture, took out a burgess ticket and paid **£5 upset** to the craft; every master paid 20 shillings per apprentice "to the upholding of the said Craft … and the poor decayed brethern thereof" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf); apprentice bookings throughout the [Trades House records](https://archive.org/details/recordsoftradesh00glas)).
- **Sons at half price.** Craftsmen's sons entered at "half upsett" — the ancestor of the modern reduced "Near Hand" entry money ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)). No "essay" (masterpiece) requirement is recorded for this craft in the sources consulted — the test of a flesher was the wholesomeness of what he sold, enforced by inspection. [extrapolated: absence of an essay requirement is an inference from silence.]
- **The Deacon's rounds.** The Deacon inspected the market stalls like an "orderly officer," booking members for faults; the craft "had up" culprits for "banning and swearing," for "saucing and speaking 'evil words' concerning the Deacon," and apprentices "abandoned enough to indulge in cardplaying on the Green during the hours of divine service" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **Fines and poinding.** The Deacon's authority was made effective "by fines recoverable by poinding by the officer of the Craft along with an officer of the Town," and disputes went to "the arbitration of the Deacon and Masters" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **The oath.** Office-bearers honoured their "declaration de fideli as use is" — the promise of faithful administration ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **Widows of grace.** "From the very first the Incorporation was liberal enough to admit widows of deceased members to trading privileges and to allow them to occupy their husbands' stalls in the market … their admission to trade was of grace, not of right" — hence women's names on the earliest lists ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **The mort cloth.** Old grants paid for "the hire of a 'mort cloth' or shroud to cover the dead for decent burial where the cost of a coffin could not be afforded" — survived into the modern quarter's-pension-after-death custom ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **Offices.** Deacon and four Masters (later eight, four named by the Deacon, four by the Craft); the Collector began as an avoided drudge-treasurer and rose to rank "next to the Deacon … the stepping stone to the diaconate"; there was always a wage-paid officer with "a suit of decent black clothes every year and occasionally a new wig" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **Feast and election.** Deacons' Choosing Day in September, when the Clerk reads the Trades House Order of 1920 at the AGM; the modern craft keeps a Deacon's Choosing Dinner and Dance, elects 4 members to the Trades House, and runs pensioner theatre outings through its service group ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf); [Trades House of Glasgow — Fleshers](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-fleshers/)).
- **Relics.** The 1820 bell (returned from Canada) on its presentation stand in the Clerk's office ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)); an 1888–89 gilt deacon's medal survives in the trade ([Dart Silver listing](https://dartsilverltd.co.uk/product/large-incorporation-fleshers-glasgow-gilt-medal-case-1888-89/), page not fully accessible).

## Sensory World

- **Steel and bone:** the pole-axe's single crack; cleavers and the "flesher's knives" of butcher heraldry; the score of the knife "on ilk schulder" of a mutton carcass; saw and steel and block.
- **The market:** the flesche mercat at the Cross, later the covered King Street market (1755); stalls under inspection; "tuging & touking" — the tugging and hawking the Council had to rebuke ([Glasghu Facies](https://archive.org/details/glasghufaciesav00murgoog)); the market bell; the Deacon's bell.
- **The smell:** a witness deposed that the Slaughter-House by the Molendinar Burn "occasions a nauseous Smell; and the Deponent has found that nauseous smell himself when walking in the Green of Glasgow" ([deposition printed in Glasghu Facies](https://archive.org/details/glasghufaciesav00murgoog)). Blood on the causeway — "na ky be bocht on the hie gait for bluid lying thairon."
- **The by-products economy:** tallow by the stane for the candlemakers; hides "haill and nocht carledderit" for the tanners; sheepskins for the skinners; dung sold from the market — "dung money" that paid a schoolmaster; paunches and tripes that must NOT land on the High Street.
- **The beasts:** cattle ("marttis") driven in by unfreemen paying dues; the Incorporation's own cattle grazing Fleshers' Haugh by the Clyde; "Cattle and Fleshers' Dogs" on the Green's paths ([Glasghu Facies](https://archive.org/details/glasghufaciesav00murgoog)).
- **The howff:** the Black Bull Inn, "a favourite howff of the Fleshers," where Burns, when in Glasgow, "may well have foregathered with the Deacon" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).

## Values (5-7 core values, each with WHY rooted in evidence)

1. **Honest goods, honest scales.** The whole charter is anti-fraud: bans on "blawing of muttoun," unmarked "bull beef," diminished hides, feet left hanging to cheat the weigh-beam. The craft existed to make the flesh trade *trustworthy* ([Glasghu Facies statutes](https://archive.org/details/glasghufaciesav00murgoog)).
2. **Duty to feed, come what may.** "Men must eat though Empires fall"; the craft "kept a calm sough and carried on with its task while thrones shook. Its Minutes reveal no panic in times of crisis" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
3. **Nothing wasted.** Hide, skin, tallow, dung, blood — every by-product had a destination and a price, down to school fees paid out of dung money and a House of Lords fight over market dung and blood ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
4. **The fraternity feeds its own.** The "one little clause" on poor and decayed brethren became "the key-stone of the arch": widows given stalls of grace, mort cloths for the unburied poor, grants to "a poor sailor despoiled by the Turks," pensions that continue today ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf); [Trades House](https://www.tradeshouse.org.uk/crafts-fleshers/)).
5. **Lawful stubbornness.** They fight — but through courts, not riots: against the Magistrates' dues (won, 1802), against the Markets Bill (won, in the Lords), the 1870 pendicle plea. "The litigious character of the Scot asserted itself" ([SCOS Archive](https://archives.law.virginia.edu/scos/explore/cases/fleshers-glasgow-v-magistrates-glasgow); [The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
6. **Loyalty to constituted authority.** Royalist through the Stuart troubles, funders against the American "un-natural rebellion," the Deacon on his charger for the King's judges, watch-and-ward honoured centuries after it lapsed ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
7. **One fraternity, no castes.** When operatives tried to shut pendicles out of the school, the craft's own court settled it: "All were 'Jock Tamson's bairns'" — and the Clerk boasts the craft "has had far fewer 'rows' throughout its whole history than the average kirk in a single decade" ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).

## Shadow (the craft's virtue curdled into failure)

- **Honesty's twin is the perfected cheat.** The men who wrote the rules against fraud knew every fraud: blown mutton, scored carcasses hiding flaws, the "double deception" of the feet on the hides. The inspector's eye and the con-man's eye are the same eye, differently employed.
- **Duty-to-feed curdles into profiteering.** The monopoly that guaranteed wholesome meat also guaranteed that no one else might sell it; "much dispute and litigation" followed when the town outgrew the privilege ([The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **Thrift curdles into squeezing blood from the market — literally.** A craft that will take a Markets Bill to the House of Lords over dung and blood money can forget the city downwind of its slaughterhouse, walking the Green through the "nauseous smell."
- **Loyalty curdles into stonewalling.** The 1667 Deacon preferred ward (jail) to opening his books; deference to rank nearly smothered a freeman's son's plain right ([Trades House records](https://archive.org/details/recordsoftradesh00glas)).
- **Fraternity curdles into dynasty.** Watsons, then Brooms, then Gilmours holding the deaconship for generations — the band of brothers shading into a closed shop of cousins ([Past Deacons from 1604](https://www.tradeshousemuseum.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/incorporation_of_fleshers_~_2018.pdf)).
- **The strong stomach curdles into callousness.** The trade's necessary unsentimentality — beasts into meat, daily — can become the inability to be moved by anything at all.

## Rivals & Kin

- **Tanners (rivals).** The feet-on-the-hides "double deception" was a formal supplication by the tanners against the fleshers, decided against the fleshers ([Glasghu Facies](https://archive.org/details/glasghufaciesav00murgoog)).
- **Skinners (rivals & kin).** The Council rebuked "the wrong done by the fleshers by tuging & touking, & cutting the sheip skinnes"; statute demanded hides and skins be delivered "haill and nocht carledderit nor diminischit" — the skinner's raw material was entirely in the flesher's hands, a permanent demarcation friction ([Glasghu Facies](https://archive.org/details/glasghufaciesav00murgoog)). In Edinburgh the same seam produced an outright Skinners-vs-Fleshers quarrel ([Incorporated Trades of Edinburgh](https://edinburghtrades.co.uk/trades/fleshers/) — site intermittently unreachable; noted from search results).
- **Candlemakers (dependents).** Tallow price and quantity were regulated at the fleshers' end; candlemakers too weak to incorporate had to affiliate to a "like" craft — the Skinners or the Fleshers ([Glasghu Facies statutes](https://archive.org/details/glasghufaciesav00murgoog); affiliation noted in [John Gray Centre — Haddington Candlemakers](https://www.johngraycentre.org/places/1-places/towns-villages/haddington/haddingtons-incorporations/haddington-candlemakers/), an East Lothian parallel).
- **The Magistrates and the Merchants House (structural adversaries).** From the 1605 Decreet Arbitral settling Trades-vs-Merchants rights, to the 1799–1802 dues fight, to the Markets Bill — the Fleshers' great enemies were never other crafts so much as the civic power itself ([SCOS Archive](https://archives.law.virginia.edu/scos/explore/cases/fleshers-glasgow-v-magistrates-glasgow)).
- **Unfreemen and "outten tounes" fleshers (the excluded).** Country butchers paid dues, had to present flesh "with hyd, skin, and talloun," and their meat was to be "cut up and retailed by the freemen of the Craft" ([Glasghu Facies](https://archive.org/details/glasghufaciesav00murgoog); [The Clerk, 1935](http://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/a_note_upon_the_history_of_the_fleshers.pdf)).
- **Kin crafts elsewhere.** Edinburgh's Freemen Fleshers (arms granted 2017), Aberdeen's Fleshers (lamb crest, "Virtute Vivo"), Dundee's Fleshers among the Nine Trades, London's Worshipful Company of Butchers — one heraldic and moral family: pole-axes, bulls' heads, and the feeding of towns ([Heraldry Wiki](https://www.heraldry-wiki.com/heraldrywiki/index.php?title=Freemen_Fleshers_of_Edinburgh); [Doric Columns](https://doriccolumns.wordpress.com/7-incorporated-trades/incorporated-trades-arms/fleshers-1534/)).
- **Within the House.** Tenth of fourteen in precedence; four seats in the Trades House; contributors alongside all crafts to hospital, kirk, college and the town's pleas ([Records of the Trades House](https://archive.org/details/recordsoftradesh00glas)).

## Lexicon

1. **Flesher / fleschour** — a butcher; the craft's own name for itself.
2. **Lettir of dekynheid** — letter of deaconheid; the seal of cause creating the craft's right to a deacon.
3. **Blawing of muttoun** — inflating a carcass with air to fake plumpness; the forbidden art.
4. **Bull beef** — tough meat of the bull, which must be marked as such or the seller fined.
5. **Scoring of mutton** — knife-scores on a carcass; only "ane skore on ilk schulder" allowed.
6. **Packing and peiling with unfreemen** — illicit trading partnership with men outside the freedom.
7. **Talloun / tallow** — rendered fat, sold by the stane to candlemakers; export controlled "quhill fastrenisewin" (until Fastern's Eve).
8. **Hyd and skynnis, haill and nocht carledderit** — hides and skins, whole and not damaged.
9. **Marttis** — cattle fattened for slaughter (hence Martinmas marts).
10. **The flesche mercat** — the flesh market; also *mercat dues*, the tolls on unfreemen's beasts.
11. **Buikit** — "booked": entered in the craft's book as an apprentice, bound for "sewin zeiris."
12. **Upsett / half upsett** — the entry fee to set up as master; half for a freeman's son ("Near Hand" money today).
13. **Pendicle** — a non-operative member, "something which hangs on to, like the pendulum to the clock."
14. **Poinding** — seizure of goods to recover the Deacon's fines.
15. **Haugh** — river meadow; the Fleshers' Haugh, the craft's cattle-grazing on Glasgow Green.
16. **Howff** — a favoured tavern; the Fleshers' was the Black Bull Inn.
17. **Mort cloth** — the hired funeral pall for a poor brother's decent burial.
18. **Guid-ganging plea** — a "good-going" lawsuit, long-running and relished.
19. **Jock Tamson's bairns** — all members alike, no caste between trade and pendicle.
20. **Keep a calm sough** — stay steady, breathe even, while thrones shake.

## Question Seeds (Dostoyevsky-grade dilemmas rooted in this craft's lore)

1. **The blown carcass.** Trade is bad, your bairns are thin, and every stall beside yours blows air under the skin of its mutton so it gleams fat and full. The Deacon's rounds are Tuesday. You can sell honest lean meat no one buys, cheat like the rest, or report your neighbours — men whose widows you'd be bound to feed. *Choosing honest-and-poor or informer-over-cheat reveals* **honest goods, honest scales**; choosing to cheat "because all do" reveals the shadow of the perfected cheat.
2. **The feet on the hide.** The trick is not even a lie — custom says the feet go with the hide, and the tanner should weigh better. A legal cunning that pays a third more, against a fair cut that pays less. Where is the line between sharp practice and fraud, when the court has not yet drawn it? *Refusing the trick before any law compels you reveals* **fair dealing beyond the letter** — the craft's deepest test, since its whole charter is the drawing of that line.
3. **The Deacon's books (1667).** You sit on the Master Court. The Deacon — your patron, who made you — has refused a freeman's son his entry and will not show the accounts these three years. The Trades House demands the books; the Deacon would rather lie in ward than open them. Stand with him and rot the craft, or break him and break the loyalty that holds the fraternity together? *Forcing the books open reveals* **accountability above rank**; shielding him reveals loyalty curdled into stonewalling.
4. **Killing in sermon-time (1583).** The Kirk censures you for slaughtering while the town prays; but the flesh must hang and cool before Saturday's market or bairns eat tainted meat. Obey the Session and sell worse food, or serve the body and defy the soul's appointed hour? *Keeping the knives moving reveals* **duty to feed, come what may**; laying them down reveals piety and conformity — and the quiz should not pretend either is safe.
5. **The rebel army on your haugh (Christmas 1745).** The Prince's starving Highlanders camp on the meadow where your cattle graze, in a town that hates them. You are a King's-man craft; but they are hungry men, and beasts are beasts and meat is meat. Feed the rebellion at a price, feed it free out of mercy, or drive your herd away and let soldiers starve? *Selling at the fair market rate — no more for their desperation, no less for their treason — reveals* **the steady scale under empire's fall**; refusing reveals loyalty; giving freely reveals mercy over allegiance.
6. **Jock Tamson's bairns (1870).** The dung money is the operatives' dung — their beasts made it, their trade earned it — and it schools their children. The pendicles paid only fees, never bled a beast in their lives, and now claim equal shares for their bairns. Blood-of-the-trade, or unity of the fraternity? *Choosing the pendicles' side, as the Court of Session did, reveals* **one fraternity, no castes**; choosing the operatives reveals the dynasty-shadow — the craft as a closed shop of cousins.
