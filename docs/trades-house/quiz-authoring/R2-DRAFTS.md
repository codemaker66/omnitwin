## Q-A · "The Half-Done Thing" — form F11 (Deathbed Charge)
**Guards: Masons–Weavers (cos 0.79), primary axis A1 Lasting/Living.**

> *The narrator, low:* "Auld Rab worked beside ye thirty years, and now he's dying,
> and he grips yer hand like a man gripping a ledge. Two things he leaves ye, and
> breath for one sentence: the great work o' his life, half-done where he left it —
> and the apprentice lad, half-taught at the door. 'See it finished,' he says. And
> then he says nothing, ever again. Ye cannae do both well. Choose."

| Option (copy) | Cost named in copy | Axis vector | Quietly serves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Finish the work itself** — to his ain design, exact, though it takes ye ten years. It will stand when every soul who knew him is gone. | ten years of yer own life | `A1+2 A4−1 A5+1` | Masons |
| **Finish the lad instead** — teach him till HIS hands can finish it. Slower. And the work will change in his hands, for nae two hands are the same. | the work will not be Rab's | `A3+1 A4−2` | Weavers |
| **Sell the half-done thing** — and put the price in his widow's hand before winter. Rab's dead; she isnae. | the great work dies | `A2−2 A3−1` | Bakers/Maltmen |
| **Leave it exactly as his hands left it** — cover it, keep it, let the half-done thing BE his memorial. Finished is not the only kind of true. | it will never serve a living soul | `A1+1 A5−2` | Coopers/Skinners |

Four kinds of good (L4): completion · transmission · provision · custody. No option
is the clever one (L5). The dying man's charge is slightly wrong (F11 shape 3): he
asked one sentence of a two-sentence problem.

**Guards: Hammermen–Tailors (cos 0.77), primary axis A3 Bench/Hearth.**

> waiting on yer mouth."

| Option (copy) | Cost named in copy | Axis vector | Quietly serves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Judge the thing.** It is true work and it passes — the measure cannae bend to a man's mood, or it is no measure at all. | the buyer leaves grieving | `A1+1 A2+2 A3+2` | Hammermen |

## Q-C · "One Armful" — form F9 (The Fire) — proposed OPENER
