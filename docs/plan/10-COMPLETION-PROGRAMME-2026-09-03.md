## 1. What completion means

The brief's maximum ambition has one sentence at its centre: a client opens Venviewer, sees the real Trades Hall, plans a real event in it with real furniture, books it, and the venue runs that event from the product. Everything else in the brief is a way of making that sentence true at the highest standard. So completion is a set of measurable conditions, not a feature count:

1. **The rooms are ours and verified.** Every room served is an owned reconstruction with a rights record, a registration row, a measured certification tier, a per-device runtime tier, human-scale behaviour (floor, doorways, collision, eye height) and a fixed-viewpoint comparison that beats or matches the vendor build. Today: eight vendor builds served, three of them closed, one registry row, zero training runs.
2. **The Floor is complete.** Altitude, Ghosts, Timeline, Live Numbers, Command and Truth Mode, the six primitives of docs/plan/01, all shipped and passing the launch gates of docs/plan/02. Today: Timeline (as the Run of Show) and walk mode shipped; the other four not started; two doctrine conflicts open.
3. **One Real Saturday.** A real Trades Hall event carried from enquiry to hold to ink to layout to sheet to pack to the morning after, entirely inside the product, with actuals recorded and read back. Today: zero events operated end to end; the hallkeeper sheet ignores the Diary.
4. **The front door earns bookings.** One homepage, the Arrival, a real enquiry send, a provable room-and-date answer, a shareable layout with its own card, an FOH client register. Today: five homepages, no enquiry control on the served one, the Arrival on a branch.
5. **The platform is a product.** Clerk live and verified, error and uptime monitoring, tested backups, CI green on Linux, deploys that migrate themselves, pricing and payment wired, legal pages reviewed. Today: none of those verified; CI red on the security audit; pricing a draft.
6. **The Foundry exists.** An owned, scanner-agnostic reconstruction pipeline with rights and identity gates, capture certification, an operator companion, and the neural digital set as a typed composition. Today: the bridge and the trainer package exist; the trainer has never run; contracts exist for twenty-one families and zero assets have crossed the chain.
7. **The frontier is opened in order.** Event Cinema, presence, multi-venue, the Concierge, and the sixteen frontier items of docs/plan/08, each after its prerequisite.

The programme below reaches all seven, in the order the dependencies allow, with a gate after each phase that a number or an artefact proves.

## 3. The engine: how the work will run

- **One programme, seven phases, gates between them.** A phase closes when its gate artefact exists, never on documentation alone (the brief's §33 rule). Phases overlap where their dependencies allow; the agent works the critical path first and fills idle compute with the next phase's independent slices.
- **The state rite replaces the ledger as the instrument.** Every session regenerates `docs/state/STATE.md` from probes (production versions, migration ledger, branch distances, served rooms, training runs) and the session logs; the ledger becomes an index of intent. Phase 0 builds the generator.
- **Compute.** The laptop does development, tests and anything under an hour. Everything heavier runs on RunPod against the `omnitwin-foundry` network volume: CUDA COLMAP, training, comparisons, decimation. Pods stop when idle; every run writes a row to `state/training_runs.jsonl` with cost.
- **Verification chain before "done".** Typecheck, unit tests, the relevant e2e or preflight, a reviewer pass for anything load-bearing, then commit and push. Windows-green is not Linux-green; CI decides.
- **What the agent decides alone.** Everything reversible inside the repo and the volume. **What waits for you.** Money, accounts, keys, legal words, rights, doctrine conflicts, anything the Blake Clause names, and anything outward-facing (deploys of new behaviour, public copy, the front door swap).
- **Cadence.** A short written status each session in the session log; a weekly one-page state for you; a decision list that never grows silently.

## 6. Risks, and the one that matters most

- **Rights.** The repo's own licence matrix contradicts the verbal grants; every own-training result is rights-pending until the record exists. The programme treats the record as phase 0, not paperwork.
- **The trainer has never run anywhere.** Seven named runtime blockers stand between the package and a bundle; phase 1.2 is the riskiest engineering in the plan and is scheduled first.
- **The ledger as instrument.** It understates delivery and overstates blockage; the state rite fixes that before planning off it again.
- **The 50 GB commit ceiling on this laptop.** Everything heavy moves to RunPod; a larger pagefile on D: helps the rest.
- **Scope gravity.** Twenty-one Foundry contract families and 263 open rows can absorb any amount of effort; the gates above are what stops that, and each phase closes on its gate or is cut, never on a document.

The one that matters most: **a real event has never gone through the product.** Every lane above improves a thing that has not yet been used for its purpose. Phase 3.4 is placed as early as the dependencies allow because the First Real Saturday will reorder every priority after it, and that is the point.
