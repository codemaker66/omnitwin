# The fused twin: one hall from every source, beautiful, at 60 frames a second

**Date:** 2026-09-03 · **Author:** the engineering agent, for Blake · **Instruction:** "not just a splat but a super-combined-something-new of all data coming together to make something beautiful, splats, e57 files, panoramic images, videos, pictures, ai generations, all the research tools I provided ... beautiful and functional without any lag ... 60fps when moving around the viewer" · **Basis:** six research readers on 2026-09-03 (the twenty-two owner-named tools traced to primary sources with licences read; the fusion, runtime, repair and registration literature to September 2026; the viewer code as it stands), with every claim not confirmed from a fetched page marked as such · **Sits under:** docs/plan/10 (the programme), as the technical content of its phases 1 and 5

## 1. The verdict

It is achievable this year on our data, and it has an honest shape.

**Lag and beauty are two different problems with two different fixes, and only one of them is research.** The 15 fps under drag is not caused by the splat's quality or by how it was trained. (Written that afternoon, the next sentence blamed six million splats sorted every frame; measured that evening, the cause was twelve renderer hosts in one scene, and one host runs the full six million at 176 fps. §2 carries the measurement and what shipped; the paragraph below is kept as written so the correction is visible.) It is caused by rendering all six million splats of the Grand Hall on every camera move, sorted from scratch each time, at full device pixel ratio. Spark 2.x, the renderer we already use, exists to cap the rendered set to a per-device budget with a level-of-detail tree and to sort on an interval. That work is a week in the web package, needs no new licence and no training, and every measured number in the field says it takes us from 15 to the region of 60 on the laptop and 30 to 60 on phones. Nothing on the owner's list of twenty-two tools makes the viewer faster except PlayCanvas splat-transform, which prepares tiers; the super-resolution tools make it slower because they add Gaussians.

**Beauty comes from fusing the sources we already own into one training, in a specific order.** The literature for indoor scenes is consistent: LiDAR initialisation and dense depth supervision beat image-only training on flat, dark and repetitive surfaces (our plaster walls, floor and dome); the highest-resolution colour we hold is the 148 native 8K Matterport panoramas, which cost only a crop script to add as training views once registered; the fisheyes should train natively through 3DGUT rather than through virtual pinholes; and colour consistency across four physically different cameras plus a Matterport ISP needs a physical model (PPISP) rather than per-frame patches. Generative repair belongs last, only where no camera looked, and always labelled.

**What must be given up.** A single native representation that ingests panoramas as panoramas does not exist in mainline tooling; panoramas enter as pinhole crops. Native fisheye training and the panorama crops live in the same trainer (gsplat), but PPISP and the faster fisheye path need gsplat's main branch rather than the 1.5.3 release, so the pod is rebuilt once. The 60 fps target on thin laptops above about two million on-screen splats needs GPU sorting that our WebGL2 renderer does not have; the budget ladder is how we live inside that limit until a WebGPU path (three.js r186's native splats, or PlayCanvas's compute sort) matures. And nothing generated is ever the measured hall: the product shows generated content only in its imagination mode, with a badge, and never in anything a hallkeeper or a client relies on.

## 2. Track A: no lag (the viewer, this week)

**Measured the same evening (docs/reports/splat-drag-budget-2026-09-03.md), and the cause was not the one written here that afternoon.** The walk mounted a Spark renderer host on every one of its twelve tiles. Each frame therefore sorted, read back and uploaded the whole room twelve times over; the profile showed `texSubImage2D` holding the main thread for 1.9 s of a 4 s drag. That, not the splat count, was the 14 fps: with one host the same six million Gaussians drag at 176 fps (p95 12.4 ms, heap 434 MB against 1.5 GB) on the RTX 4090 laptop, and the sort interval, the motion pixel ratio and the tail radius each measured as nothing at all. The level-of-detail tree is real and works (with twelve hosts it still lifted the drag to 35.6 fps at 2.5 M, 56.8 at 1.5 M, 75.7 at 1.0 M, 125 at 0.5 M; with one host it holds the 240 Hz display ceiling at any budget from 1.0 M to 2.5 M), but its place is protection for weaker devices, not the fix for this one.

What shipped that evening (commit recorded in the report): the one-host fix; Spark 2.1; a per-tier runtime profile (`src/lib/splat-runtime-profile.ts`) with the tree on, a resting budget above every room's leaf count so a still view is complete, and a motion budget applied only while the camera reports movement (high 2.5 M, medium 500 K, low 300 K, poster 150 K, the last three extrapolated and marked so); the harness `scripts/splat-drag-budget.mjs` with DEV-only query overrides so any device can be measured without a source edit. The tree costs about seven seconds of load on the Grand Hall until the prebuilt `.rad` trees ship (built with `build-lod`, loaded WITHOUT the `lod` flag, which otherwise rebuilds the tree it already has; 6.1 s load measured).

Still to do in this track, in order:

1. ~~Prebuilt trees in the staged bundle~~ Built the same evening (T-575) and measured: `lcc2 lod` builds chunked trees beside the tiles, the manifest carries them, the scene can load them `paged: true` without the `lod` flag, and the Grand Hall then loads in 1.9 s against 12.6 s for a browser-built tree; but the wire says 348 MB against 102 MB for the tiles (the compact encoding changes nothing), so prebuilt trees win only above about 250 Mbps and stay an opt-in. The shipped default: high tier runs the tiles with no tree at all (176 fps at full detail, one host); weaker tiers build the tree in the browser. Next lever for weak devices: serve the vendor's own coarser level per tier, which the bundle already holds.
2. Measure the other three tiers on real devices (one integrated-GPU laptop, one iPhone, one mid Android) with the harness, and replace the extrapolated budgets.
3. Audit every other mount for the per-tile host: the captures page had it too (fixed the same evening); the fresh walk, the living hall and the visual page already mount one.
4. The structural mesh (LCC2 LiDAR mesh, or the Matterport mesh once registered) as an invisible depth occluder so planner furniture and the splat interpenetrate correctly.
5. PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS of each motion budget against the full set at the four fixed viewpoints; the readback screenshots in the report show the name boards legible at rest and mottled at 1.0 M in motion, which is the trade the motion budget makes on purpose.

The ceiling to know about: with a GPU sort (PlayCanvas WebGPU) six million splats run at 140 fps on an M4 Max where WebGL2 gives 87, and one million on an iPhone 13 Pro Max at 78 where WebGL2 gives 38. On a thin laptop even a GPU sort leaves 5.8 M splats at 16 fps, because rasterisation, not sorting, is the wall above two million. Budgets matter either way; the WebGPU lane is tracked, not built now.

## 3. Track B: beauty (the fusion recipe, on the H100, as A/Bs against tonight's run)

Tonight's run is the baseline: gsplat 1.5.3, MCMC, 3DGUT on virtual pinhole views, sparse depth loss, bilateral grid, cap 3 M. Every step below is an A/B against it, scored on ten held-out panoramas and the four fixed viewpoints, and each one that wins stays.

1. **One frame for every source.** Keep the LCC2 SLAM frame and the 15,044-frame COLMAP model (1.36 px). Register the Matterport E57 cloud to the LCC2 LiDAR cloud with KISS-Matcher (MIT, pip) and refine with ICP: one rigid transform places all 149 sweep centres and the 148 panoramas. Refine each sweep by localising six 2048 px cube faces (or twelve overlapping crops) of the 8K panorama into the COLMAP model with hloc and LightGlue (DISK or ALIKED, not SuperPoint on plain plaster), then a rig-constrained adjustment with the sweep as one rigid cube. If plaster walls defeat feature matching, GLUEMAP (BSD, CVPR 2026) with a Pi3 or VGGT backbone is the fallback. Verification is the fixed-view reprojection and the LiDAR-to-splat distance we already compute.
2. **Initialisation.** The union of the 2.49 M COLMAP points and the LCC2 LiDAR cloud colourised and voxel-downsampled to one or two centimetres. Every source found reports LiDAR initialisation beating structure-from-motion initialisation indoors, consistently with fewer floaters on texture-less walls.
3. **Cameras.** The two 4000×3000 pinholes as pinhole-with-distortion. The two 200-degree kb4 fisheyes switch from five virtual pinholes each to native OPENCV_FISHEYE under 3DGUT with a 160-degree validity mask: a 2026 study on real 180-plus-degree fisheyes found 3DGUT stable at 200 degrees and a 160-degree crop best overall. The panorama crops join as pinholes. Everything stays on gsplat MCMC and 3DGUT, the only densification 3DGUT supports.
4. **Geometry supervision.** Replace the sparse depth loss with a dense edge-aware log-L1 depth term (DN-Splatter's shape, about a hundred lines in the trainer) fed by depth maps rendered from the LCC2 mesh and cloud and from the registered E57 sweeps into every camera model including the fisheyes, plus a normal term on planar regions. Where LiDAR is missing (the dome, the high ceiling), Depth Anything 3 (Apache-2.0 base and metric weights) scale-aligned to LiDAR fills the gap.
5. **Colour.** PPISP (NVIDIA, Apache-2.0, on gsplat main) instead of the bilateral grid: per-frame exposure and white balance, per-camera vignetting and response, five camera groups (two pinholes, two fisheyes, Matterport), and a distilled controller so the exported PLY needs no network at view time. This is the one step that needs the pod rebuilt on a pinned gsplat main commit, with the T-514 preflight re-run against it.
6. **Export and serve.** PLY to SOG through splat-transform, then `build-lod` to `.rad` (Track A). Hold-out scoring on every A/B.
7. **Repair, only if under-observed regions remain.** First NVIDIA Fixer (the Difix3D+ method, Apache-2.0 code, Cosmos base) in the loop every few thousand steps on pseudo-views between training and held-out cameras: low hallucination risk, cleans floaters and smear a metre or two off the capture path; every fixed pixel gated by the LiDAR mesh (reject where rendered depth disagrees by more than ten centimetres). Then ArtiFixer3D+ with the 1.3B checkpoint (code Apache-2.0, weights research-only, fits one 80 GB GPU) aimed only at the dome interior, the coffers seen from far below, the gallery undersides and behind the columns: its opacity-mixing design yields a native mask of where it was free to invent, and we ship two layers, measured and generated, with the generated one mounted only in imagination mode with the badge.
8. **Second-round A/Bs.** 3D Gaussian Flats (planar Gaussians on detected flat regions, licence to verify) for the texture-less walls; SplatSuRe's weight-map idea (not its trainer) to sharpen only far-only regions such as the dome; CL-Splats (MIT on gsplat) for the day a room is recaptured after redecoration; GR3EN (Google, Apache-2.0, 48 GB) later as a product feature, evening lighting states for the hall derived from the capture rather than a synthetic render.

## 5. Licence position, stated plainly

Everything on the critical path is MIT or Apache-2.0: Spark, splat-transform, SuperSplat, SPZ, gsplat, 3DGUT, PPISP, KISS-Matcher, hloc, LightGlue, Depth Anything 3 base weights, Fixer's code, ArtiFixer's code, GR3EN. The research-only chains (Difix3D's weights, ArtiFixer's weights, everything built on the Inria rasteriser) are acceptable under the owner's stated stance, with one flag the readers raised and I pass on: those licences say "research or evaluation purposes only", which is narrower than "no revenue"; a live venue site may sit outside it even at zero income. The plan keeps such tools off the measured layer and out of anything operational, so the question only bites if generated imagery ever ships publicly; that is the owner's call, recorded here, not decided here.

## 6. Traps

- The frame-rate work must be re-run after any repair step: pseudo-view supervision grows the Gaussian count.
- Generated content never merges into the measured layer without the LiDAR gate and a provenance flag (D-012); the product must be able to say what is measured and what is imagined.
- Panorama registration on featureless plaster is the step most likely to fail quietly; verify with reprojection and LiDAR distance, never by eye alone.
- PPISP and the faster fisheye path need gsplat main; pin the commit, re-run the preflight, keep 1.5.3 as the fallback.
- iOS Safari kills tabs well below a gigabyte; the Mobile tier's paged pool must stay under about a hundred megabytes.
- The 6.0 M vendor leaf set is the interim Ultra source; the own-trained cap replaces it only after it wins at the fixed viewpoints.

## 8. The first week

Day 1: Track A measurements A and B on the vendor leaf set; Spark 2.1 bump; sort interval and DPR governor. Day 2: one `.rad` per room, `lod: true, paged: true`, the tier table from the measured curve; the structural mesh as occluder. Day 3: KISS-Matcher registration of the E57 cloud to the LiDAR cloud; panorama cube-face localisation into the COLMAP model; verification. Day 4: LiDAR initialisation and the dense depth term; A/B against the baseline. Day 5: native fisheye A/B; PPISP on a gsplat-main pod. Then the trained per-tier caps, and only then repair. Each step lands as a bundle, a row in the training log, and a fixed-viewpoint comparison.
