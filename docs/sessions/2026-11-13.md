**Order and limits.**

1. L0 first, because every other lane cites its rows.
2. Then at most three lanes at once: L1, L2 and L4 first. L3 starts when one of them is waiting on Blake. L5 runs
   when a slot is free, and before piece C is designed.
3. Designs do not collide; builds do. Only one piece that changes the database lands at a time, and the others
   catch up after it. A lane changes its own services, routes and screens. It touches the shared schema file and
   the migration journal only inside its own merge slot, and a migration's number is fixed at merge.
4. Every pull request: its tested head goes to the merge queue and to the release lead's review; nothing is
   pushed after the head is sent; no lane merges its own work.
5. A piece is finished when its "done when" in the design has been watched on real work, not when it merges.
6. Each fortnight the lead reports to Blake how many wave-1 jobs became live.

**What every piece's design contains.** Intent and why; what exists today, read from origin/master with file and
line; the approaches considered and the one chosen; the change (tables, routes, screens) and what it leaves
alone; the rule in the database and the check that proves it; tests first, including the failure paths, tenancy
and concurrent writes; the migration, its backfill and its rollback; what only Blake decides; and the "done when"
from the design, unchanged. In plain words.

**Design approvals never proceed by default.** No lane writes product code before Blake has approved its design.

### The durable rulings

Numbered as in the build's ledger. Each is binding on the engine and the parts after it.

- **R30, as amended by R48 (28 October; amended 13 November): render each set-down in a Worker as its drop starts.** The Worker transfers the Float32 channels back, and nothing renders on the main thread. On master, contact comes 50 ms after the drop starts for a carried heavy piece (stage, AV and lighting), 67–69 ms for light and table pieces, and 83 ms for a drop-in. The review measured 7.5–23.9 ms medians on the build desktop with the Grand Hall's 18 reflections, so a phone at 3–5 times that misses contact for heavy pieces. When a heavy piece is picked up, its variants for the surfaces under it are rendered ahead on idle, and at contact the exact render plays if it is ready within the 20 ms heard window, otherwise a variant. Render-at-drop is confirmed only by part 2's phone timings, a gate before the plan's Tasks 12–14.
- **R42 (28 October): levels by loudness, peaks to −6 dBFS.** Levelling each set-down against the Chiavari anchor is the level rule. Peaks sit between −32 and −6 dBFS on the pre-pan mono source before the master. The look-ahead limiter stays only as a safety above −6 dBFS. The 8, 4 and 3 dB windows stand.
- **R45 (12 November): the levelling metric is momentary loudness.** That is BS.1770-style momentary loudness: the maximum over the render of the K-weighted mean square in a 400 ms window, with a 10 ms hop, ungated. The 10 log10(v) term uses each play's own drawn speed, so plays vary naturally inside the 3 dB rule.
- **R46 (13 November): the tail's frame scales with the sample rate.** The STFT frame is 2048 × 2^round(log2(rate ÷ 48 kHz)), so bins stay about 23 Hz wide, and the tail's cap is a length in seconds (1.2 × the longest band's reverberation time), not bytes. Nothing changes at 44.1 and 48 kHz. At 96 kHz the tails' 125 and 250 Hz bands had decayed 12–23 % off their targets.
- **R47 (13 November): the slab's contact is the timber face's.** Timber on a slab takes the timber face's Hertz modulus across the grain, as on the oak boards: a 1.8 mm contact's stress lies within about 5 mm of a 20 mm board, so the slab cannot enter it. The slab is heard in its missing joist-and-void thump and its stiffer boards.
- **R48 (13 November):** see R30 above.
- **R49 (13 November): the rooms' data.**
  - The Robert Adam Room's two ceilings are plaster on lath. The survey puts the room under the Grand Hall's joisted floor and the Saloon's timber subfloor, so each ceiling has a void above it.
  - The plaster-on-lath row is Adrian James's "plaster on lath, deep air space": 0.20 / 0.15 / 0.10 / 0.05 / 0.05 / 0.05 from 125 Hz to 4 kHz.
  - The Grand Hall's dome takes that row as an estimate that says no source gives its construction.
  - Survey figures are labelled measured or EST with their basis.
  - The dado band loses only each opening's part of it, and the room constant includes the air's 4mV.
  - Then the rooms are retraced with the same rays and seeds, and refitted.
- **R50 (13 November): what the tracer proves, and its provenance.** Each room is traced as a box with the model's volume and surface areas (the Grand Hall with a spherical cap of the scanned dome's volume). The check tests Eyring's diffuse-field assumption, not the data or the outlines. The model is fitted on empty and held within 15 % of the tracer half and fully laid. The trace records the sha256 of `raytrace.py` (line ends read as LF) beside that of `rooms.json`, and a test compares it with the committed file. The Grand Hall's range-check exception is a named list in the test, not a README heading.
- **R51 (13 November): every room gets a plausibility row** against a measured comparable room, with its source and state (empty, furnished or occupied), the model in the matching state, a stated tolerance and a verdict. Rows whose states match are tested. Where they cannot be matched, the gap is recorded in numbers and left to Blake's ear test, named.
- **R52 (13 November): the tests.**
  - Float32 renders are compared byte for byte.
  - The 15 ms render timing moves to the environment-gated figures writer, which also writes per-piece medians dry and with the Grand Hall's 18 reflections.
  - A 44.1 kHz case is added.
  - Two other tests are added: one fails if a surface's high cut or hardness stops reaching the render, and one tightens the tail-bias test to the measured bias.
  - Test names say what they assert.
- **R53 (13 November): three nits taken.**
  - The rattle draws its noise from its own stream, so a seed's mode draws do not depend on the rate.
  - The direct gain is clamped to 0–1.
  - The tail tests read band decays from Blackman-Harris inner-bin energy.
