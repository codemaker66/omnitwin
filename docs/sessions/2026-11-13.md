## Venviewer 2027: the second edition of the replacement map, and how its build is run (T-665, T-702 to T-720)

On 13 November Blake asked for a plan to "completely replace salesforce and cvent", on a platform where "the user

(16 October, 17 to 18 October, 11 November, 13 November), so the answer is now filed where every session can find it.

### What was made

- **The design:** [2026-11-13-platform-plan-design.md](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-13-platform-plan-design.md).
  It is the second edition of the design Blake approved on 18 October
  ([2026-10-18-replacement-map-design.md](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-18-replacement-map-design.md)). The October
  design stays in force; section 11 of the new one lists every change.
- **How it was made.**
  - Five readers re-traced every job in the October inventory against master `4cac3380` and added what had shipped

### How the build is run

One session leads the programme and builds nothing itself. Each lane is its own session in its own worktree cut
from origin/master.

| Lane | Piece | Task | Its first deliverable must settle |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | Docs and ledger | T-665 | This log, the design filed, one task row per piece |
| L1 | B, the spine | T-704 | The links from proposals, deals and worked enquiries to their event; who may see an event, recorded on the event, with a test that fails if one client sees another's; the backfill and its review list; the stage worked out on read, never stored; when an enquiry becomes an event; event merge; the money ledger |
| L2 | 0, the plumbing | T-702 | A job list in the database that is safe with two servers; sends written in the same transaction as the work; a sender and verified domain for each venue; mail coming in, with a reply address for each event; encrypted storage for each venue's keys; the providers and what they cost |
| L3 | A, the Diary holds the whole book | T-703 | An import staging area (raw rows kept, where each came from, a dry-run report, a mapping review); regular hirers' series with exceptions; rooms sold together; set-up and breakdown; lapsing holds |
| L4 | Planners, as soon as possible | T-719 | What a planner can do alone and free for an event at any venue, with a mock-up |
| L5 | The shape | T-720 | A mock-up of Today and one event page, and the first-click test cards |

**Order and limits.**

1. L0 first, because every other lane cites its rows.
2. Then at most three lanes at once: L1, L2 and L4 first. L3 starts when one of them is waiting on Blake. L5 runs
   when a slot is free, and before piece C is designed.
3. Designs do not collide; builds do. Only one piece that changes the database lands at a time, and the others
   catch up after it. A lane changes its own services, routes and screens. It touches the shared schema file and
   the migration journal only inside its own merge slot, and a migration's number is fixed at merge.
4. Every pull request: its tested head goes to the merge queue and to the release lead's review; nothing is
   pushed after the head is sent; no lane merges its own work.
5. A piece is finished when its "done when" in the design has been watched on real work, not when it merges.
6. Each fortnight the lead reports to Blake how many wave-1 jobs became live.

**What every piece's design contains.** Intent and why; what exists today, read from origin/master with file and
line; the approaches considered and the one chosen; the change (tables, routes, screens) and what it leaves
alone; the rule in the database and the check that proves it; tests first, including the failure paths, tenancy
and concurrent writes; the migration, its backfill and its rollback; what only Blake decides; and the "done when"
from the design, unchanged. In plain words.

**Design approvals never proceed by default.** No lane writes product code before Blake has approved its design.
