## People-privacy-5: erasure and the 24-month pass (#247, lane L1)

The privacy page promises erasure on request and the deletion of unbooked enquiries and contact records 24 months
after the last contact. #247 does both through one erasure (`services/people-erasure.ts`); the operator's page is
[erasure requests](../operations/erasure-requests.md).

### Decisions recorded

- **A contract or a deposit keeps the person (review finding B2).** A contract prepared or signed, or a deposit
  requested, on a proposal of theirs refuses the erasure with `PERSON_HAS_CONTRACT`. Contracts cannot be changed
  at all (migration 0104), and both are kept six years with the booking's records, as the privacy page's
  accounting rule says.
- **What is someone else's stays theirs.** An address another live person at the venue holds identifies nobody;
  a deal made from the person's enquiry that another live person leads is that person's; what the person wrote on
  someone else's proposal stays for that client, without their name. Only the erased person's full name and
  addresses leave such records.
- **Full names only.** A first or family name alone, and a full name another live person at the venue goes by,
  stay in kept text; the erasure page lists them under what stays by hand.

### Left by hand

- **The six-year pass.** People kept for a booking or a contract are listed by id (`kept` in the pass's result and
  in `POST /admin/retention/people`). Once the booking's financial year is six years past, the venue erases them by
  hand (erasure page, sections 2 to 4).
- The stores the erasure page names: accounts, `quotes`, `follow_up_tasks`, receipts not about an enquiry, and
  enquiry notices another enquiry could equally have written.

### The 2028 backlog

The People Book's backfill falls due together in 2028. A night acts on at most 100 people and 200 lone enquiries,
looks at no more than 500 of each and starts no step after 150 s (the cleanup job stops at five minutes), so the
backlog clears over several nights; the log says how many were left each night. After a restore from backup, the
recovery runbook repeats `POST /admin/cleanup` until nothing is deferred.

### Evidence

Disposable PostgreSQL only (a private cluster with every migration applied); see the PR for the runs and the
mutation checks.

## Field web vitals (#309, T-685): the release lead's round at c6fecbae

The release lead's review of #309 at c6fecbae held one major finding (the PR body still described the October build) and minors on privacy, the server and the front door. This round fixes them, or answers them where the decision is Blake's. Both switches stay off; #355 is stacked on #309.

### Decisions

- **A report's page is one of a list, not a shape.** `WEB_VITAL_PAGES` in `@omnitwin/types` holds every production route's pattern and `/*`; the browser sends nothing else and the API's schema accepts nothing else. `route-inventory.test.ts` holds the list equal to `router.tsx`, so a new route fails that test until the list has it. The database keeps a shape check only, which a short code spelled in letters (about 1 plan code in 6) would pass.
- **The sample rate is a decimal below 1.** Anything else, `1` included, is off: the privacy page says the browser "may" measure a page load.
- **No measurement code in a build without the rate.** The entry's condition is a build-time constant; Vercel production does not set the rate. The web `.env.example` now leaves it commented out, since any value, even `0`, keeps the code in the entry.
- **Optional chunks never reload the page.** While `loadOptional` (`route-error-boundary.tsx`) loads the reporter or its library, a script's preload failure takes no reload in the stale-chunk listener: it falls to its own import, which for a route is the route boundary (it reloads on that error) and for the reporter is its catch. A stylesheet that will not preload still reloads, since the route boundary does not know that error.
- **The daily ceiling is each process's count.** A restart or a second replica starts its own; the docs say so. A report that fails to store gives back its place, and only on the day it took it (`admit` returns the day). Reading the count from the database (so it holds across restarts) was not built.
- **The report's window** starts at a UTC midnight, counted in hours (`webVitalsWindowStart`): an interval of days follows the session's time zone and would move an hour across a change of clocks.
- **Cleanup passes are independent.** Each runs even when an earlier one fails; the job then fails with the failure, or an `AggregateError`.
- **A row keeps the UTC day** whatever the session's time zone; its index beside the primary key is on that day, and the report's window counts whole UTC days in hours, so a change of clocks cannot move it.
- **`buildServer(env, { logStream })`**: tests can read the production logger's lines; nothing else changes.
- **`WEB_VITALS_COLLECTION` is not in the API's env schema**, as `ENQUIRY_FUNNEL_BROWSER_SIGNALS` is not: a strict enum there would stop the API starting on a mistyped value, and anything but `on` is off.
- **Shuffled hourly batches** (Blake, 13 Nov, by the question form: "Shuffled hourly batches (Recommended)"). Rows written as reports arrive could be paired with the host's request log by row order, `xmin` and restore history. The route now holds reports in memory, with nothing about who sent them, and writes them hourly (`createWebVitalsBatch`): a group only once it holds ten, every row in random order, in one transaction. A group was one page, one device class, one arrival day and one set of vitals (round 4 made it one vital; below): a share page's own API call is logged with its token seconds before the report, the log's user agent tells phones from desktops, each row keeps its day, and CLS comes only from Chromium, so a lone page, phone, day or Android among iPhones would otherwise be singled out (the release lead's point and the batching self-review's major finding). A group of today under ten waits; one of an earlier day, or any at shutdown, is dropped. Each write is bounded to 15 seconds. Limits, in the doc: ten reports, not ten people (repeat loads or deliberate decoys fill a group), and a page opened fewer than ten times a day per device and browser is never measured. (Round 4 changed these: ten samples of one vital, within one process's life in a UTC day; below.)
- **#355's wording** (Blake, 13 Nov: "Apply all three (Recommended)"): the GPC/DNT sentence, a lawful basis (Article 6(1)(f)) with "no third-party analytics product", and the stored rating and day named. These are for #355's round. The answers reached this session by relay after the account switch closed its question; the first was checked in the app's own answer record, and the search for the second timed out.

### Decisions

- **B1: every value on its vital's step**, in the browser before it is sent and in the API before it is held (`WEB_VITAL_STEPS` in `@omnitwin/types`: 10 ms; CLS in hundredths after the second review, as at a thousandth a score could name a screen an in-app browser's user agent names), rated again from the stepped value with web-vitals' own bounds. A row carries nothing joining it to its report's other vitals, so its anonymity set is its group's rows in its batch; CLS still needs ten CLS samples. ### The durable rulings

Numbered as in the build's ledger. Each is binding on the engine and the parts after it.

- **R30, as amended by R48 (28 October; amended 13 November): render each set-down in a Worker as its drop starts.** The Worker transfers the Float32 channels back, and nothing renders on the main thread. On master, contact comes 50 ms after the drop starts for a carried heavy piece (stage, AV and lighting), 67–69 ms for light and table pieces, and 83 ms for a drop-in. The review measured 7.5–23.9 ms medians on the build desktop with the Grand Hall's 18 reflections, so a phone at 3–5 times that misses contact for heavy pieces. When a heavy piece is picked up, its variants for the surfaces under it are rendered ahead on idle, and at contact the exact render plays if it is ready within the 20 ms heard window, otherwise a variant. Render-at-drop is confirmed only by part 2's phone timings, a gate before the plan's Tasks 12–14.
- **R42 (28 October): levels by loudness, peaks to −6 dBFS.** Levelling each set-down against the Chiavari anchor is the level rule. Peaks sit between −32 and −6 dBFS on the pre-pan mono source before the master. The look-ahead limiter stays only as a safety above −6 dBFS. The 8, 4 and 3 dB windows stand.
- **R45 (12 November): the levelling metric is momentary loudness.** That is BS.1770-style momentary loudness: the maximum over the render of the K-weighted mean square in a 400 ms window, with a 10 ms hop, ungated. The 10 log10(v) term uses each play's own drawn speed, so plays vary naturally inside the 3 dB rule.
- **R46 (13 November): the tail's frame scales with the sample rate.** The STFT frame is 2048 × 2^round(log2(rate ÷ 48 kHz)), so bins stay about 23 Hz wide, and the tail's cap is a length in seconds (1.2 × the longest band's reverberation time), not bytes. Nothing changes at 44.1 and 48 kHz. At 96 kHz the tails' 125 and 250 Hz bands had decayed 12–23 % off their targets.
  - Corrected 16 November (#416's review, NA3; ruling R93): the exponent is rounded up, 2048 × 2^ceil(log2(rate ÷ 48 kHz)), the smallest such frame whose bins are no wider than 48 kHz's 23.4 Hz. Rounding to the nearest gave wider bins wherever log2(rate ÷ 48 kHz) has a fractional part strictly between 0 and one half: 8, 16, 32, 50, 64 and 128 kHz among the usual rates (31.25 Hz, or 24.4 Hz at 50 kHz). It read the 250 Hz decay up to 6.6 % short (the Reception Room laid for dinner at 32 kHz; 5.3 % empty). Those rates' frames now double. The frame is held to 256–16,384 points, so the rule holds up to 384 kHz. 44.1, 48, 88.2 and 96 kHz keep their frames, and their tails are byte-identical.
- **R47 (13 November): the slab's contact is the timber face's.** Timber on a slab takes the timber face's Hertz modulus across the grain, as on the oak boards: a 1.8 mm contact's stress lies within about 5 mm of a 20 mm board, so the slab cannot enter it. The slab is heard in its missing joist-and-void thump and its stiffer boards.
- **R48 (13 November):** see R30 above.

### Decisions made, with reasons

- **A lens shift cannot be a view offset here.** Temporal anti-aliasing sets and clears the camera's view offset on every frame, so the first attempt drew black. The shift lives in the camera's own projection update.
- **The picture is one proportion (2.39:1), and spare height is night.** Showing the whole floor and the whole window wall from a level camera fixes the camera's height by the picture's proportion; 2.39:1 puts it near 11 m, where the chandeliers still hang in view. - **The top bar is not a surface.** The dark above the walls would otherwise be left over; setting the words on it uses it.
