## Venviewer 2027: the second edition of the replacement map, and how its build is run (T-665, T-702 to T-720)

is never daunted", "especially venue admins and venue hallkeepers". It is the fourth time he has asked for this
(16 October, 17 to 18 October, 11 November, 13 November), so the answer is now filed where every session can find it.

### What was made

- **The design:** [2026-11-13-platform-plan-design.md](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-13-platform-plan-design.md).
  It is the second edition of the design Blake approved on 18 October
  ([2026-10-18-replacement-map-design.md](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-18-replacement-map-design.md)). The October

- **How it was made.**

  from the first call to the close of the night. Wave 1a ("the switch") is pieces 0 and A to K; wave 1b ("the

**Order and limits.**

1. L0 first, because every other lane cites its rows.
2. Then at most three lanes at once: L1, L2 and L4 first. L3 starts when one of them is waiting on Blake. L5 runs
   when a slot is free, and before piece C is designed.
3. Designs do not collide; builds do. Only one piece that changes the database lands at a time, and the others
   catch up after it. A lane changes its own services, routes and screens. It touches the shared schema file and
   the migration journal only inside its own merge slot, and a migration's number is fixed at merge.
4. Every pull request: its tested head goes to the merge queue and to the release lead's review; nothing is
   pushed after the head is sent; no lane merges its own work.
5. A piece is finished when its "done when" in the design has been watched on real work, not when it merges.
6. Each fortnight the lead reports to Blake how many wave-1 jobs became live.

**What every piece's design contains.** Intent and why; what exists today, read from origin/master with file and
line; the approaches considered and the one chosen; the change (tables, routes, screens) and what it leaves
alone; the rule in the database and the check that proves it; tests first, including the failure paths, tenancy
and concurrent writes; the migration, its backfill and its rollback; what only Blake decides; and the "done when"
from the design, unchanged. In plain words.

**Design approvals never proceed by default.** No lane writes product code before Blake has approved its design.

## The planner's new screen (T-723): 13 November

Branch `claude/planner-hud-tft`, behind `?chrome=hud`. "Seen" means in the app's browser pane at `/dev/planner-lab?chrome=hud` (the backend-free lab) unless it says otherwise.

### What happened

- **Blake asked for the planner to be redesigned** in the manner of Teamfight Tactics with "the philosophical aesthetic of the sublime and beauty", rejected five image-model looks of a first layout the same morning as "ai slop", and asked for the taste of "the best fashion houses in Paris" and an aesthetic that "clicks".
- **Two studies were commissioned** (what Kant, Burke, Alberti and the research on visual ease agree on; what the Paris houses measurably do) and joined into one doctrine: the hall is the sublime and takes the screen; the interface is small, exact and made of one rule.
- **The hall is now held as a stage** (`lib/lens-shift.ts`, `grand-hall/hall-stage.ts`, `HallStage.tsx`): a level camera square on to the window wall through a lens shifted down, so every upright is upright, the floor's near corners stand on the picture's foot corners, and the only dark is the night above the walls. A wheel, a drag or a pan lets go in one frame to an ordinary orbit showing the same picture.
- **The screen was rebuilt on it**: no top bar (the words stand on the night), one shelf with eight cards and one primary key whose label is the next move, a chosen piece marked by a thin line on the floor.
- **Blake was shown four real frames and chose the bone ground, and kept the stage view** (14:36 UTC). The other two grounds were removed.

### Decisions made, with reasons

- **A lens shift cannot be a view offset here.** Temporal anti-aliasing sets and clears the camera's view offset on every frame, so the first attempt drew black. The shift lives in the camera's own projection update.
- **The picture is one proportion (2.39:1), and spare height is night.** Showing the whole floor and the whole window wall from a level camera fixes the camera's height by the picture's proportion; 2.39:1 puts it near 11 m, where the chandeliers still hang in view. A lower camera crops the floor's near corners; a higher one looks down on a model.
- **The top bar is not a surface.** The dark above the walls would otherwise be left over; setting the words on it uses it.
- **Words that float over the room are off under this screen** (`roomCallouts`), because the shelf says each of them once; what is chosen is marked on the floor instead (`chosenMark`).

### Read whole before release (20:00 to 21:00 UTC)

CI's typecheck caught one fault first: a read-only triple given to a mesh's rotation, which react-three-fiber 8 refuses (the local typecheck had been starved of memory all afternoon). The branch was then read whole, as a reviewer reads it, before being offered for release. What that found, and what was done:

- **Putting the enquiry form away unsent marked the plan sent.** The form has one `onClose` for a cancel and for a success. It now tells its opener when the enquiry has arrived (`onSubmitted`), and "sent" is kept by the layout's id, so another layout opened on the same page is not called sent.
- **A right click un-squared the stage.** A right click with no drag puts the card in hand down, and the stage let go at any right press. It now lets go when the press becomes a drag (the planner's own 5 px) and keeps the orbit from turning the held picture until then.
- **Letting go while the camera squared up left the tilted camera's lens on a nearly level camera**, so the picture jumped by up to 30 degrees. The camera is aimed at the floor again whenever the stage lets go.
- **A right click with an empty hand opened a viewpoint's draft that nothing shows** under any chrome but the first, and an open draft holds the camera's arrow keys. The draft is now opened only where its composer is mounted. This was already so under `?chrome=v2`.
- **Plan asked for a camera beyond the orbit's reach on squarer windows** (33.8 m for the hall). Its height is capped at 32 m and its lens widens to fit.
- Sheets hand the keyboard in and out; a phone keeps the "more" glyph, which is the only way to the sound's setting; a double-click on a table selects it, as under v2. The rest are in the pull request.

### Kept behind its flag (21:00 to 22:00 UTC)

The release lead ruled, on #357, that code only a flag reaches must not grow what the default planner downloads, and asked this branch to be built to that before its review. Against master's own CI build, this branch had grown the planner's main chunk by 10.99 kB (3.71 kB gzipped) and the shared sound chunk by 6.96 kB (2.66 kB gzipped).

- **The stage's hold on the camera, its measurements and lens, and the floor mark are now one module the scene fetches only where a screen has asked for them** (`components/planner-hud/hud-scene.ts`). `HallStage` says where its camera stands through `hall-view-store`, so the views' director imports none of the stage's code. A test fails on a static import of any of the screen's modules from anywhere else.
- **The screen's sound is taken out of this pull request.** The planner's sound engine keeps its controls in a module every planner and the landing page download; nothing in production used them, so the bundler had been leaving them out, and wiring them to this screen put them back for everyone. #357 changes the same module for the same reason, so the sound (a card lifting, a set landing, the setting in More tools) follows once those controls can be fetched on their own. The removed wiring is kept, outside the repository, for that follow-up.

### Seen in a real browser (22:50 to 23:50 UTC)

Until now the screen had been seen only in the app's own browser pane. It was run in installed Chrome on WebGPU (`three-native`, backend `webgpu`) against the lab, one browser at a time under the house's rules for a 3D run, at 1920 x 1080 and seven other sizes, and every frame was read. The stage (held four seconds after the page opened), its views, a card in hand, a right click that puts it down, the sheets and the first screen were as designed. Six things were not, and each was then seen mended in two further runs, at eleven sizes with the planner's own plaque:

- **A sheet lost the keyboard in development.** React's strict mode runs an effect, its clean-up and the effect again; the title's clean-up asked for focus to go back a moment later, and that moment came after the second run. Focus now goes back only where shutting the sheet left the keyboard nowhere. The tests written for it found a second fault of the same kind: a sheet replaced from inside itself took focus from the new sheet's title.
- **Under 1024 px the bar's words were printed over one another** (at 820 px, "Send" and "Plan"). The name, four steps and the view need about 1010 px in one line, so below that the steps take a line of their own. The stage keeps the bar's whole height dark, and the bar now says how tall it is, so that height is no longer written down twice.
- **The shelf's side columns could be narrower than their words**: "Redo" fell to a second line and the primary key broke in two, between 1024 and 1111 px and between 1200 and 1255 px. A side column is never narrower than the quiet words now.
- **A card's caption ran into the next card's under 1200 px** ("Round of 10 x8" in a 72 px column). A column is never narrower than 84 px. Where nine will not fit between the side columns (under 1280 px) the shelf stands in two rows with the hand across the whole width, if the window has the height; in a short window the hand slides.
- **The plaque's line was cut in the middle of a word.** It gives way a whole item at a time.
- **A window made upright and then wide again did not get its stage back**, and while upright it showed the hall from where the stage had stood, through the wrong lens. A canvas that can no longer hold the stage now hands the camera to the rig's own opening view, which is made for any canvas; from there the stage is taken again when the canvas can hold one, unless a hand has moved the camera meanwhile.

Smaller: a focus ring on a key at the bar's edge was cut by the screen, so in the bar the ring is drawn inside its key; the guest count on the Lay out sheet showed the browser's own spin arrows.
