## 19:00 — The planner's new chrome, slice 5: one keyboard owner and one tool store (T-671)

Branch `claude/planner-chrome-5-keys`, stacked on #156 (slices 1–4) and merged with `origin/master`
f5b0acaa. PR #159. The second build session took over from slice 5 after the first stopped at slice 4.
Build ledger and evidence: `D:/claude/planner-chrome-20261020/`.

**What changed**

- `lib/planner-keys.ts` is the planner's one keyboard owner: a single window keydown listener (bubble
  phase, registered only while something uses it) for every shortcut and every Escape. Shortcuts are
  bindings with exact modifiers and a priority, so one press does one thing; mnemonic letters follow the
  character typed and Q/E and W A S D the key's position. Escape walks one stack, one layer a press: a
  tooltip on screen, a dialog that holds the keyboard, the operation in progress, the open popover, the
  open panel (focus back to its key), the selection. It never leaves Walk or the planner, and leaves an
  open native `<dialog>` its own Escape.
- The 22 handlers that listened for Escape on their own are migrated; a guard test walks the planner's
  import graph and fails on any other file that reads Escape or adds a keydown listener.
- `stores/tool-store.ts` is the only tool state (Select, Add, Lay out, Measure, Draw; the pill's hands until
  slice 8). The toolbox's own copy is gone, so the deck's Done puts the pen down and D picks it up.
- `lib/planner-keymap.ts`: Q and E turn opposite ways (Q anticlockwise, as the piece in hand always did),
  R a quarter turn, each table with its chairs; arrows nudge 10 cm, 1 m with Shift, 1 cm with Alt, one
  undo step a press; + and − seats; Ctrl G groups and only Ctrl Shift G ungroups; A (F for one release),
  M, D and V; W A S D walk in Walk only, so D no longer pans; a key pressed with the pointer gives the
  arrows back to the room.

**Changed in today's planner, on purpose:** Esc no longer leaves Walk; Esc puts the T tape away and keeps
its lines (it deleted them all); Ctrl G no longer ungroups; Q/E/R turn a table's chairs with it (they
turned each chair in place and snapped its angle, so a quarter turn left them facing sideways).

## 21:00 — The Grand Hall's walls coloured from the sharpest photograph (T-652, room fixes S4a)

Branch `claude/planner-room-fixes` (worktree `E:/claude/planner-room`), after S3d and a merge of
master (`02f6a323`). Blake's complaint: the names on the deacon boards and the portrait are blurry.
The investigation found the cause in the survey build, not the renderer: `project.py` blended every
station that saw a wall texel (median 6.2 per board texel), which doubled the lettering and carried
the varnish's glare into a haze. The walls' atlas (`walls-4096.webp`, `walls-2048.webp`) is rebuilt
from each 40 mm block's sharpest photograph; same files, sizes, runtime and glass alpha; survey
revision 5.

- **Build** (`tools/grand-hall-survey`): `label-views.py` gives every block one station: sharpness
  (Laplacian energy times panorama-pixel size), occluders and glare set aside, a seam cost halved
  along relief steps, alpha-expansion on SciPy's max-flow. `project-walls.py --labels` samples each
  texel 2 × 2 (2.5 mm) from its station, levels seams with Waechter et al.'s per-label offsets and a
  local Poisson blend, feathers them over a block; `pack-atlas.py` reduces 2.5 mm to the atlas in one
  Lanczos step and keeps the published atlas's alpha; `check-atlas.py` checks sizes, alpha and glass
  area. Inputs: the public 8K panoramas (hashes checked against the manifest) and the existing depth
  cubemaps; not the E57 cube faces.
- **Departures from the brief, with evidence.**
  - The photo-consistent depth sweep is computed and recorded but not applied: with one station per
    block it read fewer board names (26.6% against 31.6% at the relief, end wall) and lifted
    cornices' lit edges up to about 25 mm onto the attic panelling (offsets of 18-20 mm where every
    station looks up from about 54°).
  - The seam cost is not lowered by colour contrast (a seam through lettering is the worst place);
    a cost raised where relief crosses a seam was tried and changed seams by 3.5% with no gain.
  - The glass alpha is carried over from the published atlas: recomputed from the sharper
    photograph it lost 2.1% of the glass (10,830 texels, a median 28 mm in from the edge).
  - Gains in linear light instead of offsets were tried: they keep the portrait's dark canvas at its
    photograph's level but met their bound on blue at the window wall's glass. Offsets stay.
  - The membrane is solved in strips (whole-wall solve 2.1 GB; strips within one level, 1.2 GB peak).
- **Double run.** Runs A and B gave byte-identical labels, orthophotos and atlases. Run A's door
  labelling crashed once (status 139, no Windows error record); run B's passed and A's rerun matched.
