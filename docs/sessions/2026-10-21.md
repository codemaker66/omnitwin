Build ledger and evidence: `D:/claude/planner-chrome-20261020/`.

**What changed**

- `lib/planner-keys.ts` is the planner's one keyboard owner: a single window keydown listener (bubble
  phase, registered only while something uses it) for every shortcut and every Escape. Shortcuts are
  bindings with exact modifiers and a priority, so one press does one thing; mnemonic letters follow the
  character typed and Q/E and W A S D the key's position. Escape walks one stack, one layer a press: a
  tooltip on screen, a dialog that holds the keyboard, the operation in progress, the open popover, the
  open panel (focus back to its key), the selection. It never leaves Walk or the planner, and leaves an
  open native `<dialog>` its own Escape.
- The 22 handlers that listened for Escape on their own are migrated; a guard test walks the planner's
  import graph and fails on any other file that reads Escape or adds a keydown listener.
- `stores/tool-store.ts` is the only tool state (Select, Add, Lay out, Measure, Draw; the pill's hands until
  slice 8). The toolbox's own copy is gone, so the deck's Done puts the pen down and D picks it up.
- `lib/planner-keymap.ts`: Q and E turn opposite ways (Q anticlockwise, as the piece in hand always did),
  R a quarter turn, each table with its chairs; arrows nudge 10 cm, 1 m with Shift, 1 cm with Alt, one
  undo step a press; + and − seats; Ctrl G groups and only Ctrl Shift G ungroups; A (F for one release),
  M, D and V; W A S D walk in Walk only, so D no longer pans; a key pressed with the pointer gives the
  arrows back to the room.

**Changed in today's planner, on purpose:** Esc no longer leaves Walk; Esc puts the T tape away and keeps
its lines (it deleted them all); Ctrl G no longer ungroups; Q/E/R turn a table's chairs with it (they
turned each chair in place and snapped its angle, so a quarter turn left them facing sideways).
