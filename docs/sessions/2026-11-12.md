## Theatre auto-fill keeps a rear walkway (#410)

Decision, Blake, 12 November 2026 (about 11:05 UTC), verbatim: "Keep a rear walkway (Recommended)". The option read: auto-fill leaves at least 1.2 m clear behind the last row, about what the old layout left, and theatre goes back to 20 rows and 280 seats.

Why it came up: #391 moved the planner's Grand Hall to the scanned 21.135 × 10.59 m, which let `planTheatreLayout` fit a 21st row (294 seats). It used the 0.7 m wall clearance as the limit for the back row's centre, so only 0.445 m was left between the last row's chair backs and the end wall (the old 21 × 10.5 m hall left 1.21 m).

The rule (`packages/web/src/lib/theatre-layout.ts`, `planTheatreFill` in `auto-fill.ts`):

- `rearWalkwayM` (default 1.2 m, in `THEATRE_SPACING`) is measured from the back edge of the last row's chairs to the back wall: the last row's centre stops `rearWalkwayM + seatDepthM / 2` short of the wall. Side clearance (0.7 m) and the other defaults are unchanged.
- It is strict, at least 1.2 m behind whichever chair is in the last row. Rows are planned for the first chair's depth (Burgess Turini, 0.58 m); if stock gives the last row a deeper chair (the checked banquet chair, 0.62 m), the rows are planned again for that depth, on the door-clear path as well. At recorded stock this costs no row in any room; the one case found is the Reception Room with 24 to 119 usable pink chairs, which goes from 10 rows (120 seats) to 9 (108).
- The stated basis now names it: "… 2.4 m clear at the front and 1.2 m behind the last row."

## Trades Hall's front (T-681, #335): the cut's touching solids settled

Orchestration revived #335 today (Blake: "continue the tasks/sessions we were doing"). The release lead's plan: merge master, cap the cut at its plane (offsetting as the fallback), extend `hall-exterior-skin.test.ts` to the front's own solid pairs, measure again on real animation frames under the house locks, then push.

### What was wrong

Through the window wall's cut, the front's own touching solids flickered as light and dark checkers in motion (the 5 November orbit; present before #331 too). The front draws a solid's inside, seen through the cut, flat in the section's colour. Where two blocks touch, one block's face seen from inside and its neighbour's lit outer face lie on one plane at one depth, and the two take turns.

### What was done (2091d2b7)

- **Capping at the cut's plane was dropped before any frame was drawn.** Drawing each inside face at the depth where its view crosses the cut's plane is right only where the view enters that solid through the cut. From a camera above the cut it also pulls the inside of every uncut solid, and of a cut solid's outside below the cut, in front of that solid's own lit faces, so the lower front would read as cut colour. A true cap needs to know where the view enters a solid: a stencil count, or a depth pass of the front alone. The planner's scene pass has neither, and adding one is a larger change than v1.0.1 needs.
- **The cut wins its ties instead.** The front draws in two passes over one geometry:
  - the outside, as before;
  - the cut (each solid seen from inside), moved toward the eye by `CUT_EYEWARD`, 2.5e-4 of its distance, in the vertex stage.
  - A point moved straight toward the eye projects where it did, so the cut covers the same pixels and is lit at its own place.
  - Out to 30 m that is more than ten steps of the depth buffer, with the planner's 0.1 m near plane. At a solid's own edge it shows across under a quarter of a pixel.
  - Depth is never written per fragment, so the depth test still turns hidden faces away before they are shaded. The cut pass draws after the scene (`renderOrder` 1).
- **The test.** `hall-exterior-skin.test.ts` now sorts the front's own coplanar overlaps into two kinds:
  - back to back: touching solids, 6,655 pairs, whose ties the cut pass wins;
  - flush.
  - It refuses flush faces of two materials, and found 73 (table below).
