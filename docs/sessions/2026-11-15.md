# 15 November 2026

## The other rooms as surveyed (T-662): 13 to 15 November

How it stood at the end of 15 November. "Seen" means on the RTX 4090 in headless Chrome over WebGPU; "proved" means compared array for array; nothing here is published or live.

### What Blake asked and chose

- 13 November: "we were building every other room in the building in the new planner redesign way, have you completed that?" It had not been completed: the work stopped on 20 October at the tools stage, with the Saloon measured and nothing in the planner.
- 13 November, by button: **"Both, 3D first"**: the live 3D rooms as planned on 18 October, the Saloon first; after each room is in, its photographic set for the redesigned planner (T-687) from the same data.
- The 18 October decisions stand: measured sizes for drawing and planning, published capacities kept; the Robert Adam Room one room on two levels; Saloon, Reception Room, Robert Adam Room, then the galleries.

### What was done

- **The build runs from a room config** (`tools/surveyed-rooms/build-room.py`): one config per room, every path the Grand Hall never used behind a named gate (the hall opens none), and one sub-step at a time (`--list`, `--only`, `--resume`), because a whole stage held the shared machine's heavy lock for 59 minutes on 13 November.
- **Walls by master's method.** Since October the Grand Hall's walls take each part from its sharpest photograph. The room build now does the same by reusing the hall's own scripts through small seams, not a second copy (the copied port of October is what went stale). Proved on real data, the whole of the hall's end wall, master's scripts against the room build: labels, the 5 mm and 2.5 mm photographs, the portraits' varnish veil and the 2 mm image, 23 files and 49 arrays, none different.
- **A deadlock in master's label step, found and fixed.** `label-views.py` runs four band threads, each calling a matrix product through BLAS, which ran four threads of its own: on this machine (numpy 2.4.2, OpenBLAS 0.3.31) it stalled or crashed in three runs of four, once holding the heavy lock for 18 hours. A stack dump put the stall in `project.depth_lookup`. BLAS is now held to one thread there, in `build.sh` as well as in the room build; the labels are identical either way (21 arrays and the report, on a region).
- **Two faults that only a second room could show**, both fixed with tests: a wall whose rendered tiles are one texel narrower than its rounded length (gate B23), and two texels of 868,824 that the renderer placed behind its own camera at floor level, which the labels' 1 mm check refused (gate B24; they are left out and recorded). After each, the hall's results were compared again and did not move.
- **The Saloon, built twice**: on 13 November by the old blend, and on 15 November by the sharp method (12 walls, 288 detail tiles of 2 mm). Its gilt boards read line by line in the second where the first doubled and smeared them. The build's own check of the stained glass against the scan found no disagreement.
- **Two more rooms measured** into room configs, each with its numbered scripts, a README and a second check by ray casting:
  - Reception Room: an L of 16 faces, 101.75 m². Production stores a 13.4 by 11.2 m rectangle of 150 m²; the venue's own plan agrees with the scan to centimetres. Its two columns are square (0.28 m); no partition track was found; the floor rises 3 to 5 cm towards the windows. **Its window widths are a lower bound** (1.62 m): drapes hide every edge.
  - Robert Adam Room: 64 faces, 164.5 m² (lower 109.0, the steps 3.1, upper 52.5, each rounded), the upper floor 0.594 m above the lower in four risers. The venue's site gives 139.4 m²; production stores only the upper part. No vaults: flat panels between flat beams.
- **The web's room-driven path** ([#431](https://github.com/codemaker66/omnitwin/pull/431)): beside the Grand Hall's code and touching none of it, a room is drawn from the `survey.json` its build publishes. An independent review found nothing blocking and ten faults, all answered. Seen: the Saloon on a dev page (every wall strip on its wall, lettering unmirrored), and, on a stacked branch not yet in a pull request, the Saloon in the planner's own scene behind `?rooms=surveyed`, with the near wall and the ceiling stepping aside by the camera's side.

### Open

- **Ghost outlines in plain plaster above the Saloon's pediments** by the new wall method, which the blend did not have. Cause not found. It must be fixed behind a method revision, so the Grand Hall's output stays what it is, before the Saloon is published.
- The build refuses free-standing columns, a curved wall and more than one floor level, so the Reception Room and the Robert Adam Room cannot be built yet.
- The record the build writes is version 1; the web's reader takes version 2 (a room block, each wall's height and kind, the atlas's and the floor's file lists).
- The Saloon's edits are not made: two doors stood open in the scan, and a piano and a sideboard stand in it.
- The planner's opening view is too tight for a surveyed Saloon: it comes from the hand-drawn 11.9 by 6.9 m outline. The measured outline (12.19 by 7.47 m with three shallow bays) replaces it after #411, whose tests pin the hand-drawn one, with the stored outline brought along by migration.
- The galleries are not in the July capture. Their floor is at E57 Z 7.55 (T-697's section), not the 8.6 to 9.2 inferred in October.
- Not proved: the hall's other three walls and its full two-hour build end to end; the Saloon's labels and photographs were computed once.
- For Blake, at the venue: the width of one Reception Room window's glass by tape, or a photograph with the drapes drawn back.

## The hall's photographs as scanned by default (T-701, second pull request)

On 14 November Blake compared the planner at three toes for the hall's photographs (five sheets: A as now, B half given back, C all given back; the chairs, tablecloths and floor the same in all three) and picked by button: "C: as scanned (Recommended)". The option read: "I build the follow-up PR so the hall's photographs draw exactly as the scan saw them: panelling and honour boards open up, furniture untouched. Exports and thumbnails are brought along in the same PR." This pull request is that pick as the default. It is stacked on #422.

### What changed

- **The default.** `DEFAULT_FILM_TOE` is 0 (`lib/film-toe.ts`): a page that asks for nothing draws the hall's photographs with no toe. `?toe=0.04` draws the hall as it was under Neutral alone, and a value between draws the photographs with that toe.
- **No switch left.** #422 threaded a `photographToe` option through the hall's builders so that a page asking for nothing built the old materials. With the addition on by default the option could only ever be true, and its `false` default would have given any new caller the old look without a word. It is removed: the photographs' materials always carry the addition, and the address only sets how much of the toe they give back.
- **Two things matched to the photographs follow them.** Neither is drawn by the photograph materials, so #422 left both under the toe; neither was lifted in the frames Blake picked from. They follow the rule he picked.
  - The two photographed door linings (`hall-section.ts`, the wall bodies' material). In linear light the toe takes under 0.005 from them. In display levels that is not small: at the scan's own light their medians, sRGB 42/20/8 and 42/22/14, draw as 39.5/14.6/0.1 and 37.4/12.2/0.4 (worked out from the curve), beside a door surround now drawn as scanned. The addition sits in the lining's own branch of that material, so its other finishes keep the toe.
  - The windows' bars (`hall-materials.ts` `paintSurface`). Their paint is the colour the scan photographed, relit so that they match the photographed sash frames in every light. Left under the toe they would sit about 7 display levels darker than those frames (worked out from the paint's level, not measured). The outer reveals in the same material are art direction and keep the toe.
- **Only under Neutral.** For the photographs, a draw counts as the view's own frame only where Khronos PBR Neutral follows it (`lib/native-renderer.ts`). Every surface that draws the hall sets Neutral today, but nothing held it to that: with the addition on by default, a hall drawn under another curve would have had its darks washed, and no test would have failed. The curve is read before the pipeline runs, because three's `RenderPipeline.render` takes the renderer's own off while its passes draw and applies it in its last one.
- **The lab.** `/dev/planner-lab`'s `measure()` helpers draw the pipeline inside `withNativeComposedFrame`, as the frame loop does, so their frames are the view's own.
- **The test.** `hall-photograph-toe.test.ts` walks the hall's materials' node graphs for the `filmToe` uniform: the photographs and the bars read it and the drawn floor, gilt, brass and painted mahogany do not; in the windows' paint the bars do and the reveals do not; of the wall bodies' eight finishes only a photographed door lining does; the rooms past the doors do not. It cannot see inside a deferred `Fn` body, and says so.

### Exports and thumbnails: nothing to bring along

- The one picture of the 3D scene the planner exports is the plan thumbnail sent with a layout (`lib/ortho-capture.ts`). It is drawn into a render target, and three draws a render target with no tone mapping and no exposure (`Renderer.currentToneMapping`, read in r186). So the thumbnail never had the toe. It is not the view's own frame either, so the photographs add nothing in it. This is from the source; the browser look is to measure it.
- No end-to-end spec that keeps screenshots opens the planner's 3D view (they open the dashboards, the operations and share pages, the front door, and the twin with its canvas hidden), so no stored picture was recorded again.

### Left as it is, and why

- The dimmed rooms past the doors (`hall-beyond.ts`) keep the toe. Their floors are held by eye to at most 0.4 of their photograph against the hall's own floor, which is drawn, not photographed, and keeps the toe too.
- The painted mahogany, the gilt, the brass and the drawn floor are as in the frames Blake picked from.
- No mood was tuned again. Blake's sheets showed Daylight, Evening and Candlelight at each toe, so the moods are as he saw them at C.
- The captured rooms were never affected: they draw with no tone mapping (`lib/capture-display.ts`).

### An independent review, and what it changed

A second model read the diff against #422's head, three's own source beside it, and ran nothing. It found no draw path where a photograph is lifted without Neutral's toe following, or the other way about, and no fault in the guard or in the arithmetic at no toe. Its findings, all acted on: the windows' bars (above); the dead `photographToe` switch and the stale comments around it (removed); a test that could not tell a lifted lining from a lifted wall body (it now walks the body's finishes one by one); and the engineering note's claim that the addition is exact wherever nothing else touches the pixel, which is not so for a varnished photograph's reflection or for an edge resolved from several samples (the note now names both; each leaves a little of the toe and adds no light).
