### Master red: a press that lands on a gliding lane

CI on `f21d3550` failed the timetable's phone case three times out of three
(`e2e/diary-timetable.spec.ts:92`, run 36276141556), and the case passed here. The difference was the
browser. Playwright 1.59.1 runs Chrome 147 on CI; this container has only Chromium 141. Chrome for
Testing 147.0.7727.15, fetched into the scratchpad, reproduced the failure.

What happened, from instrumented runs:
- Chrome 147 flings an emulated swipe as a phone flings a finger's. The case pressed straight after
  its swipe, so the press landed while the lane was still gliding.
- The browser dispatched that touchstart uncancelable, and the first touchmove after the lift too.
- So the lane scrolled, the browser cancelled the pointer, and the lifted block dropped. Chromium 141
  never flung the emulated swipe.

Phones do the same, so on Chrome for Android a coordinator who pressed a still-gliding lane saw the
block lift and then die.

The fix:
- `lib/touch-scroll.ts` now reports, from the press's own touchstart, whether the browser will let
  the page hold it.
- A press the browser has already given to the scroll lifts nothing, whether a block or a tray slip.
  It only stops the glide, as it does natively.
- While the board is writable, the page keeps a do-nothing non-passive `touchmove` listener, because
  WebKit decides at touchstart whether to wait for the page. dnd-kit's TouchSensor ships the same
  listener for iOS Safari. That part is inferred from dnd-kit's source and is not tested on an
  iPhone.
- The timetable case waits for the lane to rest before its long press.

Evidence:
- The fixed spec passes 3 of 3 on Chrome 147, and on Chromium 141.
- An instrumented press during the glide, on Chrome 147, logs the touchstart uncancelable, no lift, the
  finger scrolling the lane, and no update.
- Removing either new check fails its unit test (hook and tray).

CI 36278184989 passed on `e43a65a4`, all four CPU shards included, and Deploy 36278887962 followed.
