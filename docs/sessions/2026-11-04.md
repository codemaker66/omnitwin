## Trades Hall's own front in the planner (T-681)

Blake, 28 Oct, on a frame of the Grand Hall seen from Glassford Street: "this outside of our building is not
accurate, there should be many examples on our computer and online to have an accurate outside facade of trade's
hall". The city's LiDAR contour model drew the hall's own front as a stair-stepped outline 1.0-1.2 m in front of
the real one.

- The front is now modelled from measurements (`hall-exterior-spec.ts`), each number tagged with its source: the
  E57 scan through the hall's windows and front door for the faces, the venue's 2010 plans for the columns' axes,
  the 2020 DSM for the end bays' steps and the dome's centre, photographs for heights and details, and the room's
  own openings for its five windows. The sources, readings and audits are outside the repo, in
  `D:/claude/th-facade-20261028/`.
- The 0.9 m disagreement between the DSM and the scan was the DSM's front line (its shadow on a front facing away
  from the survey aircraft), not the city's registration, which the E57 confirms to about 2 cm. So the city keeps
  v2's registration and the front is anchored to the room and the scan.
- City v3 (`merchant-city-v3.bin`, built by a subagent with `build_context.py --front-cut`) cuts the old front out
  to bare pavement and straightens the neighbours' street fronts; the lamp bake moves the door lanterns onto the
  modelled podium. Checked here independently: inside the front's cut only 8 ground triangles at the pavement, and
  every triangle that differs from v2 within |x| 47.3 m. 131 tests across the city, lamps, section, sun and front.
- After dusk the front takes the street lamps' light as the city's walls do (`streetLampLight`, moved out of the
  city's shader unchanged: the RC path's frames agree to a mean 0.04-0.12 of 255 away from edges, run to run).
- The front's code loads only with the front (a lazy chunk, 28,839 B, 10,885 B gzipped, at #332's head); with the
  front off the planner's route chunk grows by 1,513 B (357 B gzipped) against the RC (5c5cee13), both built in
  one mode. The city drawn round the front, with its lamps, is 2,568 B smaller than v2's (committed files).
  `/tour` on the Vite dev graph: 15,099,805 B here against 15,108,486 B on a master-based server measured back to
  back (budget 21,000,000).
- Photo-match against photograph A from its solved camera (24.12 m from the links' face; the photograph's
  24.01 m was from the true face, not the DSM's line): the render's edges a median 0.95 px (about 2.6 cm) from
  the photograph's, the skyline within 1-4 px; the photograph's edges a median 3.28 px from the render's (the
  stone's joints, the carving, the signs and the street have none in the model). Tooling in
  `tools/grand-hall-survey/front`.
- Frame time (RTX 4090, Chrome 156 WebGPU, 160 guests, pages off/on/on/off at fixed day and night times), at
  #332's head: the front changed the mean by -0.04 to +0.20 ms, within the spread of repeated runs (0.26-1.66 ms).
  Foreign CPU load was 14-41% (other sessions), so absolute times carry that; the comparison alternates to cancel
  it. The sky's WebGPU pipelines were failing to compile throughout (below), so this is measured again once they do.

- An independent review (a subagent, read-only) found eight things; checked here before acting. Fixed:
  - the shadow maps were asked to redraw before the front's meshes had mounted, so a frame between could miss
    them; the redraw now runs once they stand, as the city's does;
  - the cut faces read the uncut caster's shadow (they lie inside it), so the front's section was shaded beside
    the hall's sunlit one; the cut now takes the sun without the map, as the hall's cut tops do;
  - the ground floor's glow lit a band at knee height; it now covers the ground floor's glass up to the string
    course, the front door's fanlight included;
  - a failed fetch of the front's chunk would have taken the scene down; it now leaves the city without its front;
  - the cut's discard came before the derivatives below it, undefined under GLSL along the cut's edge; it is last;
  - the lathe's corners blended their normals, so the drum, the shafts and the pipes shaded as balls; lathes
    take an opt-in crease (`MeshBuilder.lathe`, 60° on the front; every other caller is unchanged);
  - a new renderer after a lost device rebuilt the front and leaked the old geometry; the build now depends on
    its detail level alone and each geometry is disposed when it is replaced.

  Checked and left: the ambient occlusion reading the cut as a pit. The office profile (WebGL2) has no occlusion,
  and on the desktop profile (WebGPU, occlusion on) the cut's tops at Blake's angle show no darkening.
- The lamp bake hung Trades Hall's lamps off faces that are not the modelled front's: the door lanterns from a

## T-682: the snapshots on WebGPU with the sky on

The v1.0 release lead passed on the exterior session's probe
(`D:/claude/th-facade-20261028/perf/wgsl-probe.mjs`, the first note under T-681 above): on WebGPU with `?sky=1`,
seven or eight pipelines failed with an empty `struct OutputType { }`, and the
sky drew black.

Measured on the RTX 4090 (Chrome WebGPU, D3D11), master 5c5cee13; the probes,
reports and frames are outside the repository in `D:/claude/webgpu-target-safe-mrt/`.

- With the view's own frame loop, sky=1 compiles 158 shader modules with 0 WGSL
  errors at 12:00 and at 18:00, and the sky draws. The 23-25 failures appear
  only when the planner lab's `advance()` drives the frames: R3F's advance draws
  through NativeCanvas's precompile hold, with the job's target and MRT still
  set. The lab's advance now waits for the hold (`nativePrecompileSettled`), and
  driven so the probe reports 0.
- The failure that ships is in the plan snapshot (`lib/ortho-capture.ts`, the
  image sent with a layout and with Submit for review), which draws into a
  target of its own whose one texture has no name. A material's own outputs
  (`material.mrtNode`: the panes, the street's light, the floor marks) keep
  none of their members there, three writes an empty output struct, and one
  refused pipeline voids the image: with the sky on, the plan came back blank
  at 12:00 and at 18:00. The current-view snapshot
  (`lib/native-current-view-capture.ts`, a development-only poster hook in
  RoomSplatScene), called by the probe in the planner lab, threw the same way
  at 18:00 ("Native poster readback is blank"). With the sky off both drew.
- The fix, `lib/target-safe-mrt.ts`: where a target names none of a material's
  outputs, they write their colour alone into its first texture. Where the
  scene pass has an MRT, three merges them into it as before, and the 158
  modules the view draws are byte-identical to master's (SHA-256 per module,
  12:00 and 18:00). Both snapshots also draw with the renderer's MRT cleared
  and handed back: a precompile job holds the scene pass's outputs while it
  compiles, and a snapshot taken then would build every material for outputs
  its target does not name (found in review, not seen). With the fix both
  snapshots draw with the sky on (12:00, 18:00) and off, with no failed
  pipeline (`snap/sheet-snapshots.png`). A floor mark the view shows (a door's
  clear space, circulation) now draws in the plan too; before, by three's
  source, it wrote no colour there on WebGL2 and voided the image on WebGPU.
- Tests: `lib/__tests__/target-safe-mrt.test.ts` drives r186's own
  `MRTNode.setup`: a plain `mrt` keeps 0 members in a target with no name or in
  PMREM's cube (`PMREM.cubeUv`), the target-safe one keeps 1, and a scene-pass
  target keeps all 3; every `material.mrtNode =` must call a target-safe maker,
  and only the scene pass may make plain outputs. The two snapshot tests draw
  with a job's outputs held, and `native-precompile.test.ts` settles only past a
  second job. The Grand Assembly's capture handle (`window.__assembly`) waits
  for the hold as the lab does.
- Reviewed before the PR by a workflow: four lenses (three's internals,
  coverage and behaviour, the harness and tests, cost and claims) raised 19
  findings; two or three skeptics tried to refute each of the nine above nit
  level, six survived (all minor), and each is fixed here.

## Folding the planner's tools away goes to ship (T-680)

Blake, 28 Oct: "The UI elements, I want to be able to minimise them for cleaner design. make it as aesthetic as
possible, the philosophy of the sublime as we have discussed throughout the project should be employed." He chose
"Fold any panel + clear all", "Open; remember their fold", "Shows when you reach for it" and "Crest hides; name goes
home". On 4 Nov, shown frames re-shot on master 5c5cee13, he chose "Ship it".

- Branch `claude/planner-chrome-fold` (worktree `E:/claude/planner-chrome-fold`), rebased onto master 5c5cee13.
  Spec and plan: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-28-planner-chrome-fold-design.md` and
  `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-28-planner-chrome-fold.md`. Evidence on `D:/claude/planner-chrome-fold-20261028`.
- The re-shot frames matched 29 Oct to the value and the chrome to the pixel, except frame 09: the crest's name came
  back about 400 ms after a press made in the frame the hand arrived. A probe in Chromium found why. React 18 draws
  a hand's arrival as a continuous update; the press's toggle forced a synchronous render without it, so the crest
  decided from a hand it had not yet drawn. A slower press, with the hand resting first, always worked.
- The first fix (answer the name on pointerdown) was refuted by an adversarial review: it took the name's hit area
  away mid-press, so a press on its words sent its release, and the click, past the crest. The crest now decides
  from where the events say the hand and the keyboard are (refs the handlers set), and answers the name as the tools
  go or come (a store subscription). Unit tests reproduce the race with a transition lane and fail on the old code;
  the Chromium probe's cases pass.
- Known limits, recorded in the spec: the bottom spine names nothing; a hand cutting across the venue's name loses
  the crest's name (a pointer grace area would keep it); a phone clears only with `\` until the More sheet's item
  lands (planner-qol), and the note should then say tap.
- The browser lock was shared with another lane's film runs between 22:22 and 22:29, and the PC ran at 99.7 % CPU
  across 29 Claude sessions: two probe runs timed out in Playwright's 120 s server boot. The rerun starts this
  worktree's dev server first and reuses it.
