## A pass that fails on one request costs no other its escalation or its announcement (T-768)

- The fault, measured on master's passes against a disposable PostgreSQL. Two due requests were set up, and the
  database refused the second one's inbox copy (a test trigger, as a constraint or an outage would refuse it).
  The pass threw. Afterwards the database held:
  - the first request claimed, emailed and with its inbox copy, but never announced: the pass threw before it
    returned its list, so no push and no live frame went;
  - the second claimed, never emailed, and with no inbox copy. Its claim had landed before the insert failed, so
    no later pass would take it, and nobody was told.
  - And the email pass ran after the escalation pass with no guard between them, so an email-pass failure dropped
    the escalation pass's good list too.
- The cure:
  - `runRequestEscalationPass` returns `{ escalated, failed }` instead of a list. Each request is on its own: its
    claim, its reads and its inbox copy commit together, in one transaction. A request that fails before its inbox
    copy is written stays unclaimed, so the next pass escalates it (with T-766's wait).
  - Its emails go after the commit. An email step that throws leaves `escalation_emailed_at` unset, so the email
    pass sends it later; the escalation still stands and is announced.
  - `runEscalationEmailPass` tries every row, and throws once at the end if any failed.
  - `runSweepPasses` (it was `runBothPasses`) runs the email pass whatever the escalation pass did, and gives the
    scheduler both the list and the failures.
  - The scheduler announces what escalated, and counts the wake as failed when anything failed, so the wait and the
    hourly report apply.
  - The escalation-pass endpoint announces what escalated, then answers with an error if any request failed, so a
    cron and Sentry hear of it.
- The review's "its email can be lost" is refuted: the email pass picks up any escalated request whose email has
  not gone, and the new case shows it sends it. The other two losses are as the review described, and fixed.
- Tests, each watched failing on master's passes:
  - in `requests-postgres.test.ts`, the refused inbox copy (the request left unclaimed, the other escalated and
    announced, the failure reported, the next pass taking it) and the email step that throws (announced, reported,
    sent later by the email pass);
  - in `escalation-scheduler.test.ts`, an email pass that throws loses nothing the escalation pass escalated, and a
    partly failed wake announces, reports and waits.
- Every earlier caller of the pass in the Postgres suites now asserts that the pass failed on nothing.
- From a fresh disposable database: the scheduler, request and Sentry suites pass 140 of 140, and no request is
  left claimed and unemailed afterwards. Six more suites that import the changed modules pass, 49 of 49. The
  seventh, `floor-long-names-postgres`, pins the house test cluster, which this lane did not use; it never calls
  the changed endpoint, and CI runs it.

## T-768 after its review: a request that keeps failing is held on its own floor

- The release lead's verdict at `395acdd8` confirmed the cure, and the three answers measured for it (two
  replicas, the scrubbing, the email's idempotency), but asked for one push. The review's 24-hour model of a
  request whose claim always fails: under T-766's whole-wake back-off it is tried every minute for ever (1,444
  passes a day instead of 207), every other escalation waits up to a minute against a two-minute window, the
  database never gets its five idle minutes, and the hourly alarm names no request (LN1). This PR made that loop,
  since master spent one failed pass on such a request.
- LN1. A request the pass failed on now costs the rest of the sweep nothing:
  - A failed claim holds that request back on a floor of its own: 5 s after its first failure, doubling, to an
    hour. Until then it is left out of the pass's candidates (`skip`) and out of the plan, which counts it at its
    deadline instead; then it is tried again, so nothing is dropped.
  - The wake's own back-off (T-766) stays for a pass that fails as a whole, its read of what is due.
  - Each request's alarm names it (`escalation.request`, a UUID) when its streak starts and at most hourly after.
  - The candidates are taken longest-waiting first.
  - The state is per process. A request quiet for an hour past its last chance is forgotten, and one that
    escalates, or whose email goes, is cleared.
  - An email the escalation pass failed on that the same wake's email pass then sent is no failure (LN5).
- The simulation that pins it: request A's claim always fails and request B falls due 30 s later.
  - B escalates exactly on time, and A's tries widen 5, 10, 20 … seconds to the hour.
  - The plan sleeps past five minutes; every alarm names A, at most hourly.
  - A failed candidate select still takes the wake's back-off.
- LN2, measured: two titles overflowed on a long room's name, not one (wording corrected after the next review,
  LR3).
  - The thread title, `varchar(160)`, which the review did not name, refused the raise itself for a room of about
    135 to 152 characters or more, depending on the request's kind and quantity; that was so on master too.
  - So the notification title, `varchar(180)`, was reached only by a room renamed past about 140 to 157 characters
    after a request was raised there: that request's escalation was then refused at every pass.
  - Both are now clipped by code point, as `clipName` does: the thread title at its one insert, and the
    notification title in `insertNotifications`, the request paths' one insert into that column (the column's other
    four inserts were already bounded).
  - A PG case with rooms of 160 and 190 characters raises and escalates both, with the thread's title at 160 and
    the escalation's at 180.
- LN3: a route case (the pass stubbed: one request escalated and published and pushed, then the generic 500 naming
  nothing) and a PG email-pass case (one row's send throws: the other is sent and marked, and the pass rejects
  with an `AggregateError` of one, naming that row).
- The nits went too:
  - LN5: the failure says "claim" or "email", and so does the endpoint's message.
  - LN6: T-768's row is in progress.
  - LD1: each request's alarm is its own event, carrying only its own message.

## T-768 after its second review: only a request's own fault holds it back

- The release lead's verdict at `a568476b` closed LN1 to LN6 and LD1. Its 24-hour model of a request that always
  fails, re-run on that head, gave 240 passes a day against 207 clean (it was 1,443), every other request on time,
  the clean idle gap, and 24 tagged events a day. The floors had opened three small holes, fixed in one push with
  two faults this lane found.
- LQ1: a fault of the whole sweep (writes refused, a pool timing out) failed every claim and so put every due
  request on its own floor, up to an hour; after a 30-minute fault they escalated up to 21 minutes late, with one
  Sentry event per request. Each failure is now classified by its SQLSTATE.
  - Data and integrity errors (classes 22 and 23) are the request's own, and take its floor.
  - Every other database error, and a claim error with no SQLSTATE, is the sweep's. It takes the wake's own
    back-off and its one alarm (T-766), and advances no floor.
  - A simulated half-hour fault over twenty requests now ends with every one escalated within a minute of
    recovery, and one alarm.
- LQ2: a hold outlived a state change. A held request that was taken and handed over had its relief deadline
  hidden behind the old floor. A hold is now kept to the instant the request was due at when its claim failed, and
  applies only while it still is (compared to the millisecond, as drizzle reads it: see LS3 below). Any move releases it, and a
  failure at the new instant starts a fresh streak.
- LQ3 and LR1: the Postgres case for the plan's exclusion of held requests passed without the exclusion (a mutant
  that spins at four passes a second). It now runs on instants no other row precedes and compares exactly: the plan
  is the hold's deadline, not the held window; after a take and a hand-over it is the relief deadline; and a hold
  kept to the relief deadline leaves it out of the relief query too.
- F-A, this lane's own: the email pass listed a row it tried as sent even when the send answered false and the row
  stayed unmarked, so the sweep cleared that request's streak and dropped its email failure. Only marked rows now
  count as emailed.
- F-B, found by this lane while answering the lead's question, and there since 18 October: an email still waiting
  was retried 15 minutes after each plan, and every raise and handover plans again. With raises every ten minutes
  a failed email was never retried: a probe tried it 0 times in four hours. The retry is now anchored to the email
  pass's last run (the start counts as one): 16 tries in four hours, 15 minutes apart. Found and fixed here, so no
  new row.
- The answer to the lead's question, measured: an email that always fails cannot loop hot. Its floor is the
  15-minute retry, and its alarm is hourly.
- The nits:
  - LQ5: the endpoint's comment says it shares neither the floors nor the report cadence.
  - LQ6: a held claim logs `escalation.request_held` with its due instant, an email failure logs
    `escalation.request_failed`, and one request and step count once a wake.
  - LR2: the PR description is rewritten for the head.
  - LR3: the LN2 record above is corrected.
- Each of the ten fixes, taken out in turn, turns its test red. The first try at the LQ2 scheduler mutant passed by
  coincidence (its retries every beat landed on the asserted instant), so that case now asserts every try.
- Checked on a fresh disposable database. Typecheck and lint are clean. The request and reopen suites pass 31 of 31
  twice in a row, with no request left claimed and unemailed. The 17 other suites the change touches pass 266 of

## T-768 after its third review: the email retry counts from the end of a run

- The release lead's verdict at `4140a2c2` closed every item of the round before, by run: a write-refused fault
  now recovers 15.25 s after it ends with one pass event, the hand-over relief escalates on time, and the 24-hour
  model of a request that always fails is unchanged. It asked for one small push.
- LS1: F-B anchored the email retry to the start of a run. With a provider failing slowly and several emails
  waiting, one email pass can last longer than the 15-minute retry, and the next then started a quarter of a
  second after it ended. The lead's model put that at 1.8 times the provider calls, with the database awake 94% of
  the incident against 67%. The retry now counts from the end of the run that ran the email pass. A case whose
  runs take 20 minutes each asserts that every run starts 15 minutes after the last one ended.
- The email path's connection faults, ruled in the last round and due now that a push was needed: an email
  step's error with no SQLSTATE stays the request's own, unless the error or one of its causes is a Node system
  error. That means a code such as ECONNRESET or getaddrinfo's EAI_AGAIN together with the syscall that failed,
  which makes it the sweep's. Node's own ERR_ codes match the pattern but carry no syscall, so they stay with the
  request. Measured on Node 22: a failed lookup carries getaddrinfo, a refused connection connect, a peer reset
  read, and ERR_INVALID_ARG_TYPE nothing.
- `faultOf` now says that a trigger refusing a single row should raise a class-23 code, and that any claim error
  with no SQLSTATE is the sweep's by ruling, which would cost a request T-766's pace if a JavaScript error in the
  claim came from one row's data. No such throw is reachable today.
- LS3, a correction to this lane's record: the hold's millisecond comparison is exact, but not for the reason
  first given. Drizzle 0.45.2 has both drivers return timestamptz as text and makes the Date itself with
  `new Date(text)`, so the drivers' own parsers, which this lane measured, never read these columns. What matters
  is V8's Date: 0 of 4,000,000 values (every microsecond of one second at four offsets) failed to truncate to
  their millisecond here, and the lead's run found 0 wrong in 12,000,000.
- LS6, not this PR's: while the email provider is slow, an escalation waits behind the passes' one-at-a-time
  sends, so a request raised then can escalate many minutes late. It is named in the PR and left for its own row.
- Each new fix, taken out in turn, turns its test red: the end anchor, the connection rule, its syscall guard and
  the underscore that admits EAI_AGAIN.
- Checked on a fresh disposable database. Typecheck and lint are clean. The request and reopen suites pass 31 of 31
  twice in a row, with no request left claimed and unemailed. The 17 other suites the change touches pass 268 of
  268. `floor-long-names-postgres` pins the house cluster; CI runs it.
