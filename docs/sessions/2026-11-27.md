## A pass that fails on one request costs no other its escalation or its announcement (T-768)

- The fault, measured on master's passes against a disposable PostgreSQL. Two due requests were set up, and the
  database refused the second one's inbox copy (a test trigger, as a constraint or an outage would refuse it).
  The pass threw. Afterwards the database held:
  - the first request claimed, emailed and with its inbox copy, but never announced: the pass threw before it
    returned its list, so no push and no live frame went;
  - the second claimed, never emailed, and with no inbox copy. Its claim had landed before the insert failed, so
    no later pass would take it, and nobody was told.
  - And the email pass ran after the escalation pass with no guard between them, so an email-pass failure dropped
    the escalation pass's good list too.
- The cure:
  - `runRequestEscalationPass` returns `{ escalated, failed }` instead of a list. Each request is on its own: its
    claim, its reads and its inbox copy commit together, in one transaction. A request that fails before its inbox
    copy is written stays unclaimed, so the next pass escalates it (with T-766's wait).
  - Its emails go after the commit. An email step that throws leaves `escalation_emailed_at` unset, so the email
    pass sends it later; the escalation still stands and is announced.
  - `runEscalationEmailPass` tries every row, and throws once at the end if any failed.
  - `runSweepPasses` (it was `runBothPasses`) runs the email pass whatever the escalation pass did, and gives the
    scheduler both the list and the failures.
  - The scheduler announces what escalated, and counts the wake as failed when anything failed, so the wait and the
    hourly report apply.
  - The escalation-pass endpoint announces what escalated, then answers with an error if any request failed, so a
    cron and Sentry hear of it.
- The review's "its email can be lost" is refuted: the email pass picks up any escalated request whose email has
  not gone, and the new case shows it sends it. The other two losses are as the review described, and fixed.
- Tests, each watched failing on master's passes:
  - in `requests-postgres.test.ts`, the refused inbox copy (the request left unclaimed, the other escalated and
    announced, the failure reported, the next pass taking it) and the email step that throws (announced, reported,
    sent later by the email pass);
  - in `escalation-scheduler.test.ts`, an email pass that throws loses nothing the escalation pass escalated, and a
    partly failed wake announces, reports and waits.
- Every earlier caller of the pass in the Postgres suites now asserts that the pass failed on nothing.
- From a fresh disposable database: the scheduler, request and Sentry suites pass 140 of 140, and no request is
  left claimed and unemailed afterwards. Six more suites that import the changed modules pass, 49 of 49. The
  seventh, `floor-long-names-postgres`, pins the house test cluster, which this lane did not use; it never calls
  the changed endpoint, and CI runs it.

## T-768 after its review: a request that keeps failing is held on its own floor

- The release lead's verdict at `395acdd8` confirmed the cure, and the three answers measured for it (two
  replicas, the scrubbing, the email's idempotency), but asked for one push. The review's 24-hour model of a
  request whose claim always fails: under T-766's whole-wake back-off it is tried every minute for ever (1,444
  passes a day instead of 207), every other escalation waits up to a minute against a two-minute window, the
  database never gets its five idle minutes, and the hourly alarm names no request (LN1). This PR made that loop,
  since master spent one failed pass on such a request.
- LN1. A request the pass failed on now costs the rest of the sweep nothing:
  - A failed claim holds that request back on a floor of its own: 5 s after its first failure, doubling, to an
    hour. Until then it is left out of the pass's candidates (`skip`) and out of the plan, which counts it at its
    deadline instead; then it is tried again, so nothing is dropped.
  - The wake's own back-off (T-766) stays for a pass that fails as a whole, its read of what is due.
  - Each request's alarm names it (`escalation.request`, a UUID) when its streak starts and at most hourly after.
  - The candidates are taken longest-waiting first.
  - The state is per process. A request quiet for an hour past its last chance is forgotten, and one that
    escalates, or whose email goes, is cleared.
  - An email the escalation pass failed on that the same wake's email pass then sent is no failure (LN5).
- The simulation that pins it: request A's claim always fails and request B falls due 30 s later.
  - B escalates exactly on time, and A's tries widen 5, 10, 20 … seconds to the hour.
  - The plan sleeps past five minutes; every alarm names A, at most hourly.
  - A failed candidate select still takes the wake's back-off.
- LN2, measured: two titles overflowed on a long room's name, not one. The notification title, `varchar(180)`,
  refused the escalation of a room over about 160 characters. The thread title, `varchar(160)`, which the review
  did not name, refused the raise itself for a room over about 140, its own 160-character example included. Both
  are now clipped at their one insert each, by code point as `clipName` does. A PG case with rooms of 160 and 190
  characters raises and escalates both, with the thread's title at 160 and the escalation's at 180.
- LN3: a route case (the pass stubbed: one request escalated and published and pushed, then the generic 500 naming
  nothing) and a PG email-pass case (one row's send throws: the other is sent and marked, and the pass rejects
  with an `AggregateError` of one, naming that row).
- The nits went too:
  - LN5: the failure says "claim" or "email", and so does the endpoint's message.
  - LN6: T-768's row is in progress.
  - LD1: each request's alarm is its own event, carrying only its own message.
- Checked on a fresh disposable database. Typecheck and lint are clean. The request and reopen suites pass 31 of 31
  twice in a row, with no request left claimed and unemailed. The 17 other suites the change touches pass 259 of
  259. `floor-long-names-postgres` still pins the house cluster; CI runs it.
