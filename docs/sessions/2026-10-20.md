## 01:30 — The front door's fonts and chunk waterfall, and an honest build stamp

Branch `perf/front-door-fonts-chunks`, from `origin/master` 7304f055. Run-first item 10 and the build-stamp
part of item 6 of the 16 October report "Deeper upgrades across the Venviewer stack". The first worktree
had started on the stale local master (1,420 commits behind); the branch was cut from `origin/master`.

**What the bundle visualiser showed** (rollup-plugin-visualizer on a production build of `origin/master`,
plus the Vite manifest):

- **Clerk did not have to load on `/`.** `router.tsx` imports `ProtectedRoute`, which imported
  `useClerk` from `@clerk/react` for one button on its access-denied screen, so the whole `clerk` chunk
  (84 KB raw, 35 KB Brotli) sat in the entry's static graph for every visitor.
- **`runtime-package-resolution` was 40.6 KB Brotli, 36.9 KB of it
  `data/generated/trades-hall-splat-bundles.ts`**, the 215 KB tile-digest manifest the walk and planner
  need. The home cards read four numbers and a name per room from it. It also dragged in the 14 KB
  `asset-version` chunk (`TRADES_HALL_RUNTIME_ROOMS` only supplied room names).
- **The 432,136 font bytes are exactly four files**: Fraunces roman 121,016 + Fraunces italic 149,720 +
  Newsreader roman 132,000 + Geist 29,400.

**What changed**

1. `ClerkExitButton.tsx` split out of `ProtectedRoute.tsx`, lazy-loaded behind a Suspense fallback and an
   error boundary (a failed chunk load leaves a plain link to `/login`, not an error page).
2. `data/room-card-facts.ts` (generated by `scripts/derive-room-card-facts.mjs`, 2 KB) replaces the
   manifest on the home page; `front-door-weight.test.ts` fails if it drifts from the manifest.
3. Fonts: `files/` and `site*.css` stay Google's bytes. `scripts/slim-site-fonts.py` derives `slim/*-core.woff2`
   and `site-slim*.css`, splitting each Latin face into `latin-core` (ASCII plus the site's copy
   characters) and `latin` (Google's original file, remaining range). Only glyphs are dropped.
   `scripts/verify-slim-fonts.py` shapes 847,524 character pairs at 12 sampled axis settings per face and
   compares advances, offsets, clusters, the outline of every glyph the shaper chose, every core
   character's outline at all 108 (Fraunces) / 15 / 5 / 3 settings, and every hinting program: no
   difference at all (worst outline deviation 0.000 units). Home font bytes 432,136 → 327,336.
4. `home-route-preload-plugin.ts`: reads the final chunk graph and writes the home route's chunks as
   path-gated `modulepreload` links (with a `preload as=script` fallback where the browser lacks
   modulepreload) at a marker in `index.html`, before the stylesheet links. It also fails the build if the
   entry or home route reaches Clerk or the splat manifest. No font preloads (24 September).
5. `/health/version` `builtAt`: the Dockerfile writes the build time into `.build-timestamp` inside the
   image (`ARG RAILWAY_GIT_COMMIT_SHA`/`RAILWAY_DEPLOYMENT_ID` named in the command so a new deployment
   misses the layer cache); `lib/build-stamp.ts` reads it once and prefers it to the hand-set
   `BUILD_TIMESTAMP` variable, which sat at 24 September.

**Not done, and why**

- **Lighthouse CI:** the keyless PageSpeed API still answers 429 and no key is in the environment.
- **Axis trimming:** limiting `wght` to the `@font-face` descriptors would save about 19 KB more on `/`,
  but re-basing a variable font's default moved points by a unit or two and advances by up to 2 units in
  2,000 (0.1% of an em). Left for Blake. `SOFT`/`WONK` are animated by the hero and `opsz` is automatic,
  so they stay.
- **The biggest remaining cost on `/` is image and font contention, not scripts:** 893 KB of images (the
  hero and seven room cards) and 327 KB of fonts share 200 KB/s with everything else, and LCP is the hero
  image finishing. The card images load at about 2.8 s; deferring them below the first screen is the next
  lever (a design call about the rail).
- Accented characters outside the site's copy (a typed "Siobhán") still render from Google's original file,
  so kerning does not cross the core/remainder boundary for those words. The copy itself is all in the core.

## 01:30 — E8: production blends splats in a different colour space from training (T-639)

measured gap with an unmeasured answer"). It was research only and production is unchanged. The GPU went through the

`notes/PREREG.md`, written before any render.

- **Answer: yes.** Production converts splat colour to linear light and blends it in the half-float pass. gsplat and
  our trainer composite the stored, display-encoded values. On the production Grand Hall tiles at 20 held-out E57 poses,
  using the product's intrinsics, today's draw renders soft pixels about 6 code values brighter than gsplat. gsplat's
  own renderer, switched to a linear blend, shifts them by +7.9.
- **Rule passed for both steps.** B, the encoded blend, beats today by +1.03 dB on soft pixels and +7.17 dB elsewhere.
  C, which also drops the per-splat clamp at 1 (gsplat clamps at 0 only), adds +4.13 dB on the chandelier and window
  crops. Overall C reaches 36.29 dB against today's 32.10, and comes closer on 20 of 20 views. The instrument floor is
  49 dB. [Results note](../reports/e8-splat-blend-2026-10-20.md).
- **Also found.** At DPR 2 the antialias compensation production turned on on 17 October (T-658) moves the image a
  further 12.5 dB from the trained look (C → C0). It belongs to T-658 and T-639, not E8, and needs a per-DPR decision.
- **For Blake.** Today's blend against C at the product's arrival, chandelier, gilding and plan poses, with 1:1 crops:
  [comparison page](https://claude.ai/artifact/B4oo64bjznCxPTdt6royfa). Today's blend puts a halo on every chandelier
  bulb and slightly washes the gilt lettering. Adopting C is a separate PR under T-639 (I1b), after his look decision.
- **Scope.** The production tiles come from XGRIDS's closed trainer, so gsplat stands in for a standard 3DGS trainer.

  to it directly.
