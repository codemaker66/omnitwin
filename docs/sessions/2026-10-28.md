nearly blocks 3/4 doors." The door rule (branch `claude/door-clear-spaces`, now lane E's) keeps 1.5 m of floor in
front of each Grand Hall door, its width plus 0.3 m past each side, built from hall-spec's openings. Measured with
that rule's own code (`doorsBlockedBy`), the Assembly's dinner of 18 rounds of 10 stood five rounds in the spaces
of all four doors.

Blake chose on 28 Oct, shown both plans: "we can sit 12 a table, but 10 is the average. yeah keep firedoors clean.
let people place tables within the red zone but have a warning on it (an aesthetic warning of some kind, not
something annoying or makes it look bad)".

- The dinner (`floor/assembly/fixture.ts`) is now 15 rounds of 12: 180, the venue's published Grand Hall dinner
  capacity (`TRADES_HALL_ROOM_CAPACITIES`). Ten a round stays the planner's default; twelve is the 6 ft round's
  catalogue maximum. Six rounds across the middle (Table 1, the top round, before the fireplace), five along the
  windows with the fire exit's corner left open, four on the door side between the doors. Checked with the door
  rule's code: every door space is empty, the nearest piece 0.29 m from one. Chair backs stay at least 0.21 m
  apart and 0.35 m from the long walls, and the 0.91 m aisle from the main door to the centre window remains.
- The device-day layout (a public draft on production) was re-saved with Blake's OK: revision 2 to 3, 15 rounds
  and 180 Chiavari (195 pieces), read back and checked against the door rule with no door blocked. The live
  planner shows 15 tables and 180 seats.
- The rest of his answer revises the door rule itself, and went to lane E with the branch: a hand-placed piece may
  stand in a door's space and gets a quiet, good-looking warning instead of the slide-out; automatic layouts still
  never use the spaces. The warning's look goes to Blake on real frames.
- The test that the live dinner keeps every door clear lands with whichever of this change and the door rule's PR
  merges second; the door rule's notice test moves to a frozen copy of the old positions.
