**Offline requests (#296).**
- A keyed `PATCH /requests/:id` runs through the conversation-command ledger.
- A kept step carries the state it was pressed against, and is refused once the request moved on.
- A request step's ledger read, transition and record run in one locked transaction.
- A key is bound to its person and command.
- On the web, a press that cannot reach the house waits, keyed, with its author, said beside the card.
- The queue replays in order. A refusal is said on the slot, and the person's later steps on that
  request are not sent.

A max-effort adversarial review found two blocking defects before merge, both confirmed and fixed with
tests that fail on the old code:
1. A kept Done behind a refused accept closed a colleague's work.
2. One key sent twice at once could hand a request over twice (14–20 of 40–60 races).

A second max-effort pass over those fixes confirmed the second (0 of 60 doubled; no deadlock under
mixed load), and found the first only half fixed:
- The refusal was remembered for one replay only.
- The precondition named a state, and Graham's "accepted" reads like Elaine's.

So a kept step now also names the owner it was pressed against, and the server refuses it once the
request has changed hands. The queue also keeps the order pressed when the clock stands still or
steps back.

Evidence: offline-replay Postgres suite 14/14 (including 20 + 20 concurrent rounds), API full suite
4,349 passed and 0 failed, web affected suites 286/286, the new route-mocked e2e 2/2 in Chromium.
