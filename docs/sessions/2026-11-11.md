## A correction before building

The option offered to Blake said a reissued sheet would show what changed since the version the
hallkeeper last saw. The code says a layout is never reissued: `approved` leads only to `archived`
(`VALID_CONFIGURATION_REVIEW_TRANSITIONS`, packages/types/src/configuration-review.ts), and only a
venue or platform admin can edit an approved layout, which never reaches the frozen sheet. A first
version wired a "Setup sheet changed" record into approval; it could never fire, so it was taken out
before any commit.

What does change after approval, and what the floor now reads:

- **The event:** guests, hours and details, through `PATCH /events/:id`. These were already recorded
  in the event's change feed, needing a hallkeeper's acknowledgement, but only the ops event page
  showed them.
- **Its phases:** recorded in the same feed already.
- **Its bookings, moved in the Diary:** hours or room. These were not recorded anywhere; a
  hallkeeper who read the day at seven had no way to see at ten that the dinner now starts later.

Tables added or moved belong to a different layout, which is the second build ("what moves").

## What was built (branch `claude/practical-faraday-5t2em4-what-changed`)

- **One read for the board.** `GET /events/awaiting-acknowledgement?eventIds=` returns, for each event
  the caller's venue holds, the changes needing acknowledgement that nobody has acknowledged, newest
  first, and who last acknowledged anything. Other venues' events are left out, not refused. The floor
  acknowledges as one, as the event-day board does.
- **A Diary move is told to the floor** (`services/booking-move-feed.ts`, from `updateBookingLocked`):
  - the title is "Times moved", "Room changed", or both;
  - the summary says how far, in the venue's clock ("Grand Hall: 30 min later", "starts 1 h
    earlier");
  - before and after carry the exact hours ("Grand Hall, Sat 14 Nov, 18:00 – 23:00");
  - it is recorded in the move's transaction;
  - a booking already over by its new end tells no one.
- **Event edits say what changed** (`describeEventEdit`): the guests, the new hours in the venue's
  clock, and which details changed (name, occasion, client, notes), never their words. A guests-only
  edit keeps its old words, "150 guests" to "180 guests".
- **On the Day Board:**
  - Each slot with a change to read carries a copper-ruled "Changed" beside its title. The word
    stays ink, and its words are in the slot's label.
  - A change sits on the slots it concerns: a moved booking on its own slot, a phase on the slot that
    holds it, an edit to the event on every slot of the event.
  - The day's sentence names the rooms: "Grand Hall has a change to read."
  - The slot's panel shows What changed: each change's title and time, its Was and Now, and its
    sentence when Was and Now cannot say it all.
  - One "Got it" acknowledges what that slot shows. While a change waits, it is the panel's one cream
    button.
  - Afterwards the panel says who has it: "Changes seen by Jamie at the door, 09:12."
  - A manager reads the changes but has no button, because the API takes acknowledgements from the
    hallkeeper, staff and admin roles only. The wall shows the mark, and a short wall spares it rather
    than clip a room.
  - The board reads again every minute while in view, when shown again and when back online. A
    failed read keeps what it last knew.

## Independent review, and what changed after it

An adversarial review (the deep-reviewer agent) of the first commit found no blocker. It
reproduced its findings on the disposable database, in Chromium and in tsx. All nine are fixed,
each with a test that fails on the old code where a test can hold it:

- **A drag and its Undo left two changes** on a slot whose hours never moved, and fiddly drags
  piled up. A move of a booking whose last move nobody has acknowledged is now folded in:
  - the earlier moves stay in the feed as history the floor need not acknowledge, and their bells
    go;
  - one change carries the place the floor last knew (its Was) to where the booking stands now;
  - a move straight back to that place tells the floor nothing.
- **A failed read said nothing.** The board now says "Couldn’t read what changed since 14:05",
  with Try again, and the panel says the same.
- **"Got it" disabled itself while sending**, which drops the keyboard's place to the page in
  Chromium. It now stays enabled (`aria-disabled`), so a failed send leaves focus on it.
- **Shift words counted absolute time.** A move to the same wall time a day later across 25
  October read "1 day 1 h later"; the shift is now counted on the venue's clock.
- **The panel hid an event edit's sentence** when its Was and Now held only the guests, as older
  rows do, so a change of hours could vanish. Only a Diary move, whose Was and Now carry
  everything, now says itself once.
- **A proposed window that moves a hold** (`proposeWindowCore`) now tells the floor, as a Diary
  move does. It was the one other path that moves a booking.
- **A function moved off the day shown left without a word.** The board remembers the events it
  has shown for a day, and a moved-off function's change stays on that day until the floor has
  it.
- **Reads were thrown away after an acknowledgement**, and a move's mark waited up to a minute.
  A read now follows each "Got it" and each calendar refresh.
- **An old "Couldn’t send that" came back** under a change nobody had tried to send. A failure now
  clears once the waiting changes differ.
- **Also:** the read names who acknowledged by id, so your own acknowledgement reads "seen by
  you" after the server answers.

## Limits and follow-ups

- **Not recorded yet:**
  - a booking released or cancelled, which takes the slot off the board;
  - a booking added for today.
- **Notifications.** A move's notification opens the ops event page, as the feed's other entries do.
- **Clock.** The board reads changes every minute and with each calendar refresh; no live frame
  announces a change on its own yet.
- **Moved off the day.** A function moved off a day is told there only on a board that showed it
  before the move; a board opened afterwards has the change on the function's new day and in the
  bell.
