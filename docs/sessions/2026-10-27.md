**Part 2, codemaker66/omnitwin#286: guardrails and metering.**
- Drafts never state a price, availability or capacity: the rule is sent to the model, and every sentence is checked
  once, where a draft is made. A claim is taken out and the draft says so; one with fewer than 12 words left is refused.
- Four reviews changed the check. The first found the first version unsafe (partial lines, 41 of 41 claims missed,
  33 of 39 clean sentences taken out). The second and third found that every exemption let the draft's own claims
  through as a model writes them when it answers: a clause holding "you asked" or "budget" hid 210 of 248 known claims
  ("as you asked, room hire is £4,500"); a question excused its answer in the next sentence ("you asked whether the
  17th is free. It is."). Now nothing excuses a claim's shape, and the rule sent to the model says the same. The fourth
  found a bare answer left once its claim was out ("It can, comfortably."), carve-outs for times and opening hours
  hiding claims ("seats 180 to 220", "available from the 14th"), and topic words taken out as claims ("asks about a
  discount"); all fixed. The final verification found the narrowing had lost everyday forms the version before found
  ("We'd happily waive corkage", "It's included in the price"); found again, with nothing the version before found
  missed on the reviewer's probes, and the review loop stopped there.
- Measured on sentence sets written by agents that never saw the patterns, each run once: the first design read 104 of
  160 claims on bare sentences (it hid most framed answers); the current design, on a set that asked for claims in
  answers, questions, quotations and two sentences, took out 1 of 60 clean sentences and found 39 of 60 claims (19 of
  20 prices, 9 of 20 dates and rooms, 11 of 20 sizes). The rule and the person reading every draft are the other
  guards. All sets are regression tests.
- `withheldCommitments` is sent only when something was taken out, so an older web build still reads every other
