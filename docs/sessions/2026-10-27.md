## T-677: product writes share the foundry's global lock (migration 0105)

Blake chose to take the shared lock T-676 measured on a branch of its own
(`claude/t-677-foundry-shared-product-lock`), to be reviewed and released apart from the sweep.

**Why.** Migration 0058 gave 57 tables a statement-level trigger, and the 18 product tables among them a row-level
guard as well, that take one advisory lock (`hashtextextended('foundry-kill:0:global', 0)`) exclusively and hold it to
commit. Every insert or update on `configurations`, `spaces`, `venues`, `files` and the rest therefore waited for every
other open one. A transaction that locked a layout's row before its first such write (the review submit, approve and
claim do) deadlocked with a save that had taken the lock first and then waited for that row. T-676 measured both
through the real routes on a disposable database ([its 26 October log on #260](https://github.com/codemaker66/omnitwin/blob/claude/sleepy-ramanujan-noyxli/docs/sessions/2026-10-26.md)):
a planner's save during their own submit was the deadlock's victim 3 of 3, and a layout save waited 1,006-1,012 ms
behind another layout's write held for 1 s. With the product tables taking the lock shared, the same saves took
9-12 ms and nothing deadlocked.

**Change.** 0104 changes two functions and touches no trigger or table, so applying it takes no table lock (6 ms with
a product write open, in review). It adds `fdv1_lock_root_shared()`, which takes `pg_advisory_xact_lock_shared` on the
same key, and replaces:
- `fdv1_root_trigger`, which now takes the lock shared on the 18 product tables, by name, and exclusively on the
  foundry's 39, as before. One foundry statement takes it shared too: the security event `fdv1_public_guard` records,
  from inside a product write, when a product row names a quarantined object (`pg_trigger_depth() > 1`). The foundry's
  own recorders (the storage-profile pre-scan and the custody guard) fire inside foundry writes, which hold the lock
  exclusively already.
- `fdv1_public_guard`, which takes the lock shared and is otherwise 0058's, character for character.

The foundry's own functions still take the lock exclusively: their writers use it as the head of their lock order.

**What it keeps.** Shared, the lock still makes a product write wait for an open kill switch or foundry write, and the
kill switch wait for every open product write; the guard's check still sees a quarantine registered by a transaction it
waited for. What it gives up is product writes waiting for one another. The review traced every writer in 0053-0102 and
the API: nothing relies on that. The one place that would have needed care, the guard's security event inside a product
write, is the exception above.

**Review.** A maximum-effort review found that exception missing from the first version. The guard's event insert
upgraded the shared lock to an exclusive one. Two detections at once, or a detection and a save waiting on its row,
deadlocked; the rollback could take the critical event with it, or fail an ordinary save with a 500. While the
exclusive request queued, every new product write stalled. It is latent today, since no quarantine profile or custody
row exists. With the exception, the reviewer's three scenarios complete and record every event (3 of 3). The review
also found this log describing an earlier draft, which re-pointed triggers; corrected.

**Tests.** `foundry-product-lock-postgres.test.ts` joins the platform gate, so CI runs it. Its nine cases:
- 0058's 57 root and 18 guard triggers unchanged, with the function's shared list exactly the product tables;
- the lock mode each product table's statement actually takes (`ShareLock`, read from `pg_locks`);
- the guard's body against 0058's;
- a product write proceeding while another is open;
- the submit's lock order against a save's;
- the kill switch, and the foundry's own trigger, excluding product writes both ways;
- two quarantined references named at once, and a detection a save waits on: both rows refused and every event
  recorded, against a quarantine profile the suite registers and removes.

Every wait is proved with `lock_timeout`, not a clock. On a database migrated only to 0102, seven fail and the two
kill-switch cases pass. With the migration's first version, exactly the two detection cases fail (55P03). With the
migration all nine pass, 3 of 3, and the platform gate applied it over a 0102 database and passed 431 of 431.

**Merge after T-676** ([#260](https://github.com/codemaker66/omnitwin/pull/260)). Without T-676's write-time review
check, a save that deadlocked with a submit would instead wait for it and land behind the submitted snapshot. T-676
makes that save answer 409 `CONFIG_LOCKED`; with both, T-676's scratch harness saw the save refused and no deadlock.
