## The quiz learns to deliberate, to remember, to hand you your year, and to count

Blake's brief, verbatim: *"go for it if you are confident you are making a
genuinely impressive, intelligent, fun for both people who have never heard
of trade's house and people who have been coming to craft dinners for
decades, all in a voice that is identical to ursula le guins."* The format
research (`docs/trades-house/quiz-authoring/FORMAT-RESEARCH-2026-08-16.md`)
had recommended keeping the answering format and adding four things. All four
shipped in this session; the spec's new section records the architecture.

### What was built

- **The Deliberation** — when the twelve leave the top two within 0.15, one
  more scene written for the pair (twelve authored, else composed from
  fourteen world lines), weighted to decide. He announces it himself as the
  aside on the twelfth reply; the button stays "Go on".
- **The town remembers** — twenty-five asides keyed on an earlier answer,
  spoken later, never grading the answer about to be given.
- **Your Year** — a ledger line per answer; a plate beside the reveal on wide
  screens, a sheet with a scrim on phones; share where there is a share sheet,
  copy where there is not.
- **The record** — `POST /public/quiz-runs`, no PII by construction, DNT/GPC
  honoured, text/plain keepalive send; migration 0062. **Not yet deployed:**
  Blake runs `pnpm db:migrate` then `railway up`.

### The review, and what it caught

An eight-agent adversarial workflow (voice editor, lore, TypeScript, security,
UX, three blind legibility readers). Every blocker and every "should" was
applied — 45 voice findings, 7 security, 9 UX, 8 TypeScript, 4 lore.

Three lessons worth more than the fixes:

1. **The prose gates check legibility, length and no-names — not taste.** The
   editor found four moralising epigrams and a dozen tautology/pun/Latinate
   tics in copy that had passed every automated gate. Her rules are now in the
   spec.
2. **Verify, don't assume, at the network edge.** The `trustProxy: true`
   forgery claim was tested from outside: `x-ratelimit-remaining` counted
   99→94 straight through forged `X-Forwarded-For` values — Railway's edge
   overwrites the header. Recorded at the config comment so it is not
   re-raised.
3. **A double cast is a smell with a design answer.** The route test needed
   `as unknown as Database["insert"]`; the honest fix was a one-method
   `QuizRunStore` port with a Drizzle implementation, and the test needs no
   cast at all.

### The bug the screenshots found

Headless Playwright screenshots (still the only way to *see* a render here)
showed the bubble retyping the scene from the first word the moment an
answer was given. Cause: the voice player owns one audio element; an aside
started while the scene was still being spoken reset the clock the scene's
typewriter was reading. `speakAside` now settles the scene first. That in
turn made the e2e measure the fully-spoken bubble at every reply — which it
had never done, because Playwright answers mid-typewriter — and exposed a
real 25px overflow on a 375×667 phone at scene nine. On short phones the
replied bubble is now capped at four lines under a fade and scrolls.

## Nothing scrolls, on any screen, at any height

Blake, with a screenshot of the threshold at 100% zoom: *"at 100% zoom on
desktop you need to scroll to see everything: we must have no need to scroll
on any page on desktop or mobile, fix this"*.

He was right, and the guard that should have caught it had a hole in exactly
the shape of the bug: the no-scroll sweep ran at four iPhone sizes and nowhere
else. The threshold sized his portrait by WIDTH — `clamp(360px, 36vw, 560px)`
— and a width cannot know the viewport is 720px tall. Measured before the fix:
104px over at 1512x945, 195px at 1366x768, 198px at 1280x720.

### Method

A measurement harness (`packages/web/tmp/fit.mjs`, git-ignored) walks every
state — intro, threshold, twelve scenes with every option expanded, every
reply, the deliberation, the weighing, the reveal, the ledger open — at twenty
viewports, and reports the overflow per state. Three traps it taught:

1. **Measure what the test measures.** The harness first used
   `documentElement.scrollHeight` vs `innerHeight`; the e2e uses
   `max(documentElement, body)` vs `clientHeight`. The harness said clean while
   the suite went red.
2. **Measure the tallest state.** The threshold GROWS as his lines accumulate,
   and the bubble grows as it types. Sampling on arrival proves nothing.
3. **Measure the worst content.** The result stack varies by ~37px depending
   on which Craft it names. The Barbers and the Hammermen are the wordiest;
   a walk that happens to land on the Masons proves nothing.

### Found

- Both hero-painting screens sized a tall painting by width. They now cap it
  by the height available, with the gilt frame hugging the scaled image.
- The weighing screen hid `.convener-speech` — the SVG skin's bubble — but
  never `.convener-painting-speech`, the skin that actually ships. An empty
  bubble was holding 88px under a thinking face, which is both the whole of
  that screen's overflow and the exact thing the comment above the rule says
  must not happen. A rule written for one skin and never updated for the other.
- The result screen had a continuous height-compression system starting at
  1040px that stopped short of its two prose blocks and its tail of links, and
  no rule at all for 431-979px wide — so an iPad rendered at full tablet scale
  and stood 32px over. A wide-and-short screen wraps less prose than a
  narrow-and-short one, so the crest now keeps its size at ≥1200px rather than
  paying for someone else's wrapping.
- The smallest phones had 19px of headroom on the wordiest Craft — one wrapped
  line from scrolling, which is why that guard flickered instead of failing.

### Verified

Twenty viewports × every state, locally and then against production: no
scrolling anywhere. Headroom on the wordiest Craft is 18-42px where it was
8-38px or negative. The e2e sweep now runs at ten viewports rather than four,
the threshold has its own test (it costs 13s of beat timers, which is why it
cannot live inside a twelve-scene walk), and the assertion names the element
that overflowed. 26/26 e2e on three consecutive runs, 4593 web unit tests,
typecheck 0, lint clean, build ok.
