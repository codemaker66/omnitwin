## Construction and material standard

Build a coherent architectural model from the evidence. Scans and splats can guide shape and appearance, but blurry splat surfaces or camera-facing photographs do not replace finished game geometry. Preserve genuine historical irregularity while removing capture tears, double surfaces, phantom geometry and registration artifacts.

Use reusable architectural components where appropriate, consistent real-world scale and pivots, sound topology, deliberate normals, finished joins and sensible bevels. Exterior enclosing solids must close without accidental holes; intentional openings and thin cloth/glass are explicitly modeled and classified. Test both render surfaces and collision geometry.

Author engine-appropriate material layers: base colour separated from capture illumination where feasible, normal/detail normals, roughness, metalness and required engine masks, with physical glass and cloth response. Use geometry for silhouette and depth that must survive parallax. Eliminate baked chandelier reflections and duplicated cloth folds as substitutes for the actual objects. Preserve photographic artwork and inscriptions with enough detail for the declared viewing distance. Give brass, oak, stone/plaster, gilding, textiles and glass distinct responses under neutral, grazing and dramatic lighting.

Pin texel-density and close-view targets, material packing, mesh/detail budgets and export conventions after a representative engine import experiment. Those targets are production contracts, not measurements already achieved. Use BG3 environment references to assess material richness, form, lighting and scene coherence while retaining this building's identity; do not introduce unrelated fantasy architecture or decorate over defects.
