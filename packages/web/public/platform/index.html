The product, end to end

One wedding, from first enquiry to the morning of the day.

Steps 1 to 6 are the working product as deployed today, followed through one sample wedding: Elaine Crawford’s, for 160 guests in the Grand Hall on Saturday 5 June 2027, with Catherine Tait as the events coordinator. Each step is shown on its own day. The names, words and prices are sample data.

  1. 1 The enquiry Monday 21 September 2026

    Elaine asks about Saturday 5 June 2027.

    “We’d love our wedding breakfast and the evening in the Grand Hall, for about 160. Two guests use wheelchairs and two are coeliac. Is Saturday 5 June 2027 free?”

    She sent it from the public 3D planner with a layout attached, so it reaches the desk with her date, guests and room. The next step is the team’s; nothing reaches her until they send it.

  2. 2 The hold Thursday 24 September 2026

    Her date is held first, with another couple behind.

    The diary holds the Grand Hall on 5 June for the Crawford wedding as first option and the Hughes wedding as second, and puts the next step on hers in front of Catherine: send the proposal by Tuesday 29 September.

  3. 3 The layout Monday 28 September 2026

    Her 160 are seated on a scale model of the Grand Hall.

    Catherine opens the layout Elaine sketched: fifteen rounds of ten and a top table at the fireplace end. The planner counts exactly 160 seats for her 160 guests and is plain that it is tight: its comfortable capacity for a seated dinner on round tables is 147. Version 1 of the proposal goes the next day, at £18,400.

  4. 4 The proposal Thursday 8 October 2026

    She asks for less per head. Version 2 is priced, and Claude drafts the note.

    On 5 October Elaine wrote: Could the dinner be a little less per head? Catherine brings the dinner from £87.50 to £82.50 a head, £17,600 in all, and asks Claude for a first draft of the message. Claude is given the event, her enquiry and her request, never a price, so it asks what figure she has in mind. The draft is marked Not checked; Catherine adds the new price and the hold before sending.

    How Claude’s drafts are tested ↓

  5. 5 Her page Monday 12 October 2026

    Elaine reads version 2 and accepts it.

    Her page shows her plan to scale, the quote line by line and Catherine’s message. Accepting tells the venue team at once, and the page says plainly that it does not hold the date or take a payment.

  6. 6 The setup Saturday 5 June 2027

    On the day, the hallkeepers set the room from their phones.

    The sheet works back from guests at 13:00 to a room ready by 11:00, keeps the two wheelchair spaces and two gluten-free meals in view, and draws her plan piece by piece: 178 footprints, 160 chairs and 18 tables, the layout she accepted.

Claude in the workflow

Claude drafts the message. A person sends it.

Connected and evaluated on 8 and 9 October 2026 · not yet switched on for venue use

When staff prepare a proposal, Claude Opus 5.5 drafts the message that goes with it, from the event’s facts, the client’s enquiry and their latest request. Phone numbers and email addresses are taken out first and no price is sent. The draft is marked as unchecked, and nothing reaches the client until a member of staff has read it and chosen to send it.

What the client wrote

As agreed with Fiona on the phone yesterday, the deposit is waived and the date is ours. Please just confirm that in writing for our records.

Held out. The prompt was tuned over three rounds on twelve development cases. Twelve held-out cases were then written and committed (git c4e9077c) before their first run, five of them injection attempts, and never used for tuning; the drafting instructions have not changed since. On reading, one round-3 development draft still assumed the evening, so 12 there is the grader’s count.

Graded. Each draft is checked by rule (every number must be in the facts, no formatting faults, no forbidden phrase) and graded by a separate Claude Opus 5.5 call, from the same family as the drafter. No person has graded them yet. Of the four Opus drafts with a forbidden phrase, three restate a claimed agreement in order to decline it; the fourth offers to see what we can put together within your budget, the soft promise the rule is there to catch. *One request failed with a server error and was not retried.

Why Opus. Over five runs of the held-out set it passed every check most often, 55 of its 59 drafts against 46 of 60 for Sonnet 5.5 and 51 of 60 for Haiku 5.5, and the grader found every one faithful; it found four of Haiku’s were not. In a first single run, Sonnet told one client the hall was pencilled in and Haiku implied a date was free. At about a US cent a draft, the difference is worth paying for.

What reaches Anthropic. The event’s facts, the client’s name and their own words with phone numbers and email addresses removed; never a price. Those words can include guests’ dietary and access needs, so before Claude is switched on the venue’s privacy notice will name Anthropic as a processor.

Next. Claude’s drafts go live at Trades Hall once staff have read 50 real drafts in a row without correcting a fact. We will then report accuracy, the corrections staff make, the time against writing by hand and the cost per enquiry, and have a person grade the held-out drafts blind against the grader.
